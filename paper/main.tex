\begin{abstract}
Circuit discovery typically explains an output: a logit, a probability, a behavioural metric.
We ask instead what makes an internal latent fire.
For a target sparse autoencoder (SAE) latent, \emph{weighted circuit masking} (WCM) learns a circuit of upstream latents with a scaling coefficient for each, trained so that the circuit reproduces the target when every other latent is ablated (to zero, to one of two sparsity-preserving means, or to its value on a real context where the target is silent), with the SAE reconstruction errors kept live.
% 2026-11-08 (numbers pass): experiments/109-pod-run-analysis/out run2026_headline.csv, run2026_alpha1.csv,
% run2026_nulls.csv, run2026_tl_matched.csv, run2026_tl_sweep.csv.
% 2026-11-14 (compression A2/A3): the plateau sentence was cut (it implied production sits on the plateau; it sits
% just past its edge, sec:lambda-transfer); the per-model exclusion triplet 82/81/82 stays in sec:main-result.
We fit WCM at one sparsity penalty to $8{,}000$ sampled latents of TuringLLM, a 12-layer model trained on a documented synthetic corpus, and to $512$ each of Pythia-70m and GPT-2 small.
With circuits of a median $609$, $294$, and $371$ upstream latents, $88\%$, $82\%$, and $82\%$ of the scored targets ($81$--$82\%$ counting exclusions as fails) get a circuit that, on held-out text, reproduces the target under zero and both mean ablations and is necessary for it; size-matched random circuits given the same coefficient fitting pass for none of $366$ targets tested.
A size-only rule, fixed before a pilot, kept the same penalty on both public models.
The coefficients keep these circuits small: without them the same nodes pass for $2$--$14\%$ of targets, and in TuringLLM's last four layers the smallest passing unweighted circuit is a median $13.8\times$ larger ($6{,}124$ against $436$ latents).
The procedure needs no behavioural task and applies to any latent in an SAE bank.
\end{abstract}

Consider a latent in layer 10 of a 12-layer language model that fires on the possessive in ``Ohm's'', ``Snell's'', or ``Kirchhoff's'', before the word ``law'' has appeared, but stays silent on ``Darwin's'', ``Rutherford's'', and ``Picasso's''.
Given an invented name, it fires in a passage on electromagnetism and not in one on the history of painting.
Something upstream supplies which names carry laws and what the passage is about.
What makes this latent fire?

Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) decompose a model's activations into latents like this one, which often track coherent concepts~\citep{cunningham2024sparse, bricken2023monosemanticity, gao2024scaling, templeton2024scaling}, a working answer to superposition~\citep{elhage2022toy}.
A latent says what the model represents, not how it computes it.
That question belongs to circuit discovery, whose methods, over components or features and by search, attribution, or learned masks, predominantly target an output~\citep{conmy2023towards, syed2023attribution, marks2025sparse, ameisen2025circuit, bhaskar2024finding, caples2025scaling}.
Targeting latents instead can capture variables that never show up as a token, and, since latents are learned without labels, lets the model's activations rather than the tasks we thought to pose decide what to explain.

% DAN-31 (2026-10-27): CircuitLens is transcoder-based; Palit (probe target, ablation-tested) added; the claim now says
% what is intervened on (everything outside the circuit) and what is measured (the target).
Internal targets have been approached by attribution, tracing upstream of a chosen SAE or transcoder latent, or a probe direction, and thresholding the scores~\citep{ge2024automatically, golimblevskaia2025circuit, palit2026probes}.
To our knowledge, no method \emph{optimises} a circuit of upstream latents for a single target latent and tests it by ablating everything outside the circuit and measuring that latent.
We call this \emph{latent-targeted circuit discovery} (\cref{fig:overview}).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\resizebox{\linewidth}{!}{\input{figures/overview}}
\caption{How weighted circuit masking works.
\textbf{(a)}~For a target latent $f$, every latent at every upstream SAE site is a candidate node.
\textbf{(b)}~Each candidate gets a gate $m$ (kept when $\theta > 0$) and a coefficient $\alpha$: a kept latent contributes $\alpha$ times its live value; a closed one takes its ablation value: zero (Z), its activating- or contrast-context mean, Top-K-sparse (A, C), or a donor contrast context's code, redrawn each step (R).
\textbf{(c)}~Under all four, the circuit is trained to reproduce $f$'s pre-activation at its anchors, with a sparsity penalty on open gates.
\textbf{(d)}~The circuit, typically $10^{2}$--$10^{3}$ latents, passes on held-out contexts if faithful under Z, A, and C and necessary; sufficiency to induce, a donor-background test on held-out contexts, and same-size random circuits with fitted coefficients are reported, not required.}
\label{fig:overview}
\end{figure}

Weighted circuit masking (WCM) learns, for every upstream latent, a gate (whether it belongs to the circuit) and a scaling coefficient (how strongly it acts), trained so that the circuit reproduces the target under four ablations of every other latent at once, with the SAE reconstruction errors live (\cref{fig:overview}).
We test each circuit on held-out text for \emph{faithfulness} (the circuit, with every other latent ablated and the SAE errors live, reproduces the target), \emph{necessity} (removing it removes the target), and \emph{sufficiency to induce} (setting its members to their activating-context values in a silent context turns the target on).

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Circuit discovery.}
% 2026-11-14 (compression 02): the IG-variant note, "a substrate we do not run", Makou, the per-paper verbs, the
% unweighted-masking differences and MIB live in app:related; the run sizes in the abstract and intro.
After manual analysis~\citep{olah2020zoom, wang2023interpretability}, ACDC~\citep{conmy2023towards} and Edge Pruning~\citep{bhaskar2024finding} automated the search by pruning components or edges against an output metric, and attribution patching~\citep{nanda2023attribution, syed2023attribution, kramar2024atp} and its refinements~\citep{hanna2024faith, rezaeijafari2025relp} score them to first order.
Sparse feature circuits~\citep{marks2025sparse} moved the nodes to SAE features with error nodes, and attribution graphs~\citep{ameisen2025circuit, lindsey2025biology} build per-prompt graphs over cross-layer transcoder features~\citep{dunefsky2024transcoders}; all of these target an output.

\paragraph{Circuits for internal features.}
Internal targets are not new: attribution has traced the upstream subgraph of a chosen SAE feature~\citep{ge2024automatically}, transcoder feature~\citep{dunefsky2024transcoders, golimblevskaia2025circuit}, latent~\citep{duprelatour2025debugging}, or linear-probe direction~\citep{palit2026probes}, and dictionary features have been traced patch-free in Othello-GPT~\citep{he2024dictionary}.
Others rank upstream features by single ablations~\citep{cunningham2024sparse} or link them by sparse regression~\citep{yin2026scalable}.
Closer to ours, circuit probing optimises a mask over one block's neurons for a labelled intermediate variable~\citep{lepori2023uncovering}, and sparsely connected transcoders learn latent-to-latent masks while the dictionary is trained~\citep{drori2025sparsely}.

\paragraph{Masks and scalars.}
Learned masks descend from subnetwork probing~\citep{cao2021low, conmy2023towards} and edge pruning~\citep{bhaskar2024finding, li2024optimal}; over SAE latents they have localised attributes~\citep{chaudhary2024evaluating} and found behavioural circuits, with per-position masks under one per-token mean ablation~\citep{caples2025scaling}; moved to a single latent, this objective is our unweighted circuit masking (differences in \cref{app:related}).
Such masks keep a node at its natural scale or ablate it.
Learned per-unit scales serve pruning, steering, and regression~\citep{liu2017learning, kwon2022fast, stoehr2024activation, yin2026scalable}, and gate-times-magnitude parametrisations~\citep{louizos2018learning, lai2025joint} separate membership from strength outside circuit discovery.

\paragraph{Evaluating circuits.}
Optimal ablation learns the removed value per component~\citep{li2024optimal}, the dual of our per-node coefficients; ours stay fixed by data, and our sufficiency to induce is a denoising-style test~\citep{heimersheim2024patching}.
Prior work evaluates under several ablations but discovers under one; the nearest objectives sum two directions of one counterfactual~\citep{chen2025rethinking} or certify bounded patching perturbations~\citep{hadad2026formal}.
Size-matched random circuits~\citep{shi2024hypothesis, seth2026circuitkit} are unfitted, like the circuits they test; ours get our circuits' coefficient fitting.
Causal scrubbing~\citep{chan2022causal} is stricter (\cref{app:resample}).

What this paper adds is the conjunction: circuits of SAE latents \emph{optimised} for a single target latent, with a gate and a separate \emph{fitted} coefficient per node, trained under several ablations at once, and tested on held-out contexts against random circuits given the same fitting (further related work in \cref{app:related}).

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

% 2026-10-26 (DAN-147 stage a): the former "Setting" section is folded in here; its model details and the
% context protocol moved to "Experimental setup" (sec:setting, start of sec:results).
WCM applies to any model with a bank of Top-K sparse autoencoders, one per site (attention output, MLP output, and residual stream at each layer; GPT-2 small adds the residual stream between attention and MLP), each keeping $K$ latents active per position.
For a latent $f$ and a sequence--position pair $(x, t)$, write $a_f(x, t) \geq 0$ for its activation (after Top-K) and $\preact(x, t)$ for its pre-activation (before it); pseudocode is in \cref{app:pseudocode}.
% Running example (DAN-149, 2026-10-03): the case-study latent 9.resid.20419 replaces the pre-v1 temperature latent.
As a running example we follow a layer-9 residual-stream latent of TuringLLM that fires where a passage places its subject in classical Greek antiquity: after ``In the 5th century BC, the philosopher taught in the city of'' it reaches $79\%$ of its typical activation, and after ``In the 19th century, \ldots'' it stays silent (\cref{sec:case-study}).

\subsection{Problem}
\label{sec:problem}
\label{sec:evidence}

\paragraph{Targets and contexts.}
Fix a target latent $f$ and let $U_f$ be the latents at every SAE site that causally precedes $f$'s site.
Each target has \emph{activating contexts}, sequences on which it fires, each with an anchor at the target's peak and split into training and held-out sets, and \emph{contrast contexts}, sequences close to the activating ones on which it is verified silent, outside its site's Top-K (\cref{sec:setting,app:stores}).
For the running example they are history passages about other periods, such as the Enlightenment, Renaissance music, or the pre-Columbian era.

\paragraph{Circuit-only execution.}
A \emph{weighted circuit} is a node set $C \subseteq U_f$ with a scaling coefficient $\alpha_{s,i} > 0$ for each node.
% 2026-10-26 (DAN-147 stage b): the edit is defined once, in gated form; the hard circuit is its thresholding.
Under an ablation method $\pi$, the forward pass is modified at every upstream site $s$: with $c^{s}$ the site's Top-K code, computed from the (already modified) stream, gates $m_s \in [0, 1]^{d_{\mathrm{sae}}}$, and ablation value $c^{s}_{\pi}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:edit}
    x \;\leftarrow\; x + W_{\mathrm{dec}}^{s}\!\left[\, m_s \odot \alpha_s \odot c^{s} + (1 - m_s) \odot c^{s}_{\pi} - c^{s} \,\right].
\end{equation}
A circuit runs with binary gates, $m_{s,i} = 1$ exactly for its nodes: members are kept at $\alpha$ times their live value and every other latent takes its ablation value.
Editing only the decoded difference passes SAE errors through unchanged, and every downstream site, the target's included, reads the modified stream, so nodes are \emph{recomputed}, not clamped.
The ablation value is zero (Z), the latent's mean over activating contexts (A), or its mean over contrast contexts (C), optionally \emph{sparsity-preserving}, with the per-position Top-K re-imposed (\cref{app:respect-topk}); training adds a fourth, resample ablation (R), which takes it from a real contrast context.
Writing $a_C$ for the target's mean activation at the anchors under this intervention, $a_{\varnothing}$ for the empty circuit, and $a_{\mathrm{pos}}$ for the unmodified model, \emph{faithfulness} is
\[
    \phi^{\mathrm{free}}_{\pi}(C) = \frac{a_C - a_{\varnothing}}{a_{\mathrm{pos}} - a_{\varnothing}}.
\]

\paragraph{The problem.}
Faithfulness depends on the ablation method~\citep{miller2024transformer, mueller2024missed}, and a circuit trained under one method can fail under another (\cref{app:single-method}), so one weighted circuit is asked to serve all three:
\begin{equation}\tag{P}\label{eq:p2}
    n_{\varepsilon}(f)
    \;=\;
    \min \bigl\{\, |C| \;:\; \exists\, \alpha \ \text{ with } \ \phi^{\mathrm{free}}_{\pi}(C, \alpha) \geq 1 - \varepsilon \ \text{ for every } \pi \in \{\mathrm{Z}, \mathrm{C}, \mathrm{A}\} \,\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
WCM solves a differentiable relaxation of \labelcref{eq:p2}; with $\alpha = 1$ the smallest faithful node sets are several times larger (\cref{sec:weighted}).
Problem~\labelcref{eq:p2} asks for \emph{a} minimiser, and its near-optimal solutions form a large equivalence class (\cref{sec:underdetermination}), so only class-invariant quantities describe the target.

\subsection{Weighted Circuit Masking}
\label{sec:ablmask}
% BUDGET 1.5 pp. (The DAN-64 sweep varied the contrast source only, not lambda or the gamma weights; app:contrast-source.)
% RESTRUCTURE 2026-10-11 (DAN-161 step 1): the attribution-baseline summary (was 4.2) moved to app:attribution.

WCM learns the circuit directly rather than thresholding attribution scores (\cref{sec:attr-baselines}), adopting the optimisation framing of learned-mask circuit discovery~\citep{bhaskar2024finding, caples2025scaling} with a continuous-sparsification gate~\citep{savarese2020winning}, and moves its objective from an output to a single latent.
Each upstream latent gets a gate, which decides membership, and a coefficient, which decides strength; the sparsity penalty charges for membership only.
% 2026-10-26 (DAN-147 stage b): one loss display with every term, then one sentence per term.
At every upstream site $s$, WCM sets $m_s = \sigma(\theta_s / T)$ and $\alpha_s = \mathrm{softplus}(\psi_s)$ in \eqref{eq:edit}, with parameters $\theta_s, \psi_s \in \R^{d_{\mathrm{sae}}}$, and minimises
% 2026-11-08 (WCM v4, protocol.md §0.2): the resample term D (gamma_D = 1.0, donors = the 48 training contrast
% contexts), the censored read (temp 0.05) on Z, C, A, rank-keep 1e-1. Daniel 2026-11-08: the resample term is R
% (gamma_R, L_R; R_C in protocol.md) and rank-keep is K (gamma_K, K_pi); earlier drafts used D and gamma_R/R_pi.
% Code: LM:1878-1994, 2261-2444.
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{L} = {} & \frac{1}{\nu} \Bigl[ \sum_{\pi \in \{\mathrm{Z}, \mathrm{C}, \mathrm{A}\}} \gamma_{\pi} \Bigl( \operatorname{mean}\bigl[(\bar{z}_{\pi} - \bar{z})^{2}\bigr] + \gamma_{\mathrm{K}}\, \mathcal{K}_{\pi} \Bigr) + \gamma_{\mathrm{R}} \operatorname{mean}\bigl[(\tilde{z}_{\mathrm{R}} - z)^{2}\bigr] \Bigr] \\
    & + \lambda \sum_{s, i} \Bigl[ \sigma(\theta_{s,i}) + \bigl(1 - \sigma(\theta_{s,i})\bigr)\, \lvert \alpha_{s,i} - 1 \rvert \Bigr], \\
    & \nu = \operatorname{mean}\bigl[z^{2}\bigr], \qquad
    \mathcal{K}_{\pi} = \operatorname{mean}\Bigl[\operatorname{relu}\bigl(\tau^{(r)}_{\pi} - \tilde{z}_{\pi}\bigr)^{2}\Bigr], \qquad
    \bar{z}_{\pi} = \tilde{z}_{\pi}\, \sigma\Bigl(\frac{\tilde{z}_{\pi} - \tau^{(K)}_{\pi}}{T_{b}}\Bigr),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $z$ is the target's pre-activation $\preact$ at the anchors of the training contexts on the unmodified stream, $\tilde{z}_{\pi}$ its value under \eqref{eq:edit} with method $\pi$, $\bar{z}$ the same censored read of $z$ in the unmodified model, and the means run over those anchors.
% PROTOCOL v2 (2026-10-25, protocol.md §12.1; exps 090/093): A and C trained under the scorer's fill
% (train_fill "sparse", sparse_grad "ste", train_means_source "eval_ref"). v1 trained them under a dense mean.
Under A and C, training uses evaluation's sparsity-preserving fill, with straight-through gradients and evaluation's own means, since circuits trained against a dense mean rely on a background that the fill removes (\cref{app:respect-topk}).

\paragraph{The terms.}
% 2026-11-08 (review pass, phase 2): one sentence per term; the rationale for each (censored-read detail, rank-keep,
% guard, normaliser, lr schedule) moved to app:loss-details.
The squared error \emph{reproduces} the pre-activation rather than the activation, which Top-K censors at zero, so the loss keeps a gradient where the mask must learn.
Under Z, C, and A it compares \emph{censored} reads $\bar{z}$, which multiply the pre-activation by a soft Top-K gate ($\tau^{(K)}_{\pi}$ the $K$-th largest pre-activation of the site's other latents, width $T_b = 0.05\,\lvert z \rvert$), so these terms also see whether the target would survive its site's Top-K.
The \emph{rank-keep} term $\mathcal{K}_{\pi}$ is zero whenever the target holds its natural rank $r$ among its site's latents ($\tau^{(r)}_{\pi}$ the $r$-th largest of the others' pre-activations under the circuit).
The weights $\gamma_{\pi}$ score one mask under all three methods at once (\cref{app:single-method}).
The \emph{resample} term (R) replaces every latent outside the circuit with the sparse code of a donor, one of the target's training contrast contexts redrawn at every step, so the mask must also hold its target against real backgrounds; its donors are never used in evaluation.
The penalty is flat per latent, and the guard $\lambda\,(1 - \sigma(\theta))\,|\alpha - 1|$ stops a closed gate from carrying an inflated coefficient.
The temperature $T$ decays geometrically from $1$ to $0.05$~\citep{savarese2020winning}, and the circuit is $C = \{(s, i) : \theta_{s,i} > 0\}$.
Coefficients start at $\alpha = 1$, and \emph{unweighted circuit masking}, like any unweighted circuit, keeps them there.
Training runs AdamW for $400$ steps on batches of $4$; the weights, penalty, learning-rate schedule, and their provenance are in \cref{tab:protocol}, and each term's rationale in \cref{app:loss-details}.

\label{sec:eval}

\label{sec:eval-abl}

Every score in the main text is read on held-out contexts and reads the target's activation (after Top-K) in every term; \cref{tab:protocol} gives the full family.

\paragraph{Intervention scores.}
% PROTOCOL v1 (2026-09-22, DAN-7): necessity uses the SFC-completeness form
% below, on every model, replacing the earlier (A+ - A+_int)/(A+ - A-) form.
% Under a post-Top-K read a_empty is usually 0 and the ratio reduces to
% 1 - a/a_pos, which reconciles the engine and the standalone ports (DAN-65).
Let $A^{+}$ and $A^{-}$ be the target's mean activation on activating and contrast contexts.
\textbf{Sufficiency to induce}, $\phiind^{\alpha} = (A^{-}_{\mathrm{int}} - A^{-})/(A^{+} - A^{-})$, sets the circuit's nodes in contrast contexts to $\alpha$ times their mean at the activating anchors, with $A^{-}_{\mathrm{int}}$ the target's resulting mean activation: near 1, the circuit switches the target on.
\textbf{Necessity}, $\phisup(C) = (a_{\mathrm{pos}} - a_{M \setminus C})/(a_{\mathrm{pos}} - a_{\varnothing})$, is one minus the completeness score of \citet{marks2025sparse} with SAE errors preserved, $a_{M \setminus C}$ being the target's activation on activating contexts when the circuit's nodes are zeroed and every other latent is recomputed.
% 2026-09-22 (DAN-72): no per-node role labels in WCM. Leave-one-out effects are
% strongly non-additive and no signed subset passes a set-level test, so
% necessity and sufficiency to induce treat the circuit as one set.
Both treat the circuit as one set, and every score is an unclipped ratio: success is landing near $1$, and a score far above $1$ is overshoot.

\paragraph{Ablation methods.}
Mean ablation over activating contexts leaks target signal into the ablation value increasingly with depth, while mean ablation over contrast contexts does not (\cref{app:weighted-details,app:floors}).

\paragraph{Random-circuit baselines.}
% 2026-10-24 (paper prep, DAN-181; design in experiments/084-harder-nulls/README.md, decisions 2026-10-24): the two
% harder nulls added; the first null is DAN-17 (random_null.py).
Fitted coefficients could make any set look faithful, so every random circuit, size-matched site by site, gets the \emph{same coefficient fitting} on its own nodes.
Its members are latents active near the target's training anchors, matched to the circuit's on corpus frequency (\emph{anchor-active}) or also on contribution to the target (\emph{contribution-matched}; \cref{app:nulls}).

\paragraph{Pass criterion.}
% DAN-8, 2026-09-27. experiments/062-h100-protocol-v1/pass_rule.py applies it. 2026-11-08: specificity, the
% near-threshold stratum and the pre-activation pass rate moved to app:loss-details / tab:protocol (not yet reported).
A circuit passes if, on the held-out strongest contexts and the activation read, its faithfulness under Z and the sparsity-preserving A and C means lies in $[0.75, 1.5]$ and its necessity is at least $0.9$; R is not part of it, and \labelcref{eq:p2} is its idealised form.
The band is asymmetric: an overshoot up to $1.5$ still restores the target, a score below $0.75$ does not.
Necessity alone discriminates weakly: contribution-matched random circuits keep median necessity $0.66$ and $0.91$ (TuringLLM, GPT-2 small) yet never pass (\cref{app:nulls}).
Sufficiency to induce, specificity (\cref{app:specificity}), the random-circuit baselines, and a \emph{donor-background test} are reported, not required.
In the donor-background test, which patches from real contexts~\citep{heimersheim2024patching}, everything outside the circuit takes a donor context's code; a circuit passes it when it meets the pass criterion and its recovery under activating and held-out contrast donors lies in the same band (\cref{app:resample}).
A target is \emph{vacuous}, excluded and counted, when an empty-circuit baseline of the criterion already reaches $95\%$ of the unmodified activation, which depends on the target and its contexts alone (\cref{tab:protocol}).

\textbf{Targets} are sampled with no prioritisation heuristic.
On TuringLLM they are $8{,}000$ latents stratified over layer and site kind, drawn from the targets of the protocol-v1 run (\cref{app:v1-run}; its exclusions in \cref{tab:protocol}), and include the sweep, null-panel, case-study, and gallery targets.

The sparsity penalty was calibrated, and the pass criterion set, on the same TuringLLM frame, so those choices are not independent of the TuringLLM evaluation.

\textbf{Contexts.}

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Circuit size depends on the scaling convention.}
% 2026-11-08 (review pass): the drivers-closure claim (pre-v1 4-target analysis with the IG prefix-cut bug) and the
% v1 over-drive line (2.16, circuits without the resample term) were cut from the body; both stay in their appendices.
Unweighted circuits for deep TuringLLM targets need far more latents than weighted ones and pass less often at any size we fitted (\cref{sec:weighted}).
We hypothesise that a latent's activation, reached through an additive residual stream, is a dense function of many small, partly redundant contributions that fewer, rescaled latents can carry, so a circuit's size means something only alongside whether its coefficients were fitted.

\paragraph{Node sets are samples; pass rates are stable.}
% 2026-11-08: v1 stable core (175 targets) is in app:underdetermination, labelled. Never-claim: "function is stable".
A refit with reordered training contexts passes about as often on TuringLLM and Pythia-70m but shares a median Jaccard of only $0.39$--$0.51$ with the original circuit, and their shared core alone passes for $44$--$72\%$ of targets (\cref{tab:run2026-stable-core}): sizes and scores, not memberships, are properties of the target.

\paragraph{Latent targets reduce the experimenter's choice of what to explain.}

The bias moves rather than vanishes: which variables exist depends on the dictionary~\citep{leask2025canonical}, and reading a latent still needs human interpretation.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
% 2026-11-08 (review pass): one-liners; limitations that restated results (attention, mid-band, resample, all-position)
% were cut from the body; their evidence stays in app:production, app:resample and app:v1-run.
\textbf{Scope.} Three models of at most 254M parameters, Top-K dictionaries only (on dense ReLU dictionaries the fitted random-circuit baseline stops discriminating; \cref{app:dense-relu}), one sparsity penalty at a 400-step budget, and anchors only: a circuit explains the target where it fires, not at every position (\cref{app:resample}).
% 2026-11-15 (DAN-214): experiments/116-error-ablated-and-case-checks (paired, local GPU, no refit; refit pilot 18).
\textbf{A circuit is a fitted intervention, not a recovered mechanism.} % 2026-11-15 (Daniel): reframed. A circuit is judged in the regime it was fitted in; the error-zeroed refit is a
% result, not a failure. Numbers: experiment 121 (81 / 36 / 26 targets).
It is faithful in the regime it was fitted in, with the SAE errors live; fitted and scored with every upstream error zeroed, circuits pass for most targets ($69$, $67$ and $71\%$ on the three models, against $82$, $78$ and $82\%$ with the errors live), though a circuit moved from one regime to the other is not faithful there (\cref{app:error-ablation}). It inherits any ambiguity in its latents, and is validated by intervention effects, not by a planted ground truth: on $15$ GPT-2 latents at the outputs of published IOI and greater-than components, circuits keep the published positions and stages but not the published heads, which the full model's own attribution ranks first (\cref{app:known-circuits}).
\textbf{The objective is a surrogate.} WCM trains on the pre-activation, softened by the censored read, but is evaluated on the activation after Top-K, so near-threshold targets are ill-conditioned (\cref{app:protocol}).
\textbf{Faithfulness does not imply specificity:} rank-keep limits lifts past the target but leaves amplifier circuits (\cref{app:specificity}).
\textbf{Generality.} TuringLLM's synthetic corpus aids control but limits generality, which the public models ($512$ targets each) only partly address; contrast contexts are one of several defensible constructions (\cref{app:contrast-source}).

\section{Conclusion}
% BUDGET 0.25 pp.

% REWRITE 2026-10-12 (DAN-161 step 9): the two claims, then "so what?".
% 2026-11-08 (production run): numbers wait on the run's analysis; update with the abstract.
We asked what makes an internal latent fire, and answered with weighted circuit masking.
% 2026-11-08 (numbers pass): run2026_headline.csv, run2026_nulls.csv, run2026_tl_matched.csv, run2026_tl_sweep_bands.csv.
On three models at one sparsity penalty, $82$--$88\%$ of scored sampled targets ($81$--$82\%$ of all sampled targets) get a circuit of a few hundred latents that is faithful under zero and both mean ablations and necessary on held-out text, while random circuits of the same size and fitting never pass; fewer pass at depth and on weaker contexts.
Fitted coefficients keep these circuits small: without them the same nodes almost never pass, and for TuringLLM targets in the last four layers the smallest passing unweighted circuit is a median $13.8\times$ larger.
Because the targets are sampled rather than chosen, the results describe a population of internal variables, as recovered by one dictionary.
Composing circuits for single latents into chains, tested at every link, leads from dependencies to a map of the model's internal computation.

\subsection{The Loss Terms in Detail}
\label{app:loss-details}
% 2026-11-08 (review pass, phase 2): moved here from sec:ablmask and sec:eval.
\paragraph{Censored read.}
% DAN-219 (2026-11-16): first sentence shortened; it repeated the Objective row of tab:protocol (the gate formula,
% tau^(K) and T_b = 0.05 |z_b|), which keeps them.
Under Z, C, and A, both the circuit's pre-activation and the unmodified one pass through the soft Top-K gate of \cref{tab:protocol} (Objective).
The terms therefore also see whether the target would survive its site's Top-K, which is what evaluation reads, while keeping a gradient below the cut; the resample term and the normaliser $\nu$ use the uncensored pre-activation.

\paragraph{Rank-keep.}
Reproducing the pre-activation does not keep the target in its site's Top-K, since the loss constrains the stream along one direction of $d_{\mathrm{model}}$ and other latents at the site can rise past the target.
The rank-keep term, with $r$ the target's rank among its site's other latents at an anchor in the unmodified model (capped at $K$), is zero whenever the target holds that rank, so it never pulls a circuit towards a state the unmodified model does not have (rejected alternatives in \cref{app:rank-designs}).

\paragraph{Weights, guard, and normaliser.}
Under the contrast-context mean alone a mask learns the difference from that mean, never a set that suffices alone, and under zero ablation alone it is never asked what separates firing from a near-identical context that does not fire (\cref{app:single-method}); the weights $\gamma_{\pi}$ therefore score one mask under all of them.
The guard $\lambda\,(1 - \sigma(\theta))\,|\alpha - 1|$ is needed because, with coefficients free, the optimiser could hold a gate just below the keep threshold while inflating its coefficient, emptying the node set while keeping its effect.
All terms measure the same quantity on the same target, so one shared, bounded normaliser $\nu$ suffices (\cref{app:membership-studies}).
The gates are optimised directly rather than sampled~\citep{louizos2018learning, li2024optimal}; the circuit is the set of gates above $\tfrac{1}{2}$, which is the same at any temperature.
Coefficients start at $\alpha = 1$, so training starts exactly at unweighted behaviour, and the zero-ablation term anchors them (\cref{app:single-method}).

\paragraph{Learning-rate schedule.}
The learning rate warms up over the first tenth of the steps and then decays geometrically, which keeps its ratio to the gate temperature roughly fixed while the gates anneal; its peak restores the total step size of a constant rate.

\paragraph{Specificity, near-threshold targets, and the pre-activation read.}
Every score reads the target alone, so a circuit that restores the target by lifting its whole neighbourhood at the site passes them all; specificity (the target's rank, the latents lifted above the Top-K cut, and the faithfulness of co-firing \emph{sibling} latents) is a measured property, not a criterion (\cref{app:specificity}).
A \emph{near-threshold} target, whose median rank at the held-out anchors of the unmodified model is at least $K/2$, has an ill-conditioned activation read, since small shifts near the cut push it out of the Top-K.
A secondary pass rate applies the pass criterion to the pre-activation read, with Z and necessity measured from $0$ (Read, in \cref{tab:protocol}); its production results are in \cref{app:production}.

The protocol-v1 run (\cref{app:v1-run}) built its held-out sets by the 3:1 split of each stored pool, and an audit of every record found that they were not clean: $66.8\%$ of held-out strongest contexts repeated a training-side context, $35.6\%$ exactly, $50.2\%$ up to the anchor, and $55.1\%$ as a near-duplicate.
A context identical up to its anchor is the same input for every score, since each score reads the target at the anchor of a causal model.
The cause is the corpus construction: about half of all 64-token windows ($52.9\%$) were written to the interpretability corpus twice, the two copies have identical activations and so sit at adjacent ranks of a pool, and the split, which holds out one rank in every four, places most such pairs on both sides.
The mid-band held-out sets were nearly clean ($3.2\%$ of contexts identical up to the anchor, $0.4\%$ near-duplicates).

The production run therefore fits every model from records built so that this cannot occur.
For TuringLLM they are built afresh: each strongest pool is deduplicated before the split, removing exact, anchor-prefix and near copies and corpus twins; candidates beyond the stores' 64 slots come from a scan of the corpus's $18.2$M distinct windows for each target's $512$ strongest contexts, ranked by the stores' own statistic; the split is group-aware, so no group of copies straddles training and held-out contexts; and contrast contexts come from the same close, silence-checked selector, deduplicated by the exact and near rule, since they have no anchor.

\label{app:pseudocode}
% APPENDIX CUTS 2026-10-12 (tier 1): Algorithms 1-4 (contrastive / ablation / hybrid gradient discovery and

\Cref{alg:ablmask} summarises weighted circuit masking (\cref{sec:ablmask}) as run in the production run.

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Weighted circuit masking, WCM[Z+C+A] with the resample term and rank-keep, as in the production run (one target $f$; \cref{tab:protocol})}
\label{alg:ablmask}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
% 2026-11-08: v4 (protocol.md §0.2-0.3): resample term R, censored read, rank-keep 1e-1, geometric lr with warm-up.
% DAN-219 (2026-11-16): old notation "resample term D" in this comment -> R.
\Require target $f$; activating contexts $\posset$ (train on 32 strongest + 16 mid-band; hold out 16 + 16) and contrast contexts $\negset$ (48 train / 16 held out); $\lambda = 10^{-3}$; $\gamma_{\mathrm{C}} = \gamma_{\mathrm{A}} = 0.25$; $\gamma_{\mathrm{R}} = 1$; $\gamma_{\mathrm{K}} = 10^{-1}$; steps $S = 400$
\State target $z \gets$ natural pre-activation of $f$ at the training anchors (no-grad forward);\quad $\nu \gets \operatorname{mean}(z^2)$; \quad $\bar{z} \gets$ its censored read
\State $r \gets \min(K,\ $rank of $f$ among its site's other latents$)$ at each training anchor, unmodified model
\State ablation means: C $\gets$ site means over the 48 \emph{training} contrast contexts; A $\gets$ over the 48 strongest \emph{training} activating contexts (the evaluation's means); donor codes $\gets$ the 48 training contrast contexts
\State initialise $\theta_s = 4$, $\psi_s = \mathrm{softplus}^{-1}(1)$ for every upstream site $s$
\For{step $= 0, \ldots, S - 1$}
    \State $T \gets 0.05^{\,\text{step}/(S - 1)}$; \quad gates $m_s \gets \sigma(\theta_s / T)$; \quad coefficients $\alpha_s \gets \mathrm{softplus}(\psi_s)$ \Comment{geometric anneal $1 \rightarrow 0.05$}
    \State learning rate: linear warm-up over the first $S/10$ steps to $3.15 \times 0.05$, then geometric decay
    \For{$\pi \in \{\mathrm{Z}, \mathrm{C}, \mathrm{A}\}$} \Comment{same $\theta, \psi$ under every ablation method}
        \State forward with kept latents at $\alpha \times$ live value, closed latents at their $\pi$ ablation value; for C and A, the sparsity-preserving fill, straight-through (\cref{app:respect-topk})
        \State $\mathcal{L}_{\pi} \gets$ MSE of the censored read $\bar{z}_{\pi}$ against $\bar{z}$; \quad $\mathcal{K}_{\pi} \gets \operatorname{mean}\,\operatorname{relu}(\tau^{(r)}_{\pi} - \preact)^2$
    \EndFor
    \State forward with closed latents at a freshly drawn donor's sparse code (members kept, donor fills the Top-K); \quad $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{R}} \gets$ MSE of $f$'s pre-activation against $z$
    \State AdamW step on $\bigl[\sum_{\pi} \gamma_{\pi}(\mathcal{L}_{\pi} + \gamma_{\mathrm{K}}\mathcal{K}_{\pi}) + \gamma_{\mathrm{R}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{R}}\bigr]/\nu + \lambda \sum_{s,i} \bigl[\sigma(\theta_{s,i}) + (1 - \sigma(\theta_{s,i}))\,|\alpha_{s,i} - 1|\bigr]$ \Comment{penalties use $\sigma(\theta)$, not $\sigma(\theta / T)$}
\EndFor
\State circuit $\gets \{\,(i, \alpha_i) : m_i > \tfrac{1}{2}\,\}$; evaluate on the held-out contexts under \cref{sec:eval-abl}
\State \Return circuit
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Context Store Formalism}

% 2026-11-15 (DAN-218 #19): rewritten in sequence terms to match src/store/context.py (compute_seq_scores, l.37-54:
% mean activation per sequence; _update_top keeps the top sequences; _mid_band_bounds, l.242-252: band on the
% mean / std of the sequence scores, 0.5-1.5 sigma per config.py). Scan share: 111 out/run2026_a4.json
% (scan_share_strong_pct 99.999, store_rows 4).
% DAN-219 (2026-11-16): compressed from two displayed equations (TopCtx_K as an arg-top over the scored set; MidCtx_M
% as a reservoir sample) to prose with the same definitions (appendix review G, item 3). Old text in
% paper-backups/main-2026-11-16-pre-trim.tex.
The collection pass scores each corpus sequence $x$ of $T$ tokens for each latent by its mean activation over the sequence, $s_f(x) = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t} a_f(x,t)$; a context is thus a whole sequence, and its anchor is placed afterwards at the target's peak (\cref{tab:protocol}).
The top-context store keeps the $K_{\mathrm{top}}$ highest-scoring sequences, and the mid-context store a bounded reservoir sample of the sequences with $\mu_f + 0.5\,\sigma_f \leq s_f(x) \leq \mu_f + 1.5\,\sigma_f$, $\mu_f$ and $\sigma_f$ the mean and standard deviation of $s_f$ over sequences.
TuringLLM's production strongest pools come almost entirely from the corpus scan of \cref{app:heldout}, which ranks windows by the same statistic, rather than from this store.
Mid contexts distinguish a latent's ordinary operating regime from its most vivid examples and provide probe data where extreme contexts would be too narrow.

\paragraph{Contrast-context retrieval.}
% APPENDIX CUTS 2026-10-12 (tier 2): shortened (the retrieval equations were dropped); full text in
% paper/notes/main-before-appendix-cuts-2026-10-12.tex.
Each sequence $x$ is represented by $r(x) \in \R^{1024}$, the final layer's residual stream mean-pooled over positions, captured once during collection.
% DAN-219 (2026-11-16): two sentences merged into one (appendix review G, item 6).
The attribution baselines use a retrieval store, the $64$ sequences most similar by exact cosine search to the normalised centroid of a target's activating contexts, unchecked for silence, whose $2 \times 10^{5}$-sequence index leaves $38\%$ of TuringLLM targets on one shared default set.

\paragraph{Contrast contexts for WCM.}
Weighted circuit masking and its evaluation instead select contrast contexts per target at fit time.
Each of the target's strongest stored contexts $\posset^{\mathrm{top}}$ is represented, read from the index when present and otherwise computed by a forward pass; candidates from the retrieval store (the union of every latent's stored nearest neighbours, a subset of the $2\times10^5$-sequence index) are ranked by their highest cosine similarity to any of them, $\max_{x_p \in \posset^{\mathrm{top}}} \langle r(x_p), r(x) \rangle$, with all of the target's stored activating contexts (strongest and mid-band) excluded; the $128n$ most similar for $n$ requested contexts ($8{,}192$ for the 64 of the first protocol; $24{,}576$ for the deduplicated records, which request 192 so that deduplication can refill) are then run through the model in rank order, and each is kept only if the target is outside its site's Top-K at every position, until 64 are kept; the production counts of short sets are in \cref{tab:protocol} (Contrast contexts).

% "A target with fewer than 4 kept is skipped, and one with 4 to 63 uses what it has (19 and 10 TuringLLM targets in
% the protocol-v1 run)." and "In the production run, 9 TuringLLM targets kept 0--2 and got no record, 6 kept fewer than
% 64 (at least 6), and every Pythia-70m and GPT-2 small target kept 64." (111 out/run2026_a4.json).

The silence check rejects between 14\% and 51\% of the closest candidates on the targets we inspected, so unchecked retrieval would place many contrast contexts where the target fires.
The same selector with random or maximally dissimilar ranking gives the \emph{random} and \emph{distant} sources of \cref{app:contrast-source}.

\paragraph{Harder random circuits.}
Two harder nulls keep everything else and change only where the members come from.
The \emph{anchor-active} null draws, at each site, the production circuit's member count from latents active at the anchor or up to $8$ positions before it on at least one training context, matching each member's corpus-frequency decile; production members may be drawn.
The \emph{contribution-matched} null also matches each member's contribution to the target, the mean over training contexts of its activation times the gradient of the target's pre-activation, binned by deciles of the production members' own contributions, and excludes the production members: it is a substitution test, asking whether other latents of the same strength can stand in for the selected ones, as overlap-penalised search does for output circuits~\citep{chen2026rome}.
Each set is fitted exactly as above and scored on held-out and on training contexts, with fitted coefficients and with every coefficient at $1$; the latter is the size-matched, nothing-fitted null of prior work~\citep{shi2024hypothesis, seth2026circuitkit}, and once circuits carry fitted coefficients the fair control fits them on the random members too.

\section{Specificity}
\label{app:specificity}
% experiments/056-specificity (2026-09-23/24). Pilot numbers: 16 targets.

Faithfulness, necessity, and the random-circuit baseline all read the target alone.
A circuit can therefore restore its target by lifting the target's whole neighbourhood, its concept, topic, or context type, and pass all three: a concept amplifier rather than a mechanism for that latent.
The loss permits it, since reproducing the target's pre-activation constrains the site's stream along one direction of $d_{\mathrm{model}}$, and it leaks into the activation read only when the lifted latents overtake the target in its site's Top-K.

The diagnostic reads the whole code at the target's site, every latent's pre-activation and the SAE's Top-K set at the anchor, in the unmodified model, the empty circuit, and the fitted circuit under each ablation method, on the held-out contexts.
It reports the target's rank and Top-K membership; the number of latents lifted above the unmodified model's Top-K cut, against the empty circuit; the overlap of the circuit's Top-K set with the unmodified one; and the faithfulness of \emph{siblings}, latents in the unmodified Top-K at the anchor on at least half of the target's strongest training contexts, computed exactly as the target's, against a control group of the site's other active latents.
A circuit is flagged as an amplifier when, under at least one ablation method and on the pre-activation read, its target is restored to at least $0.5$ and its median sibling to at least $80\%$ of the target's faithfulness.

\label{app:error-ablation}

Every faithfulness run keeps each site's SAE error live.

Necessity is unchanged, since its numerator is a run of the unmodified model.

\section{Known-Circuit Check on GPT-2 Small}
\label{app:known-circuits}
% 2026-11-15 (DAN-215): experiments/117-known-circuits-gpt2/README.md (selection rule and pre-stated test written
% before any fit; three rule changes flagged there: eligibility frame, per-name name-mover latents, head shares of
% attention mass only with a 2x-uniform floor). Local RTX 5070 Ti; WCM v4, lambda 1e-3; L11 MLPs excluded for memory.
GPT-2 small has published circuits for indirect object identification (IOI)~\citep{wang2023interpretability} and greater-than~\citep{hanna2023how}, which gives a known-answer test of a circuit's structure.
By a rule fixed before any fit, we chose $15$ OAI v5 Top-K latents on those pathways: three name-mover outputs (per-name latents at layer 9, since name movers write the indirect-object name), three S-inhibition and three induction outputs (attention-output latents whose pre-activation is at least $57\%$ driven by the published heads, by an exact per-head decomposition), and six MLP latents at layers 8--10 that track the year at the final position.
Each was fitted with the production recipe on its corpus contexts and, separately, on task prompts.
The production circuits pass on held-out text for $7$ of $15$ targets, and the task-fitted circuits on $42$ of $60$ held-out task folds.
We attribute each target's pre-activation to every upstream latent on $128$ held-out task prompts, within the circuit and within the full model, split attention latents over heads, and compare shares with $20$ random member sets matched per site and with an even spread over heads.
The full model ranks a published head first for every target; the circuits do not: $1$ of $66$ head-class checks passes (share at least twice the null mean, above its 95th percentile, and at least twice the even rate), and in $65$ the circuit's share on the published heads is below the full model's.
What the circuits keep are positions and stages.
Five of six name-mover circuits put $51$--$95\%$ of their attribution on the indirect-object token (random sets $7$--$10\%$, full model $21\%$), four of six S-inhibition circuits are enriched at the second subject token, and five of eight greater-than circuits are enriched in late MLPs at the final position; their attention mass sits on early-layer heads at the content tokens rather than on the routing heads.
By the pre-stated verdict, the name-mover, S-inhibition, and greater-than structure is partly recovered and the induction structure is not.
% 2026-11-15 (DAN-215 rerun, errors zeroed): experiments/120-known-circuits-errors-zeroed/README.md.
A likely cause is that the published computation runs largely through the SAE errors, which every circuit keeps live (\cref{app:error-ablation}): GPT-2's attention and MLP dictionaries leave $19$--$28\%$ of the variance unexplained mid-stack.
Zeroing the error at every upstream site removes the published structure from the model itself: on the same held-out task prompts the error-free model's pre-activation turns negative for $14$ of the $15$ targets, its own attribution ranks a published head first for $3$ of $15$ (none at the head's role position), and its share on the indirect-object token falls from $21\%$ to $3$--$5\%$.
Circuits refitted and scored in that error-free regime do not recover the heads ($3$ of $66$ checks, two of them in circuits that fail the pass rule) and keep fewer of the positions ($6$ of $32$ checks, against $17$), so the error-free refit is not a known-answer test on GPT-2.
The attribution is first-order and the sample is $15$ targets at one sparsity penalty.
A weighted circuit is therefore not a head-level circuit-discovery method; it identifies which features, at which positions, a latent reads.

One untested explanation is redundancy in the basis: each residual-stream SAE re-encodes what earlier sites wrote, so latents at different sites can carry the same signal, and two fits may pick different carriers of it.

\paragraph{How the cases were found.}
% 2026-11-08: production scan from experiments/110-case-studies-v4/shortlist.md ("How they were found").
In the protocol-v1 run, we read passing circuits for targets at layer 6 or deeper ($2{,}238$ circuits) with tools that list, for each circuit, its members ranked by contribution at the target's anchor ($\alpha$ times the member's mean activation there times its decoder norm), each member's own top contexts and peak tokens, its engagement on the target's activating against contrast contexts, and its first-order logit effect.
% DAN-219 (2026-11-16): cut the clause ", and that run hand-written sentences through the clean model" (the
% Validation paragraph's probes say it).
Candidates were kept when a minimal pair of sentences separated the target cleanly.
In the production run, a token-driven topic scan read the strongest and contrast contexts of all $7{,}786$ fitted TuringLLM targets and counted physics, AI, and computing terms; passing targets at layer 5 or deeper, with most strongest contexts on one domain and an anchor that is not simply a domain word, were read by hand (about $75$), and $11$ were probed with in-domain, near-domain, and same-word-other-sense sentences, which found the fusion circuit.
Group names come from the members' own contexts and logit effects, not from automatic interpretation.

\paragraph{Validation.}
Each case is tested three ways.
\emph{Knock-outs}: the circuit is re-scored with one named group removed (its members ablated like any non-member), on the held-out strongest contexts with the production scorer, against the same number of members of similar contribution rank ($\pm 15$ ranks, excluding every named member and a generic core of latents that fire on most text), over $8$ random draws.
\emph{Probes}: the target's activation in the clean model on hand-written sentences, as a percentage of its mean activation on its held-out strongest contexts.
\emph{Specificity}: the diagnostic of \cref{app:specificity}.
\Cref{tab:case-studies} summarises seven cases; the remaining tables are in the repository (\texttt{experiments/063-case-studies} and \texttt{experiments/110-case-studies-v4}).

\section{What the Coefficients Do}
\label{app:weighted-details}
The coefficient distribution reports the production run; the natural-scale and member analyses use circuits of the protocol-v1 run (\cref{app:v1-run}), scored with its pass floor of $0.8$ on its leaky held-out sets; the production run's weighted-against-unweighted sweep and $\alpha = 1$ re-score are in \cref{sec:weighted}.

\paragraph{At natural scale.}
% 2026-11-08: the sentences below are the protocol-v1 run's detail (DAN-148 stage 3), kept as the earlier run's numbers.
% DAN-219 (2026-11-16): moved here, unchanged, from "The coefficient triad" (app:nulls).
In the protocol-v1 run (\cref{app:v1-run}), on $1{,}746$ circuits, median faithfulness falls from $0.94$, $0.95$, and $0.89$ with fitted coefficients to $0.00$, $0.09$, and $0.06$ at natural scale, under Z, A, and C.
At natural scale the nodes seldom overshoot the target: $4$ of the $1{,}746$ circuits exceed $1.5$ under any ablation method, all at attention-output targets.
More often they fall short of the empty circuit itself under activating-mean ablation ($18\%$; $7\%$ at layers 0--4, $32\%$ at 8--11, and $29\%$ of attention-output targets).
There the activating-context means that the members replace already carry more of the target (a median $1.16$ of a natural $3.06$) than the members do at their natural scale ($0.61$), and the fitted coefficients make up the difference; these circuits pass less often with fitted coefficients ($16\%$, against $51\%$ for the other targets).

\paragraph{What the coefficients do to members.}
% 2026-11-15 (DAN-219 decision 3): production coefficient distribution, from experiments/113-coefficients-shape/out
% run2026_coef_alpha_summary.csv (alpha_median, share_gt1) and run2026_coef_alpha_by_freq.csv (decile 9 = most
% frequent: median 1.0711 / 1.0026 / 1.0002; members 4.10M of 5.49M, 158k of 249k, 166k of 256k).
In the production circuits the median member coefficient is $1.11$, $1.01$, and $1.02$ on TuringLLM, Pythia-70m, and GPT-2 small, and $68\%$, $57\%$, and $58\%$ of members are amplified; members from their site's most frequent decile of latents, which make up most members, stay closest to $1$ (medians $1.07$, $1.00$, and $1.00$; \cref{fig:run2026-coef-alpha-dist}).
The member-value analysis that follows uses the protocol-v1 run.
% experiments/062-h100-protocol-v1/member_restoration.py -> out_restore/restore_v2.jsonl (2026-10-06): 600
% production circuits, 200 per depth band; 220,036 member-anchor pairs active in the unmodified model. Every number
% below is printed by REPORT=1 (and FIG=1 for the pooled shares). Edge groups: out of Top-K 9.7% of members (5.1% of
% the contribution), >= 3x 11.2% (3.3%), the rest 79.2% (91.6%), under A before alpha.
Comparing each member's value inside the circuit-only run with its value in the unmodified model, at the target's anchors on held-out contexts, shows that the coefficients do not return members to their natural values (\cref{fig:coef-members}a; $600$ circuits of the protocol-v1 run, $200$ per depth band).
Before the coefficients act, the ablated environment leaves $14\%$ of members unchanged, pushes $8$--$10\%$ out of their site's Top-K, and raises $11$--$14\%$ to three or more times their natural value; $28$--$30\%$ lie within $25\%$ of it.
After the coefficients fewer do ($22$--$23\%$), with the spread widening on both sides, and weighting members by their gradient-times-activation contribution to the target gives the same picture.
A member's coefficient is unrelated both to how far the ablation moved it and to how much it contributes (rank correlations within $\pm 0.1$).
What the coefficients change is the target.
With every coefficient set to $1$, the members alone bring the target to a median $0\%$, $19\%$, and $7\%$ of its natural activation under Z, A, and C ($21\%$, $24\%$, and $12\%$ of its pre-activation), against $94\%$, $97\%$, and $90\%$ with the fitted coefficients (\cref{fig:coef-members}b).
To first order the coefficients raise the members' summed contribution only about $1.6\times$, so most of their effect on the target arises in the layers between the members and the target; we report this as an observation, not a mechanism.
Deep and failing circuits also lose members to the ablated environment itself: weighted by contribution, $10\%$ of members of layer 8--11 circuits are pushed out of Top-K under contrast-mean ablation, against $1\%$ at layers 0--4, and $9\%$ in failing circuits against $1\%$ in passing ones.

In the production sweep, at natural scale ($\alpha = 1$), the smallest passing unweighted circuit of a target at layers 8--11 holds a median $6{,}124$ latents against $436$ with coefficients (\cref{fig:run2026-lambda-sweep}); we interpret the coefficients as locating most of that cost in redundancy, many latents each carrying a small share of a signal that fewer, rescaled nodes carry alone.

\paragraph{The coefficients are not a norm correction.}
% 2026-11-17 (Daniel approved, paper/supplement/drafts-2026-11-17.md item A). Source:
% experiments/127-norm-rescale/out/report.md (2026-11-17 GPU run; 216 production WCM v4 TuringLLM targets, 72 per
% depth band; held-out strongest, 16 rows; 062 merge rule). Per-site / per-circuit refits:
% experiments/118-coefficient-diagnostics/README.md section 2 (job 3, 54 of the 216 targets, 6 per band x kind).
With every coefficient at $1$, the member sites of a circuit-only run keep about half of their natural reconstruction norm (median ratio $0.53$ under Z), so the coefficients might only be restoring scale.
On $216$ TuringLLM production targets they are not.
An oracle that rescales every member site's reconstruction to its natural norm at each context and position, using the unmodified model, passes $6.9\%$ of targets on held-out contexts ($11.6\%$ when the decoder bias is left unscaled), against $2.8\%$ at natural scale and $81.5\%$ with the fitted coefficients, and passes none at layers 8--11.
On $54$ of these targets, a single scalar per site fitted to the pass rule itself passes $37\%$ ($0\%$ at layers 8--11), and a single scalar per circuit $33\%$.
What the fitted coefficients change is direction: under zero ablation the target SAE's input has a median cosine of $0.31$ to its natural value with them, against $0.03$ at natural scale and $0.005$ under the norm oracle, which also overshoots that input's norm ($1.40\times$ natural, against $1.04\times$ with the fitted coefficients).
Direction at the target's input is not the whole story either: under activating- and contrast-mean ablation the oracle that keeps the decoder bias matches the fitted direction ($0.44$ and $0.42$, against $0.39$ and $0.37$) yet passes $11.6\%$.
The coefficients therefore act as per-latent structure, not as a per-site gain.

Because $\alpha = \mathrm{softplus}(\psi)$ is never negative, a member below $1$ is a latent the fit kept but turned down rather than removed: $31\%$, $42\%$, and $41\%$ of production members on TuringLLM, Pythia-70m, and GPT-2 small.

In the unmodified model they push the target down ($55\%$ and $50\%$ have a negative gradient-times-activation contribution, against $35\%$ and $30\%$; together a median $-21\%$ and $-10\%$ of the target's natural pre-activation, against $+16\%$ and $+18\%$).

On GPT-2 small the circuit-only runs also hold them almost silent (before the coefficient a median $5$--$12\%$ of their natural value under Z, A, and C, against $56$--$63\%$); on Pythia-70m they do not ($55\%$ against $47\%$, over the members the ablation changes).
Their fitted level is the best one without refitting: scaling only these members by $0$, $0.5$, $1$, $1/\alpha$ (every one at $1$), and $2$ passes $25$, $34$, $35$, $31$, and $27$ of the $36$ GPT-2 small circuits and $23$, $31$, $36$, $30$, and $28$ of the Pythia-70m ones.
Removing them costs no more passes than removing the same number of members near $1$ ($19$ and $22$ pass).
Raising them to $1$ also lowers silent firing (\cref{app:off-term}; median $0.19$ to $0.04$ on GPT-2 small, $0.012$ to $0.007$ on Pythia-70m), but passes fall, and on GPT-2 small the donor test's recovery falls with it ($0.82$ to $0.67$ under A), so we do not read this as selectivity gained for free.

\section{Training Circuits to Stay Off}
\label{app:off-term}

The every-position reads of \cref{app:resample} show circuits that fire where the target is silent, and the donor-background test shows the same in production.
Set every member to its fitted coefficient times its live value on a held-out contrast context, give every other latent the code of another held-out contrast context, and count the positions at which the target enters its site's Top-K: the full model fires at none of them and the same members at $\alpha = 1$ at almost none, but the median passing production circuit fires at $45\%$ of them on TuringLLM, $7\%$ on GPT-2 small, and $1\%$ on Pythia-70m.
The firing is highest for attention targets, at depth, and in larger circuits.
Sufficiency to induce is high for the same circuits (median $0.80$--$0.90$), so the members carry the target's level, but only $0.8\%$, $4.0\%$, and $28.6\%$ of passing circuits both switch the target on ($\phiind^{\alpha} \ge 0.5$) and keep it off (firing at no more than $1\%$ of positions under every donor pool).
No training term reads the target on a silent context: Z, A, C, and the resample term all read it at the anchors of activating contexts.

\paragraph{The off term.}
We added a fifth data term over the same parameters.
On each of the target's $48$ training contrast contexts (the resample term's donors; the held-out contrast contexts are never used), members take $\alpha$ times their live value, every other latent takes the sparse code of another training contrast context, redrawn at each step, and the term charges any rise of the target's pre-activation $p$ above its unmodified value $p^{\mathrm{nat}}$, at every position:
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{off}} = \frac{w_{\mathrm{off}}}{N}\, \mathbb{E}_{\text{contexts, positions}} \Big[\mathrm{relu}\big(p - \max(p^{\mathrm{nat}}, 0)\big)^{2}\Big],
\]
with $N$ the normaliser shared by the other terms.
The term is stricter than the read it targets, since it also charges rises that stay below the Top-K cut.

\paragraph{Pilot.}
On a panel of $16$ GPT-2 small targets, chosen by cell (high firing, selective controls, and failing), each target was fitted with the production recipe and with the off term at $w_{\mathrm{off}} \in \{0.25, 1, 4\}$, on one local GPU; seven targets at layers 7--9 tripped the run's memory guard and are dropped from every arm, leaving $9$.
The term works and grows with its weight: median firing falls from $0.25$ to $0.14$, $0.06$, and $0.02$ of positions, and the mean activation at those positions from $0.125$ to $0.005$ of the natural anchor activation at $w_{\mathrm{off}} = 4$.
At that weight $9$ of $9$ targets pass (against $8$), $6$ pass while firing at no more than $5\%$ of positions (against $3$), median faithfulness moves by less than $0.01$, and median circuit size grows by $7\%$, though one target's circuit, the one that failed without the term, grows from $1{,}026$ to $2{,}606$ latents; fits take $1.4\times$ as long.
The three selective controls are unchanged.
The donor-background pass fell from $4$ to $3$ targets in every off arm.

\paragraph{Deep targets.}
% Source: experiments/131-off-term/out_deep/report.md (2026-11-20), panel from select_deep.py.
A second GPT-2 small panel of $12$ targets at layers 8--11 ($6$ at layers 10--11; $9$ firing at $20\%$ or more of positions in production and $3$ selective controls) was fitted in the same four arms on the same GPU, with no target dropped.
The term again lowers firing with its weight, from a median $0.58$ of positions to $0.37$, $0.20$, and $0.05$, but at depth it costs passes: $9$ of $12$ targets pass in every off arm against $10$ without it, and the targets that pass while firing at no more than $5\%$ of positions rise only from $3$ to $4$, at $w_{\mathrm{off}} = 4$.
At that weight the median circuit grows by $24\%$ ($52\%$ for the high-firing targets), and one layer-10 MLP target's circuit no longer moves the target at all ($Z = A = C = 0$); the three selective controls are again unchanged, and the donor-background pass again falls from $4$ to $3$.

\paragraph{Why production does not use it.}
Both panels are one model and one seed; at depth, where production firing is highest, the term buys lower firing with passes and size rather than adding selective passes; the firing at $w_{\mathrm{off}} = 4$ is still falling, so the weight is not settled; and the term costs size unevenly and one donor-background pass on both panels.
The production circuits therefore carry no off term, and the firing reported above is a property of every result in this paper.

\paragraph{A task endpoint.}
The analogous term on a behavioural endpoint, indirect object identification on GPT-2 small, where each training item has its own counterfactual prompt (the same template with a third name in place of the subject), charges any excess of the circuit's logit difference over the model's on that counterfactual.
Fitted at the attention and MLP sites with the residual stream live, the circuits already match the model there (mean excess $-0.002$ to $0.027$), so there is nothing for the term to correct: at $\lambda = 10^{-3}$ faithfulness on held-out items moves from $0.95$ to between $0.93$ and $1.01$ across hinge and squared forms and weights $1$ and $4$, and circuits grow by $1$--$10\%$; training half the rows on the counterfactual prompts instead grows them by $59\%$.

\section{Attribution Baselines and Position-Aware Node Sets}
\label{app:attribution}
\label{sec:attr-baselines}

WCM is compared against attribution methods that score every upstream latent and keep the top of a ranking.
All of them run on each target's training contexts and score a latent's effect on the target's \emph{encoder pre-activation} at the anchor,
\[
    \preact(x, t)
    = \bigl\langle W_e^{(f)},\, z(x, t) \bigr\rangle + b_e^{(f)},
\]
with $W_e^{(f)}$ the target's encoder row, $b_e^{(f)}$ its effective bias, and $z(x, t)$ the pre-SAE dense activation: unlike the activation, which Top-K censors to zero, it is defined on every forward pass, and gradients reach every upstream latent through identity passthroughs at each SAE site~\citep{marks2025sparse}, with the SAE errors at their clean values.

\subsection{Estimators}
% DAN-219 (2026-11-16): unused alias labels sec:modes, sec:cfgrad, sec:abgrad, sec:hybrid removed (0 refs).

The two gradient estimators share one path, from every upstream site's mean over the target's activating training contexts (its A ablation value, $\bar{c}^{s}$) to its natural code $c^{s}$, all sites interpolated together and held at their interpolated codes, and both multiply a gradient by $(c^{s} - \bar{c}^{s})$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Attribution patching} (AtP) takes the gradient at the clean point, giving the first-order estimate of mean-ablating each latent~\citep{nanda2023attribution, syed2023attribution, kramar2024atp}.
\item \textbf{Integrated gradients from the mean} (IG)~\citep{sundararajan2017axiomatic} averages the gradient over $10$ points of the path. It follows the $\mathrm{IE}_{\mathrm{ig}}$ node estimator of \citet{marks2025sparse}, the node-level, activation-space counterpart of EAP-IG~\citep{hanna2024faith}, in form but not in detail: theirs interpolates one submodule at a time with the rest of the model live (as in their released code), starts from a zero patch when no counterfactual exists, and scores the error terms as nodes.
\end{itemize}
The two differ only in where they linearise, with target, contexts, path, and selection held fixed.
% DAN-219 decision 1 (2026-11-16): the adapted Ge baseline was removed (it breaks the true-baselines rule; the real
% comparison with ge2024automatically comes from arena A). Cut item: "\textbf{Hierarchical attribution} applies the
% gating algorithm of \citet{ge2024automatically} to our SAE sites. Without transcoders our graph is not linear, so its
% scores are first-order gradients rather than their exact linear attributions, aggregated over contexts rather than
% per prompt; its threshold is bisected towards the weighted circuit's size, and every bisection point is scored."
% The lead-in "Two further baselines use them:" became "A further baseline uses them:".
A further baseline uses them:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Ranked members with fitted coefficients} fill the weighted circuit's per-site counts from the top of a ranking at each site, then fit coefficients exactly as for random circuits (\cref{sec:eval}: only the coefficients move, with no sparsity penalty), and are scored at $\alpha = 1$ and fitted. The rankings are IG, the latent's mean activation at the target's anchor over the training contexts (no gradient), and the correlation of that activation with the target's across contexts (co-activation). Against WCM, these rows separate what the selection contributes from what the coefficients contribute.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Rankings and Node Sets}
\label{sec:membership}
% 2026-10-24 (paper prep, DAN-159): retitled (was "Node Sets and Position-Aware Discovery"); the three rankings are
% 083's max, agg (mean summed |attr|) and sfc (|mean over contexts of the summed signed attribution|).

Attribution returns a score for every latent at every context and position, so the baselines must decide how the scores form one ranking and which node set a ranking defines.
Three rankings collapse contexts and positions: a latent's largest $|$score$|$ at any context and position; the mean over contexts of its summed $|$score$|$ over positions; and the magnitude of the mean over contexts of its summed signed score, the aggregate ranking of prior work~\citep{marks2025sparse}.
A truncated baseline keeps the top $n$ latents of one ranking, with $n$ the target's weighted-circuit size.

At natural size the Top-K constraint shapes the node set: the code is $k$-sparse \emph{per position} while a target reads its whole causal prefix, so a position-collapsed circuit must cover the union of every position's active set, thousands of distinct latents per site, or recomputed faithfulness collapses.
The baselines' node set is therefore a set of latents, the same kind of object \labelcref{eq:p2} optimises over, collected across positions.
Latents outside the circuit are ablated everywhere; nodes are not forced to any value: they are recomputed from the modified stream at every position, so positions instantiate naturally (allow, don't force), exactly the circuit-only execution of \cref{sec:problem}.
Discovery is position-aware to match: attribution is computed per position, a percentile cut on $|$attribution$|$ (pooled over sites and positions, resolved per batch of four contexts) keeps individual (latent, position) entries, and the node set is the union over the positions of the target's causal prefix.
With a mean-ablation path nearly every entry is nonzero, so the median cut keeps about half of them, which is why these unions are large (the natural-size rows of \cref{tab:matrix}).
No template or cross-example alignment is required.
Both attribution signs are kept, as a design principle rather than an implementation detail: the node set is \emph{stream reconstruction}, not a selection of what drives the target, and a negative-attribution latent still carries part of the residual stream that nodes are recomputed from, so removing it corrupts recomputed execution.

\subsection{Sparsity-Preserving Mean Ablation}
\label{app:respect-topk}
The classic dense mean ablation assigns every latent outside the circuit its mean, producing a stream with tens of thousands of simultaneously active latents per position, outside the state space a Top-K SAE can produce.
The sparsity-preserving variant applies the means and then re-imposes the per-position Top-K (kept latents, then the largest means of the other latents up to $k$, rest zero), which restores the keep-all identity to exactly $1.0$.
We use it as the cleaner protocol, not because it changes scores in one direction; at small node sets and tight Top-K budgets the two variants can disagree on \emph{rankings}, which is why the body's comparisons name their ablation method.

\paragraph{The same fill in training.}

\subsection{Loss Terms for Target Rank and Specificity}
\label{app:rank-designs}

An \emph{off-target} term charging every other site latent for rising above its unmodified and empty-circuit values cut lifts three- to sevenfold at no faithfulness cost, but addressed a property the method need not guarantee (\cref{app:specificity}) and grew circuits when it also charged harmless lifts below the cut.

The method is scoped to Top-K dictionaries because of an exploratory test on Pythia-70m with the L1-trained ReLU dictionaries of \citet{marks2025sparse}: six residual-stream targets at layers 2 and 4, an earlier form of weighted circuit masking, and held-out contexts.

\paragraph{Why close contexts generalise worst.}
Variety alone does not explain it.
Fused contexts span the widest range of similarity to the target's activating contexts (10th to 90th percentile $0.25$ to $0.87$, against $0.82$ to $0.90$ for close, $0.47$ to $0.77$ for random, and $0.19$ to $0.27$ for distant), yet fused-trained circuits do no better than random-trained ones ($-0.019$ $[-0.054, 0.017]$).
What close contexts have that the others lack is the target's topic: they sit nearest the activating contexts and nearest to firing (median pre-activation $3\%$ of the activating level, against $0$ for random and distant).
A circuit trained against their mean keeps the topic's features in its background and learns only what separates the target from near misses; against a background without those features it falls short.
The same circuits, re-scored with the C mean taken from the nearest, middle or farthest third of each target's random set, show this within a single source: close-trained circuits pass $0.40$, $0.40$ and $0.33$ as the background moves away from the target's topic (nearest minus farthest $+0.068$ $[0.039, 0.100]$), while random-trained circuits pass $0.43$, $0.44$ and $0.41$ ($+0.014$ $[-0.007, 0.036]$).

The production run uses close contrast contexts on all three models, so that the model is the only change between them.
For new work, random or distant contrast contexts give circuits whose contrast faithfulness depends less on the background they are evaluated against.

\paragraph{Discovery methods and their settings.}
% DAN-219 (2026-11-16): two sentences cut as repeats of the body's Related Work (appendix review F): "Each family of
% discovery methods is built for its own setting." and "ACDC~\citep{conmy2023towards} removes edges ... and Edge
% Pruning~\citep{bhaskar2024finding} optimises an edge mask ...; both work over components such as heads and MLPs, and
% both are anchored at an output rather than a latent." Both keys stay cited below and in the body.
Attribution patching~\citep{nanda2023attribution, syed2023attribution} replaces each activation patch with a first-order estimate, AtP*~\citep{kramar2024atp} adds two changes that address the two classes of failure behind its false negatives, and EAP-IG~\citep{hanna2024faith} scores edges by integrating gradients along a path from corrupted to clean input embeddings.
% DAN-219 (2026-11-16, hygiene): plain-prose "whose comparison with WCM is pending" -> "has not yet been run".
The path-attribution baseline of \cref{sec:attr-baselines}, whose comparison with WCM has not yet been run, adapts this estimator to nodes, integrating jointly over the upstream SAE sites from their mean-ablation values.
Transcoders~\citep{dunefsky2024transcoders} replace MLPs with sparse, wider layers whose circuits factorise into input-dependent and input-invariant terms, attribution graphs~\citep{ameisen2025circuit, lindsey2025biology} build one graph per prompt over cross-layer transcoder features, and PIE~\citep{chen2026pie} prunes cross-layer transcoder features by their gradient-weighted write contributions before interpreting them.
The transcoder-based methods need a substrate our SAE bank does not provide, and produce a graph per prompt where we fit one circuit over many contexts, so we do not run them here.

\paragraph{Masks, scalars, and ablations.}
Learned masks for circuit discovery descend from subnetwork probing~\citep{cao2021low}, applied to computational-graph vertices by \citet{conmy2023towards} and to edges by Edge Pruning~\citep{bhaskar2024finding} and by uniform gradient sampling with optimal ablation~\citep{li2024optimal}, on $L_0$-gate and continuous-sparsification machinery~\citep{louizos2018learning, savarese2020winning}.
Masks over SAE latents have been learned to localise attributes~\citep{chaudhary2024evaluating}, a procedure adopted by benchmarks~\citep{mueller2025mib, karvonen2025saebench}, and to find behavioural circuits by continuous sparsification, with a mask per token position and a single per-token mean ablation~\citep{caples2025scaling}; with its coefficients fixed at 1, its objective moved to a single latent, one mask shared across positions, and four training ablations (zero, two means, and a resample ablation over contrast contexts) with the sparsity-preserving fill and rank-keep in place of one mean ablation, the latter is our unweighted circuit masking.
Every such mask keeps a node at its natural scale or ablates it.
The continuous coefficients of \citet{li2024optimal} interpolate between the two and play the role of our gates; what they learn besides is a constant for each \emph{removed} component, the dual of our scale for each kept one.
That constant minimises the ablated model's expected loss, so a component's importance is what it carries beyond the best constant; we keep the ablations fixed and defined by data (zero, the means over activating and over contrast contexts, and real contrast contexts), so that what absence means is set before training rather than fitted with the circuit.

Learned per-unit scales appear with other jobs: scales that double as the pruning criterion~\citep{liu2017learning, huang2018data, you2019gate}; scales for the units kept after pruning, fitted after the mask is fixed~\citep{kwon2022fast, he2017channel}; steering multipliers~\citep{stoehr2024activation}; sparse fitted weights on SAE latents for a task vector~\citep{kharlapenko2025scaling}; regression weights on feature-to-feature edges~\citep{yin2026scalable}; and, after pruning, a fitted logit scale and shift together with manual rescaling of a few nodes~\citep{gao2025weightsparse}.
\citet{gao2025weightsparse} warn that rescaling nodes can hide superposition, letting a few rescaled nodes stand in for many, the concern our fitted random circuits test (\cref{app:nulls}).
% DAN-219 (2026-11-16): shortened; the rescaling-null argument is stated in full in "Nulls, variance, and
% non-uniqueness" below (was: "; our random circuits receive the same coefficient fitting as the circuits they control
% for, which tests whether rescaling alone makes a set faithful").
% DAN-31 (2026-10-27, reading C): why CircuitLasso is not a baseline.
CircuitLasso~\citep{yin2026scalable}, run among others on GPT-2 small with OpenAI's sparse autoencoders, the dictionary family of our GPT-2 small run, links features one layer pair at a time by an observational sparse regression, whose coefficients its authors note are not causal effect sizes, and scores its circuits on training data; we do not run it, since our question is which latents suffice under intervention on held-out contexts, though its coefficients could rank members for the comparison of \cref{sec:method-comparison}.
Gate-times-magnitude parametrisations separate membership from strength in sparse training and fine-tuning~\citep{louizos2018learning, guo2021parameter, lai2025joint}, where a closed gate means no change or zero rather than an ablated node.
Feature-preserving subnetworks have also been pruned by saliency in vision models~\citep{hamblin2022pruning}.

Circuits are evaluated under one or several ablations~\citep{miller2024transformer} but discovered under one; MIB compares discovery under counterfactual, mean, and optimal ablations while evaluating under counterfactual patching alone, and circuits discovered under the evaluation's own ablation score best~\citep{mueller2025mib}.
The nearest objectives sum the clean-to-corrupted and corrupted-to-clean directions of one counterfactual~\citep{chen2025rethinking}, or certify a circuit against every patch value in a bounded set~\citep{hadad2026formal}.
% 2026-10-23 (Daniel): the sentence reading the three-ablation objective as resample ablation was cut; exp 073 contradicts
% it (app:resample: 0.1% of circuits pass a resample test).
Training under several ablations is the circuit-level counterpart of attribution averaged over several baselines~\citep{kapishnikov2019xrai, erion2021improving}; since the production protocol, it includes a resample ablation over training contrast contexts, and $69\%$, $66\%$, and $41\%$ of targets on the three models also pass the donor-background test, whose contrast donors are never training contexts (\cref{app:resample}).

\paragraph{Nulls, variance, and non-uniqueness.}
Random circuits as a null follow \citet{shi2024hypothesis}, who test whether a candidate is more faithful than $90\%$ of $100$ random circuits built from random input-to-output paths, with the random circuits' size varied from the candidate's own up to most of the model, and CircuitKIT~\citep{seth2026circuitkit}, which compares a circuit with size-matched random subgraphs by an improvement ratio and a $z$-score.
In both, the random circuits are sampled and ablated but not fitted, which matches circuits that are not fitted either.
Our circuits fit a coefficient per node, and fitting alone might rescale an arbitrary set into reproducing a target, so our random circuits receive the same coefficient fitting before scoring (\cref{sec:eval}); with every coefficient at 1 they reduce to the nothing-fitted null.
The broader concern is the one \citet{meloux2025dead} raise: interpretability methods produce plausible explanations even for randomly initialised networks, so findings are better treated as statistical estimates tested against alternative hypotheses.
% DAN-219 (2026-11-16): cut as a near-verbatim repeat of the body (Related Work) and app:resample: "Causal
% scrubbing~\citep{chan2022causal} is a stricter test in this spirit, resampling everything outside the hypothesis from
% other inputs (\cref{app:resample})." (chan2022causal stays cited in the body and app:resample.)

Circuit discovery is also variable.
EAP-IG circuits vary with the data batch and the prompt template, and their faithfulness varies widely across individual examples~\citep{wu2026demystifying}; on one task, defensible analytic choices change the derived claim for most pairs of specifications~\citep{mahale2026explanation}; and Certified Circuits stabilise membership by randomised subsampling of the data, keeping only components included consistently~\citep{anani2026certified}.
On small networks where every circuit can be enumerated, many circuits reproduce the same behaviour exactly~\citep{meloux2025everything}, and in language models structurally distinct, low-overlap circuits support the same task~\citep{chen2026rome}, and circuits for one task found on different inputs differ in structure, yet a shared core recovers their performance~\citep{makou2026many}.
Our node sets are non-identifiable in the same way, which is why a circuit's function, not its exact member list, is what we report as the finding (\cref{app:underdetermination}).

\paragraph{Self-repair and inhibition.}
Ablation-based tests meet compensation: when one attention layer is ablated, later layers make up part of its effect~\citep{mcgrath2023hydra}; the self-repair of single heads persists over the full training distribution, partial and variable across prompts, carried in part by the final LayerNorm scale and by sparse anti-erasure neurons~\citep{rushing2024explorations}; and copy-suppression heads, which lower the logit of a token that earlier layers predict when it already appears in the context, account for $39\%$ of the self-repair that follows ablating the name-mover heads of the IOI circuit~\citep{mcdougall2024copy}.
Conditional co-ablation finds backup components that become important only once a primary set is removed~\citep{gong2026conditional}.
Our scores intervene on the circuit as one set (\cref{sec:eval}), so they measure what the set does with any compensation inside the model included; necessity in particular is scored with the complement recomputed, so backups outside the circuit are free to respond, while faithfulness training holds the complement at its ablation value and so never rewards including dormant backups; per-member roles, where backups matter most, are not claimed.

\paragraph{Position-aware circuits.}
Position handling divides prior work into three strategies, each right for its setting: scores summed over positions, with nodes kept per position as an option of the released code~\citep{marks2025sparse}, per-instance graphs built one prompt at a time~\citep{ameisen2025circuit}, and schema-aligned positions across variable-length examples~\citep{haklay2025position}.
WCM needs none of these commitments: one mask is shared across positions and nodes are recomputed at every position, which suits the setting none of the three targets, one reusable circuit over arbitrary non-templatic text; our attribution baselines instead select per position and take the union over the causal prefix (\cref{sec:membership}).

\paragraph{Contrast contexts.}
Retrieved non-activating examples have been used to falsify feature explanations: \citet{ma2025revising} select the sentences most similar to a feature's top activations and keep those on which it is zero at every token, and EleutherAI's \texttt{delphi} library draws nearest-neighbour hard negatives per activating example for explanation scoring~\citep{eleuther2024delphi}. Explanation scores collapse to chance when negatives come from neighbouring features~\citep{eleuther2024autointerp}, convergent with our protocol-v1 finding that close contrast contexts generalise worst to other backgrounds (\cref{app:contrast-source}). Retrieval-based corruptions have also been used for circuit discovery on a behaviour, as the nearest non-memorised samples in the model's own embedding space~\citep{lasy2025memorization}, and \citet{oneill2024sparse} design corrupted negatives for task-level circuit identification.
We use retrieved, silence-checked contexts per latent as the background a circuit is trained and evaluated against (the C mean, the resample donors, and the held-out set for sufficiency to induce), rather than for explanation scoring or as one paired corruption, and treat their construction as a controlled variable with measured consequences.

\paragraph{The SAE basis.}
Static structure among latents is well documented: co-occurrence and decoder geometry reveal modularity~\citep{li2025geometry}, coactivation-derived components steer behaviour~\citep{deng2026coactivation}, meta-decompositions show latents are not atomic~\citep{leask2025canonical}, cross-layer feature-flow graphs track latents through the forward pass~\citep{laptev2025feature}, though decoder similarity misses most ablation-validated feature transitions through an MLP layer~\citep{droste2026decoder}, and Jacobian SAEs train adjacent dictionaries for sparse computation between them~\citep{farnik2025jacobian}.
But that structure is correlational, geometric, or weight-based, where the relationships discovered here are context-conditioned and intervention-tested.
Weight-based connections need that test: in a one-layer model expanded into virtual weights between transcoder features, many large weights are \emph{interference weights}, irrelevant or harmful to behaviour, and raw weight magnitude separates them poorly from the weights that matter~\citep{turner2026interference}.
Intervention-based latent graphs have been mapped at scale in single-cell foundation models, by ablating one SAE feature at a time and measuring the change in downstream features, finding that most edges are ones whose removal lowers the downstream feature (termed inhibitory there)~\citep{kendiukhov2026causal} and a heavy-tailed hub structure with redundancy that grows with interaction order~\citep{kendiukhov2026exhaustive}; those graphs measure the effect of one feature at a time, where our circuits are fitted jointly for each target and tested as a set.
The basis itself is imperfect: reconstruction errors are pathological~\citep{gurnee2024pathological}, although much of the error is linearly predictable from the activation~\citep{engels2025dark}; SAEs trained from different seeds learn largely different features, Top-K ones most of all~\citep{paulo2025sparse}; downstream utility is mixed~\citep{smith2025negative}, and in causal-variable localisation SAE features are no better than neurons~\citep{mueller2025mib}.
% DAN-219 (2026-11-16): "; and units are not canonical~\citep{leask2025canonical}" cut (second citation of the same
% point in this paragraph; the key stays cited above).
Interventions on the basis are imperfect too: an optimised perturbation can recover a behaviour suppressed by clamping SAE features, through the reconstruction residual and with the features still clamped~\citep{cui2026sae}; the effect of ablating single-token features on their token's logit differs more across SAE families than across model scales within a family~\citep{cho2026single}; and dictionaries trained only on what an affine map from an earlier layer cannot explain recover more of the loss when several layers are replaced at once, at most sparsity levels~\citep{poduval2026resae}.
This conditions our claims: reconstruction residuals stay as explicit anchors, evaluation intervenes on measured activations, and the SAE basis is treated as a useful but imperfect sample of the model's internal variables.
