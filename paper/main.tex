\begin{abstract}
Circuit discovery typically explains an output: a logit, a probability, a behavioural metric.
We ask instead what makes an internal latent fire.
For a target sparse autoencoder (SAE) latent, \emph{weighted circuit masking} (WCM) learns which upstream latents belong to its circuit and a scaling coefficient for each, trained so that the circuit alone reproduces the target under zero ablation and under mean ablation over activating and contrast contexts at once.
On $15{,}046$ sampled TuringLLM latents, $46\%$ of weighted circuits pass one pre-stated criterion on held-out contexts, most often for shallow and residual-stream targets and least often for attention outputs \pending{Pythia-70m and GPT-2 small, DAN-40/41}.
The resulting weighted circuits hold $10^{2}$--$10^{3}$ upstream latents and, where they pass, reproduce their target on held-out contexts, while the same nodes at their natural scale reproduce almost nothing and random circuits given the same coefficient fitting fail.
% 062 sweep (DAN-76, 190 targets, protocol v1, 2026-09-27): smallest circuit with worst-of-3 |faith - 1| <= 0.3,
% unweighted circuit masking (alpha = 1) vs WCM, median nodes: L8-11 9,989 vs 789 (14.1x); L5-7 3,862 vs 447 (9.8x);
% L0-4 502 vs 128 (3.7x). The 10^4-10^5 figure belongs to ATTRIBUTION at natural scale (sec:weighted, drivers-closure).
The coefficients therefore locate a redundancy that, for deep targets, costs ${\sim}10^{4}$ latents at natural scale, about $14\times$ the weighted circuit.
Node sets are not identifiable: refits that leave faithfulness unchanged share only $40$--$85\%$ of their nodes, so node sets are reported as equivalence classes.
The procedure needs no behavioural task and applies to any latent in an SAE bank.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% PAGE BUDGET (DAN-24): main body <= 12 pages, ending at \label{end-of-main}.
% Intro 1.5 | Related 0.75 | Setting + problem + contexts 1.25 | WCM 1.5 |
% Evaluation 0.75 | Results 4.5 | Discussion + Limitations 1.25 |
% Conclusion 0.25 | buffer 0.25. This section: 1.5 pp including Figure 1.
Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) decompose a language model's activations into latents that often track coherent concepts~\citep{cunningham2024sparse, bricken2023monosemanticity, gao2024scaling, templeton2024scaling}, a working answer to superposition~\citep{elhage2022toy}.
A latent says what the model represents; it does not say how the model computes it.
That question belongs to circuit discovery, whose methods predominantly target an output: a logit, a probability, a behavioural metric.
This holds whether the nodes are architectural components~\citep{conmy2023towards, syed2023attribution} or sparse features~\citep{marks2025sparse, ameisen2025circuit}, and whether the circuit is found by search, attribution, or learned optimisation~\citep{bhaskar2024finding, kharlapenko2025scaling}.
Output targets bound the explanation to variables we can name as behaviours, while a model computes many internal variables that never surface as any single output.

Latent targets have been approached by attribution: methods that trace upstream of a chosen SAE latent on prompts where it fires and threshold the scores~\citep{ge2024automatically, golimblevskaia2025circuit}.
The optimised cell of this grid is, to our knowledge, empty: a circuit of SAE latents \emph{optimised} for a single target latent's activation and tested by intervening on that latent.
We call this \emph{latent-targeted circuit discovery}, and work in that cell (\cref{fig:overview}).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\resizebox{\linewidth}{!}{\input{figures/overview}}
\caption{How weighted circuit masking works.
\textbf{(a)}~For a target latent $f$, every latent at every upstream SAE site is a candidate node.
\textbf{(b)}~Each candidate gets a gate $m$ (kept or closed) and a scaling coefficient $\alpha$: a kept latent contributes $\alpha$ times its live value, and a closed one is replaced by its ablation value. One set of gates and coefficients is used under three ablation methods at once.
\textbf{(c)}~They are trained so that, under each ablation method, the circuit reproduces $f$'s pre-activation at its anchor positions, with a price on every open gate.
\textbf{(d)}~The resulting weighted circuit, typically $10^{2}$--$10^{3}$ latents, is tested on held-out contexts by faithfulness, necessity, and sufficiency to induce, against random circuits of the same size given the same coefficient fitting.}
\label{fig:overview}
\end{figure}

Our method, \emph{weighted circuit masking} (WCM), learns for every upstream latent a gate, whether it belongs to the circuit, and a scaling coefficient, how strongly it acts.
Both are trained so that the circuit alone reproduces the target's pre-activation, jointly under zero ablation and under mean ablation over activating and contrast contexts.
The result is a \emph{weighted circuit for a target latent}: a node set with one coefficient per node, reusable across the target's contexts.
We score it on held-out contexts by three tests: \emph{faithfulness} (the circuit alone reproduces the target where it fires), \emph{necessity} (removing the circuit removes the target), and \emph{sufficiency to induce} (setting the circuit's values switches the target on in contrast contexts), each against random circuits given the identical coefficient-fitting budget.

The main result is how often such circuits exist and how small they are.
On $15{,}046$ sampled TuringLLM latents, $46\%$ of weighted circuits pass ($65\%$ of residual-stream, $52\%$ of MLP, and $14\%$ of attention-output targets), with a median of $494$ upstream latents and $91\%$ within $10^{2}$--$10^{3}$ \pending{Pythia-70m and GPT-2 small, DAN-40/41}.
The coefficients are what make the circuits small: the same nodes at their natural scale reproduce almost nothing, faithfulness at natural scale needs ${\sim}10^{4}$ latents for deep targets, about $14\times$ more, and random circuits of the same size with fitted coefficients fail.
No behavioural task enters at any point, so the procedure applies to any latent in an SAE bank.
We contribute:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Weighted circuit masking} for latent-targeted circuit discovery (\cref{sec:ablmask}): a learned node set with fitted scaling coefficients, trained jointly under three ablation methods, with a pre-activation objective that keeps a gradient where Top-K censors the target.
\item \textbf{Weighted circuits at scale} (\cref{sec:main-result}): $15{,}046$ sampled TuringLLM targets, with circuits of $10^{2}$--$10^{3}$ upstream latents tested for faithfulness, necessity, and sufficiency to induce on held-out contexts, $46\%$ of which pass one pre-stated criterion; \pending{Pythia-70m and GPT-2 small, DAN-40/41}.
\item \textbf{An evaluation protocol for latent targets} (\cref{sec:eval,app:protocol}): every score names its ablation method and reads the target one way, random-circuit baselines receive the same coefficient fitting, and each method is shown to win the scores it was trained on.
\item \textbf{Measurements of the circuits themselves} (\cref{sec:weighted,sec:secondary,sec:underdetermination}): the scaling coefficients index the faithfulness cost of natural scale; node sets are identifiable only up to a large equivalence class; and output-targeted and latent-targeted circuits fail each other's objectives.
\end{itemize}
We release the pipeline, discovery methods, and analysis code at \href{https://github.com/DanielJamesDavies/turing-explorer-circuit-discovery}{\nolinkurl{github.com/DanielJamesDavies/turing-explorer-circuit-discovery}}.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Circuit discovery.}
Manual analysis of model components~\citep{olah2020zoom, wang2023interpretability} gave way to automated search~\citep{conmy2023towards} and to attribution patching and its integrated-gradient refinements~\citep{nanda2023attribution, syed2023attribution, kramar2024atp, hanna2024faith}.
Sparse feature circuits~\citep{marks2025sparse} moved the nodes to SAE features with error nodes for the reconstruction residual, and attribution graphs~\citep{ameisen2025circuit, lindsey2025biology} build per-prompt graphs over transcoder features at production scale.
Their objectives are anchored at an output, and circuits for one task can differ across its prompts~\citep{makou2026many}.

\paragraph{Circuits for internal features.}
Internal targets are not new.
Hierarchical attribution extracts the subgraph behind a chosen SAE feature~\citep{ge2024automatically}, dictionary features have been traced patch-free in Othello-GPT~\citep{he2024dictionary}, transcoder features are reverse-engineered from their input circuits~\citep{dunefsky2024transcoders}, latent attribution debugs a chosen latent~\citep{duprelatour2025debugging}, CircuitLens explains a feature's activation from upstream interactions~\citep{golimblevskaia2025circuit}, and attribution graphs have been extended to concept directions read by linear probes~\citep{palit2026probes}.
These methods attribute and threshold; circuit probing is closer in spirit, optimising a mask over model weights for a labelled intermediate variable~\citep{lepori2023uncovering}.

\paragraph{Masks and scalars.}
Edge Pruning casts discovery as learning a continuous mask~\citep{bhaskar2024finding}, on $L_0$-gate and continuous-sparsification machinery~\citep{louizos2018learning, savarese2020winning}, and binary masks over SAE latents have been learned by continuous sparsification for behavioural objectives~\citep{caples2025scaling, kharlapenko2025scaling}; with its coefficients fixed at 1 and its objective moved to a latent, this is our unweighted circuit masking.
Learned scalars appear with other jobs: constants for \emph{removed} components~\citep{li2024optimal}, steering multipliers~\citep{stoehr2024activation}, regression weights on feature-to-feature edges~\citep{yin2026scalable}, and hand-set rescaling of circuit activations~\citep{gao2025weightsparse}.

\paragraph{Evaluating circuits.}
What removing a latent means is an experimental choice that can decide the result~\citep{li2024optimal, miller2024transformer, mueller2024missed}; patching in clean values (denoising) and removing them (noising) answer different questions~\citep{heimersheim2024patching}, and our sufficiency to induce is a denoising-style test.
Random circuits as a null for faithfulness follow the hypothesis tests of \citet{shi2024hypothesis}, and MIB standardises circuit evaluation for output targets~\citep{mueller2025mib}.

What this paper adds is the conjunction: circuits of SAE latents \emph{optimised} for a single target latent with \emph{fitted} scaling coefficients, reusable across the target's contexts, tested for faithfulness, necessity, and sufficiency to induce on held-out contexts against random circuits given the same coefficient fitting, for ${\sim}15$k sampled latents per model.
Position-aware circuits, contrast contexts, and structure among SAE latents are discussed in \cref{app:related}.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

For a latent $f$ and a sequence--position pair $(x, t)$, write $a_f(x, t) \geq 0$ for its activation (after Top-K) and $\preact(x, t)$ for its pre-activation (before it); pseudocode is in \cref{app:pseudocode}.

\subsection{Problem}
\label{sec:problem}
\label{sec:evidence}

\paragraph{Targets and contexts.}
Fix a target latent $f$ and let $U_f$ be the latents at every SAE site that causally precedes $f$'s site.
Each target has \emph{activating contexts} from two stores, its 64 strongest-activating sequences and 64 drawn from the middle of its activation distribution, each with an anchor at the target's peak (\cref{app:stores}).
It trains on 32 strongest and 16 mid-band contexts and is scored on 16 held-out strongest contexts (primary) and 16 held-out mid-band contexts (reported); the split is stratified by activation and always trains on the two strongest (\cref{app:protocol}).
It also has 64 \emph{contrast contexts}: corpus sequences ranked by their highest cosine similarity to any of its activating contexts in pooled final-layer representation space, excluding those contexts, and kept only if the target stays out of its site's Top-K on them, so that they are close to the target's contexts but verified silent (\cref{app:stores}).
For the running example they are passages semantically adjacent to thermodynamics \pending{running-example contrast contexts under the v1 selection}.
% PROTOCOL v1 (2026-09-24, DAN-64 / DAN-7): NegContextSelector("close"), silence =
% post-Top-K activation 0. The earlier pre-built kNN store gave 38% of targets one
% shared fallback list (seq_repr capped at 200k of 24.8M sequences); WCM no longer uses it.
Targets are sampled at random, stratified across sites, with no prioritisation heuristic.

\paragraph{Circuit-only execution.}
A \emph{weighted circuit} is a node set $C \subseteq U_f$ with a scaling coefficient $\alpha_{s,i} > 0$ for each node.
Under an ablation method $\pi$, the forward pass is modified at every upstream site $s$: the site's Top-K code $c_s$, computed from the (already modified) stream, is replaced by
\[
    \hat{c}_{s,i} =
    \begin{cases}
        \alpha_{s,i}\, c_{s,i} & (s, i) \in C \\
        v^{\pi}_{s,i} & \text{otherwise,}
    \end{cases}
\]
and the stream continues as $x + W_{\mathrm{dec}}^{s}(\hat{c}_s - c_s)$, so SAE errors pass through unchanged and every downstream site, including the target's, reads the modified stream.
Nodes are \emph{recomputed}, not clamped: a node's value at each position is whatever the modified stream gives it.
The ablation value $v^{\pi}$ is zero (Z), the latent's mean over activating contexts (A), or its mean over contrast contexts (C), optionally sparsity-preserving, with the per-position Top-K re-imposed.
An unweighted circuit fixes $\alpha = 1$.
Writing $a_C$ for the target's mean activation at the anchors under this intervention, $a_{\varnothing}$ for the empty circuit, and $a_{\mathrm{pos}}$ for the unmodified model, \emph{faithfulness} is
\[
    \phi^{\mathrm{free}}_{\pi}(C) = \frac{a_C - a_{\varnothing}}{a_{\mathrm{pos}} - a_{\varnothing}},
\]
and \emph{clamped faithfulness} $\phi^{\mathrm{pin}}_{\pi}(C)$ is the same ratio with nodes clamped to their clean-run values.

\paragraph{The problem.}
Faithfulness depends on the ablation method~\citep{miller2024transformer, mueller2024missed}, and a node set trained under one method can be exactly infeasible under another (\cref{sec:ablmask}), so one weighted circuit is asked to serve all three:
\begin{equation}\tag{P}\label{eq:p2}
    n_{\varepsilon}(f)
    \;=\;
    \min \bigl\{\, |C| \;:\; \exists\, \alpha \ \text{ with } \ \phi^{\mathrm{free}}_{\pi}(C, \alpha) \geq 1 - \varepsilon \ \text{ for every } \pi \in \{\mathrm{Z}, \mathrm{C}, \mathrm{A}\} \,\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
WCM solves a differentiable relaxation of \labelcref{eq:p2}.
With $\alpha = 1$ the smallest faithful node sets are several times larger, and attribution at natural scale needs one to two orders of magnitude more (\cref{sec:weighted}); \labelcref{eq:p2} asks for \emph{a} minimiser: its near-optimal solutions form a large, non-nested equivalence class (\cref{sec:underdetermination}), so only class-invariant quantities are treated as properties of the target.

\subsection{Attribution Baselines}
\label{sec:attr-baselines}
% RESTRUCTURE 2026-09-22 (DAN-24/25/26): the attribution objectives, the four
% linearisation modes, and the position-aware node-set construction moved to
% app:attribution; this summary is all the main body keeps.
WCM is compared against rank-and-cut attribution.
Every upstream latent is scored by gradient-times-activation: a \emph{contrastive} objective on contrast contexts, taken through the target's pre-activation because Top-K censors its activation to zero there; an \emph{ablation} objective on activating contexts, where the activation is live; and their fusion.
Each runs under four linearisation modes; \emph{path attribution} is the integrated-gradients estimator of sparse feature circuits~\citep{marks2025sparse}.
Scores are cut per position and the node set is the union over the target's causal prefix, since a Top-K code is sparse per position while the target reads every position; the 128-target grid of \cref{app:grid} predates this construction and uses per-site top-$K$ caps instead (\cref{app:pseudocode}).
Objectives, modes, and the node-set construction are in \cref{app:attribution}.

\subsection{Weighted Circuit Masking}
\label{sec:ablmask}
% BUDGET 1.5 pp. lambda and the gamma weights are re-examined in the DAN-64 grid.

Rather than thresholding attribution scores, WCM learns the circuit directly, adopting the optimisation framing and gate machinery of learned-mask circuit discovery~\citep{bhaskar2024finding, kharlapenko2025scaling} and moving its objective from an output to a single latent.
For every upstream site $s$ it attaches gate parameters $\theta_s \in \R^{d_{\mathrm{sae}}}$ with gates $m_s = \sigma(\theta_s)$, and coefficient parameters $\psi_s$ with $\alpha_s = \mathrm{softplus}(\psi_s)$.
A latent is kept at $\alpha$ times its live value when its gate is open and sent to its ablation value when closed:
\[
    x \;\leftarrow\; x + W_{\mathrm{dec}}^{s}\!\left[\, m_s \odot \alpha_s \odot c^{s} + (1 - m_s) \odot c^{s}_{\mathrm{abl}} - c^{s} \,\right],
\]
where $c^{s}$ is the site's dense Top-K code and $c^{s}_{\mathrm{abl}}$ its ablation value; editing only the decoded difference leaves the SAE error untouched, as in evaluation.

\paragraph{Objective.}
The circuit is trained to \emph{reproduce} the target's pre-activation at the anchors of the training contexts, by mean squared error against its value on the unmodified stream.
The pre-activation is forced by Top-K: the activation is censored at zero, so a loss on it carries no gradient exactly where the mask must learn.
Reproducing the pre-activation does not, however, keep the target in its site's Top-K: the loss constrains the site's stream along one direction of $d_{\mathrm{model}}$, and a circuit can restore that projection while other latents at the site rise past the target.
A \emph{rank-keep} term therefore asks the target to hold its natural rank: with $r$ the target's rank among its site's other latents at an anchor in the unmodified model, and $\tau^{(r)}_{\pi}$ the $r$-th largest of their pre-activations under the circuit,
\[
    \mathcal{R}_{\pi} = \operatorname{mean}\Bigl[\operatorname{relu}\bigl(\tau^{(r)}_{\pi} - \preact\bigr)^{2}\Bigr],
\]
which is zero whenever the target is at or above its natural rank, so it never pulls a circuit towards a state the unmodified model does not have.

\paragraph{Training under three ablation methods.}
A mask trained under one ablation method learns whatever that method rewards.
Trained only under mean ablation over contrast contexts, it reaches high faithfulness under that ablation while its zero-ablation faithfulness is $0.0$ at depth: it learns the difference from the contrast-context mean, never a set that suffices alone.
Trained only under zero ablation, it suffices alone but is never asked what separates firing from a near-identical context that does not fire.
WCM therefore scores one mask under all three ablation methods at once, with gradients accumulating into the same parameters:
\[
    \mathcal{L}
    = \frac{\sum_{\pi} \gamma_{\pi} \bigl(\mathcal{L}_{\pi} + \gamma_{\mathrm{R}}\, \mathcal{R}_{\pi}\bigr)}{\nu}
    \;+\; \lambda \sum_{s}\sum_{i} \sigma(\theta_{s,i}),
    \qquad
    \nu = \operatorname{mean}\bigl(\text{target}^2\bigr),
\]
summed over $\pi \in \{\mathrm{Z}, \mathrm{C}, \mathrm{A}\}$, with $\gamma_{\mathrm{Z}} = 1$, $\gamma_{\mathrm{C}} = \gamma_{\mathrm{A}} = 0.25$ at every layer, and $\gamma_{\mathrm{R}} = 3 \times 10^{-3}$; the ablation means are computed on the training contexts only.
% PROTOCOL v1 (2026-09-24, DAN-7): rank-keep gamma_R = 3e-3. Local sweep on 16 targets
% (experiments/055, 056): 3e-3 to 1e-1 all give 7-8/16 passing faithfulness under
% Z, A and C (plain WCM 6/16) at ~330-350 nodes (plain 398). It does NOT reliably
% remove amplifier circuits; specificity is reported, not claimed.
All terms measure the same quantity on the same target, so one shared, bounded normaliser suffices, and the penalty is a flat per-latent price, which outperformed every gradient-informed site weighting we tried (\cref{app:membership-studies}).
% PROTOCOL v1 (DAN-7): gamma_A = 0.25 at every layer. The EXISTING 15k TuringLLM
% circuits are NOT v1: they used the store contrast contexts, no rank-keep, and the
% held-out leak; v1 numbers come from the new production run. The 029 panel and
% the ports used a 0.10/0.05 depth rule and are refitted under v1 (DAN-16) or
% marked pre-v1. Calibration claims below: experiments/026-floor-isolation R3/R4
% (w = 0.25); Jaccard 0.13 vs 0.54: 026 R2, single-ablation-method arms at matched size.
In calibration the A term roughly halves the smallest passing node set on layer-2 targets and raises the share of layer-9 targets with any passing circuit.
The ablation method matters more than one might expect: on four layer-2 targets, node sets trained under different ablation methods at matched size share fewer latents (Jaccard $0.13$) than node sets trained for different \emph{objectives} under the same method ($0.54$).

\paragraph{Scaling coefficients.}
Coefficients start at $\alpha = 1$, so training starts exactly at unweighted behaviour; \emph{unweighted circuit masking} keeps them there.
One guard is required: with coefficients free, the optimiser can hold a gate just below the keep threshold while inflating its coefficient, emptying the node set while keeping function intact, so latents with closed gates are charged $\lambda\,(1 - m)\,|\alpha - 1|$.
The zero-ablation term anchors the coefficients: trained without it, they calibrate against the mean ablation's background and overshoot by one to three orders of magnitude once that background is removed (\cref{app:weighted-details}).

\paragraph{Binarisation.}
Evaluation scores the hard node set $\{i : m_i > \tfrac{1}{2}\}$, so the gates are annealed during training, $m = \sigma(\theta / T)$ with $T$ decaying geometrically from $1$ to $0.05$ (continuous sparsification;~\citealp{savarese2020winning}), and the training forward converges to the binary gates that evaluation scores.
Training runs 400 AdamW steps; all settings are in \cref{app:protocol}, and the binarisation and size-calibration studies in \cref{app:membership-studies}.

% 1 - a/a_pos, which reconciles the engine and the standalone ports (DAN-65).
Let $A^{+}$ and $A^{-}$ be the target's mean activation on activating and contrast contexts.
\textbf{Sufficiency to induce}, $\phiind = (A^{-}_{\mathrm{int}} - A^{-})/(A^{+} - A^{-})$, sets the circuit's nodes to their activating-context means in contrast contexts: near 1, the circuit switches the target on.
\textbf{Necessity} is one minus the completeness score of \citet{marks2025sparse}: with $a_{M \setminus C}$ the target's activation on activating contexts when the circuit's nodes are ablated per $\pi$ and every other latent is recomputed,
\[
    \phisup(C; \pi) = \frac{a_{\mathrm{pos}} - a_{M \setminus C}}{a_{\mathrm{pos}} - a_{\varnothing}}.
\]
% 2026-09-22 (DAN-72): no per-node role labels in WCM. Leave-one-out effects are
% strongly non-additive and no signed subset passes a set-level test, so
% necessity and sufficiency to induce treat the circuit as one set.
Both scores intervene on the circuit as one set.
All scores are unclipped ratios: success means landing near $1$, and a score far above $1$ is overshoot, not quality.

\paragraph{Ablation methods.}
The choice of ablation is load-bearing~\citep{li2024optimal, miller2024transformer}.
Zero ablation needs no reference distribution.
Mean ablation over activating contexts leaks target signal into the ablation value increasingly with depth ($a_{\varnothing}$ up to $30\%$ of $a_{\mathrm{pos}}$); mean ablation over contrast contexts does not.
Dense mean ablation puts the model on streams a Top-K SAE can never emit, so the sparsity-preserving variant re-imposes the per-position Top-K after the means are applied (\cref{app:respect-topk}).

\paragraph{Random-circuit baseline.}
Fitted coefficients could make any set look faithful, so each random circuit, of the same size and drawn from live latents at the same sites, receives the \emph{same coefficient-fitting budget} on its own nodes before scoring; faithfulness then reflects selection rather than the fitting capacity of the coefficients.

\paragraph{Specificity.}
Every score above reads the target alone, so a circuit that restores the target by lifting its whole neighbourhood at the site passes them all.
We therefore read the full code at the target's site under the circuit and report, as a measured property rather than a pass criterion: the target's rank and Top-K membership, the number of latents the circuit lifts above the unmodified model's Top-K cut relative to the empty circuit, and the faithfulness of \emph{sibling} latents that co-fire with the target, against the target's own (\cref{app:specificity}).

\paragraph{Near-threshold targets.}
A target that only just enters its site's Top-K in the unmodified model has an ill-conditioned activation read: a circuit can reproduce its pre-activation while small shifts near the cut push it out, so its activation-read scores are near zero whatever the circuit.
Such targets, those whose median clean rank at the anchor is at least $K/2$, are identified from the unmodified model alone, before any circuit is fitted; they stay in the headline pass rate and are also reported as a separate stratum on both reads.

\paragraph{Pass criterion.}
% DAN-8, 2026-09-27. experiments/062-h100-protocol-v1/pass_rule.py applies it.
A circuit passes if, on the held-out strongest contexts and the activation read, its faithfulness under all three ablation methods (Z, sparsity-preserving A, and C) lies in $[0.8, 1.5]$ and its necessity is at least $0.9$.
The band is asymmetric by design: below $0.8$ the circuit fails to drive its target, while an overshoot up to $1.5$ still drives it, and whether that overshoot is specific to the target is measured for every circuit (above).
Each circuit's largest deviation $\max_{\pi} |\phi^{\mathrm{free}}_{\pi} - 1|$ is reported alongside the binary rule; sufficiency to induce, specificity, and the random-circuit baseline are reported rather than required; targets whose ratio denominator is vacuous are excluded and counted.

\subsection{Why the Coefficients Matter}
\label{sec:weighted}
% BUDGET 1.0 pp. Panel: experiments/029-panel (pre-v1, refit under DAN-16).

Three versions of each circuit separate what the nodes contribute from what the coefficients contribute: the weighted circuit as fitted, the same nodes at natural scale ($\alpha = 1$), and a random circuit of the same size given the same coefficient fitting (\cref{sec:eval}).

Three results structure the comparison.
First, at natural size every attribution arm reconstructs the target with its full per-position union, $10^{5}$--$10^{6}$ nodes (the smallest faithful prefix of the same ranking is the $10^{4}$--$10^{5}$ of \cref{sec:weighted}); at that scale the arms' node sets largely coincide, so method identity matters only under compactness pressure.

\paragraph{Discovered dependencies are not co-activation.}
Across 7{,}525 accepted attribution circuits, a circuit's nodes overlap its target's top co-activation partners by ${\sim}3.6\%$, and correlational proposals fail the intervention gates outright; even granted fitted coefficients, a co-activation selection passes single scores but never the joint suite (\cref{app:coact}).

\paragraph{Output-targeted circuits do not explain latents, and vice versa.}
\label{sec:sfc-boundary}
A replication of sparse feature circuits~\citep{marks2025sparse} on the same model, SAE bank, and targets, with the same attribution estimator, scores $\phiind = \phisup = 0.000$ on every target latent even at its most permissive threshold, and our circuits score near zero on logit faithfulness (\cref{app:logit-faith}): ``which latents support this output?'' and ``what makes this latent fire?'' are answered by circuits that fail each other's objective.
The output-targeted arm ran at its ceiling threshold without a held-out split and before protocol v1, so this is a scope result, not a ranking; \pending{re-run under protocol v1, DAN-48}.

\paragraph{A feature relayed through the residual stream.}
\label{sec:relay}
Wiring the weighted circuit of a layer-11 MLP latent that fires on ``p\emph{iv}otal'' with direct-effect edges (\cref{app:wcm-edges}) shows the residual stream carrying one feature forward: every upstream residual dictionary's best detector of the subword ``iv'' is a node, and edges shared by all three ablation methods link each layer's copy to the next.
The copies are one feature: consecutive decoder directions are nearest or second-nearest neighbours among 40{,}960 latents (cosine $0.38$--$0.68$) and co-fire on random text ($r = 0.87$--$0.95$).

\subsection{Node Sets Are Non-Identifiable; Function Is Not}
\label{sec:underdetermination}
% BUDGET 0.5 pp. Data: unweighted-mask membership studies (Jul 2026; paper-updates
% B6l/B6p), 055 leak-fix A/B, experiments/032-running-example.

The masking engine is bit-deterministic at fixed settings (two identical runs agree to six decimal places with \emph{zero} node flips), so tiny perturbations are controlled experiments on the objective, and every one we tried gives the same verdict (\cref{app:discussion}).
Float precision, the sparsity price, the stopping step, the training budget, and the contexts behind the mean-ablation values all leave size and faithfulness unchanged but exchange nodes: two node sets for the same target share only $40$--$85\%$ of their latents.
A discovered node set is one sample from an equivalence class of near-optimal solutions to \labelcref{eq:p2}, so we report node sets as equivalence classes and let set-level and functional claims carry the conclusions.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
% BUDGET 1.25 pp. REWRITE 2026-09-22 (DAN-24, DAN-29): discussion cut to two
% paragraphs; three more in app:discussion. Limitations pruned of stale items
% ("no held-out split", panel sizes) and merged.

\paragraph{Circuit size is a measurement at a stated scaling convention.}
At natural scale a latent's circuit is large for deep targets (\cref{sec:weighted}), and every one of those latents is individually removable at the noise level while bulk removal craters the target (\cref{sec:drivers-closure}).
A latent's activation, reached through an additive residual stream, is a dense function of many small contributions, so any rule that thresholds individual contributions undercounts it however the threshold is tuned.
Fitted coefficients show that most of that collective tail is redundancy that fewer, rescaled latents can carry; a circuit's size is therefore meaningful only alongside whether its coefficients were fitted, and weighted and unweighted sizes are compared only at matched faithfulness (\cref{fig:wcm-vs-unweighted}).

\paragraph{Ablation methods are part of the method.}
\citet{miller2024transformer} showed that faithfulness is not robust to the choice of ablation; our measurements push the point into training.
The home-turf effect (\cref{sec:method-comparison}), node sets trained under different ablation methods sharing fewer latents than node sets trained for different objectives, and the miscalibration of coefficients trained without zero ablation (\cref{sec:ablmask}) all say the same thing: the ablation methods used for discovery, training, and evaluation belong in the method statement.
Further discussion (equivalence classes, position handling, composing circuits into chains) is in \cref{app:discussion}.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{The SAE basis is not ground truth.}
Many latents resist clean characterisation, and circuits built from ambiguous latents inherit that ambiguity; our scores measure intervention effects, not interpretability.

\textbf{The objective is a surrogate.}
A high pre-activation does not guarantee a Top-K slot, so WCM trains on a continuous surrogate of a discrete event; the rank-keep term narrows the gap on training contexts but does not guarantee the target's rank on held-out ones, and evaluation closes the loop by scoring the activation itself.
For targets that barely enter the Top-K in the unmodified model the gap cannot be closed, which is why they are reported separately.

\textbf{Faithfulness does not imply specificity.}
A circuit can restore its target by lifting the target's whole neighbourhood; the rank-keep term limits the lifts that overtake the target, but does not reliably remove such circuits, so specificity is measured and reported rather than claimed.

\textbf{Wiring is descriptive and depends on the ablation method.}
A weighted circuit is fitted as a set of upstream latents sufficient for one target; direct-effect edges wire it (\cref{app:wcm-edges}), but the wiring is dense, much of it cancels, and edge sets agree only partly across ablation methods, so only edges shared by all three are interpreted, and composing circuits into chains still needs its own validation.

\textbf{Contrast contexts.}
Contrast contexts are verified silent only in the sense that the target stays out of its Top-K; a near-miss pre-activation can remain, and closeness is measured in one pooled representation space, so ``close'' is one of several defensible constructions.


\textbf{Generality.}
TuringLLM's synthetic corpus is an asset for control but a limit on generality, which the two public models partly address; larger models and other dictionary architectures remain open.
The method is scoped to Top-K dictionaries by construction: on dense ReLU dictionaries, a random-circuit baseline with fitted coefficients loses its discriminating power once a set is large enough to span the anchors, so that architecture needs its own baseline design.

\section{Conclusion}
% BUDGET 0.25 pp.

We reframed circuit discovery around a target latent, asking not which latents cause an output but what makes a latent fire.
Weighted circuit masking answers with a weighted circuit of $10^{2}$--$10^{3}$ upstream latents per target, tested on held-out contexts against random circuits given the same fitting; $46\%$ of $15{,}046$ sampled TuringLLM latents pass one pre-stated criterion \pending{Pythia-70m and GPT-2 small, DAN-40/41}; the coefficients locate a redundancy that costs ${\sim}10^{4}$ latents at natural scale for deep targets, about $14\times$ the weighted circuit.
Circuits for single latents are a building block: composing them into chains, with propagation tested at every link, is the path from dependencies to a map of the model's internal computation.

\subsection{A Corpus Built by Design}

The synthetic corpus was constructed in two stages.
The first produced a relatively small but high-quality foundation: Phi-3 Mini~\citep{abdin2024phi3}, a 3.8B-parameter language model available under a permissive licence, generated instructional and educational text spanning 18 broad subject areas (Physics, Mathematics, Computer Science, Chemistry, Biology, Philosophy, English, Creative Writing, Languages, Art, Music, Engineering, Economics, Business, Geography, History, Culinary Arts, and Communication), recursively generating topics and sub-topics and producing structured documents of headings and text.

The second stage addressed scale.
A fine-tuned T5-based paraphrase model~\citep{vorobev2023paraphraser, raffel2020exploring} expanded the corpus by generating 11 paraphrased variants of each source document, motivated by knowledge distillation results~\citep{ba2014deep, hinton2015distilling} suggesting that varied outputs of a stronger teacher can be advantageous over raw data alone.
The final corpus totals approximately 2 billion tokens.

Two tokenized datasets were derived using the Phi-3 Mini tokenizer.
The language-modelling training set combines original and augmented texts shuffled into epochs, concluding with a second pass over only the high-quality originals to consolidate representations around the cleaner signal.
The interpretability dataset consists of 64-token chunks shuffled into 4,096 shards, allowing the pipeline to process one shard at a time with a bounded memory footprint.

\subsection{Sparse Autoencoder Quality}
\label{app:sae-quality}

\Cref{fig:sae-quality} reports three standard quality metrics for the 36-SAE bank, per site.
\emph{Explained variance}, $1 - \operatorname{Var}(x - \hat{x}) / \operatorname{Var}(x)$, measures reconstruction fidelity.
\emph{Alive latents} is the fraction of each dictionary's 40,960 latents that fired at least once during the full collection pass over the interpretability corpus.
\emph{Loss recovered} splices each SAE's reconstruction into the model at its site and reports the recovered share of the cross-entropy gap between the original model and zero-ablating that site~\citep{marks2025sparse}, measured over 12{,}288 tokens of the interpretability corpus.
Because the SAEs are Top-K with $K = 128$, sparsity is fixed by construction and the usual $L_0$--fidelity trade-off curve is a single point per SAE.

Three observations matter for interpreting the paper's results.
First, reconstruction fidelity is healthy across the bank: attention SAEs explain 0.93--0.99 of variance, residual SAEs decline smoothly with depth to 0.85, and MLP SAEs bottom out at 0.77 in layer 9.
Second, the layer-1 attention SAE has a dead-latent crater: only 37\% of its latents ever fire (84\% at layer 2, at least 93\% everywhere else).
Third, loss recovered exposes a functional gap that explained variance misses: the layer-11 attention SAE recovers only 55\% of the cross-entropy gap despite explaining 0.96 of variance (layer 10: 84\%), indicating that the unexplained residual at the final attention site is disproportionately important to the logits.

\section{Attribution Baselines and Position-Aware Node Sets}
\label{app:attribution}
% Moved from the main body (Method 4.3-4.5) on 2026-09-22 (DAN-24/25/26).

\subsection{Attribution Objectives}
\label{sec:cfgrad}
\label{sec:abgrad}
\label{sec:hybrid}

Two gradient objectives supply the signed scores, one for each side of the target's behaviour.

\paragraph{Contrastive gradient: why does the target not fire?}
When a latent is absent from the Top-K code its activation is identically zero and a gradient through it carries no information, so attribution targets the target latent's \emph{encoder pre-activation}
\[
    \preact(x, t)
    = \bigl\langle W_e^{(f)},\, z(x, t) \bigr\rangle + b_e^{(f)},
\]
with $W_e^{(f)}$ the target's encoder row, $b_e^{(f)}$ its effective bias, and $z(x, t)$ the pre-SAE dense activation.
The pre-activation is defined on every forward pass whether or not $f$ clears Top-K, and is differentiable with respect to all upstream latents through identity passthroughs at each SAE site.
On a contrast context $x$, the objective is the squared gap $\Lcf(x) = \bigl(\preact(x, t^{*}) - \mu_f^{+}\bigr)^{2}$ to the target's mean pre-activation over activating contexts, taken at the position of maximum pre-activation, and each upstream latent $g$ is scored by gradient-times-activation averaged over contrast contexts:
$\Scf = \frac{1}{|\negset|} \sum_{x} a_g(x, t_g) \, \partial \Lcf(x) / \partial a_g(x, t_g)$.
The baseline keeps the top-scoring latents of each sign of $\Scf$ (\cref{alg:cfgrad}).
The source of contrast contexts is a controlled variable: \texttt{close} (the retrieved store), \texttt{random}, or \texttt{distant}.

\paragraph{Ablation gradient: what sustains the firing?}
The mirror score on activating contexts,
$\Sab = \frac{1}{|\posset|} \sum_{(x,t)} a_g(x, t_g) \, \partial a_f(x, t) / \partial a_g(x, t_g)$,
is large when the upstream latent is strongly active and the target is sensitive to it; its top latents form the node set.

\paragraph{Hybrid fusion.}
A hybrid arm runs both objectives on the same target and fuses the circuits by latent identity, each node keeping its per-source scores; fusion details, pruning, and acceptance modes are in \cref{app:pseudocode} and \cref{alg:hybrid}.

\paragraph{Co-activation graph.}
A second pass over stored top contexts computes, for every latent, its top 64 co-activating partners scored by clamped pointwise mutual information.
In this paper it serves as the correlational reference against which the causal discoveries are compared (\cref{app:coact}).

\subsection{Attribution Modes}
\label{sec:modes}

Both objectives are estimator-agnostic: evaluating the gradient at the clean activations is one choice of \emph{linearisation point}, not the only one.
Four modes differ only in where gradients are linearised, holding objective, probes, and selection fixed:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\texttt{local}}: a single linearisation at the natural activations (gradient-times-activation at the clean forward pass), descending from attribution patching~\citep{nanda2023attribution, syed2023attribution}.
\item \textbf{\texttt{ig\_mean}}: integrated gradients along the straight path from the site's mean-ablation value to the natural activations; this is the $\mathrm{IE}_{\mathrm{ig}}$ node estimator of \citet{marks2025sparse}, which we adopt unchanged and credit as theirs.
\item \textbf{\texttt{restoration}}: iterated greedy re-linearisation, in which each round attributes at the \emph{current} partially restored stream, admits the top scorers, restores them by live re-encoding, and re-linearises, for as many rounds as the target has upstream sites (\cref{alg:restoration}).
\item \textbf{\texttt{ig\_restoration}}: the restoration loop with per-round integrated-gradient scoring.
\end{itemize}
The taxonomy is single-point, path, and iterated-trajectory linearisation.
Adopting the published path estimator is deliberate experimental design: comparisons between output- and latent-targeted discovery (\cref{sec:sfc-boundary}) then isolate the target and the node-set rule rather than estimator quality, exactly the claim under test.

\subsection{Node Sets and Position-Aware Discovery}
\label{sec:membership}

Attribution returns ranked latents, so the baselines must also decide which node set a ranking defines.
The Top-K constraint makes the classical answer fail structurally: the code is $k$-sparse \emph{per position} while a target reads its whole causal prefix, so a position-collapsed circuit must cover the union of every position's active set, thousands of distinct latents per site, or recomputed faithfulness collapses.
The failure is a property of position aggregation, not of node selection (mechanism measured in \cref{app:union-coverage}).

The baselines' node set is therefore a set of latents, the same kind of object \labelcref{eq:p2} optimises over, collected across positions.
Latents outside the circuit are ablated everywhere; nodes are not forced to any value: they are recomputed from the modified stream at every position, so positions instantiate naturally (allow, don't force), exactly the circuit-only execution of \cref{sec:problem}.
Discovery is position-aware to match: attribution is computed per position, a per-target percentile cut on $|$attribution$|$ is applied per position, and the node set is the union over the positions of the target's causal prefix.
No template or cross-example alignment is required, and in our sweeps the percentile rule dominates top-$N$ and per-position--normalised alternatives.
Both attribution signs are kept, as a design principle rather than an implementation detail: the node set is \emph{stream reconstruction}, not a selection of what drives the target, and a negative-attribution latent still carries part of the residual stream that nodes are recomputed from, so removing it corrupts recomputed execution.

Two optional, validated levers shrink the set: a \emph{recurrence prune} (nodes must fire in at least two contexts) and a \emph{magnitude-bisection prune} (a binary search over the attribution ranking for the smallest prefix holding a requested faithfulness, automating the threshold-tuning step of prior practice~\citealp{marks2025sparse}); details and their depth-dependence are in \cref{app:mode-studies}.

A node set's size at a stated faithfulness is a measured quantity, and \cref{sec:drivers-closure} argues it measures the structure of the computation rather than a defect of the method.

\section{Control versus Faithfulness at Natural Scale}
\label{sec:drivers-closure}
% Moved from the main body (Results 5.1) on 2026-09-22 (DAN-24). Numbers are
% 14/23-Jul vintage from 3-4-target depth-spanning panels. The main body keeps
% one paragraph of this in sec:weighted.

Which latents can impose a target's activation?
A few hundred.
How many must remain for the circuit alone to reproduce it?
Tens of thousands.
The two questions are two criteria over the same node set.
\emph{Interventional control} asks for a small set whose intervention scores clear an acceptance gate, sufficiency to induce $\phiind \geq \tau_{\mathrm{ind}}$ and necessity $\phisup \geq \tau_{\mathrm{sup}}$, as the gradient attribution methods of \cref{app:pseudocode} used.
\emph{Faithfulness} is \labelcref{eq:p2} with every coefficient fixed at $\alpha = 1$: the smallest node set that reproduces the target alone, whose size $n_{\varepsilon}$ we call the target's \emph{faithfulness cost}.
The clamped/recomputed decomposition separates them, and on depth-spanning target panels their answers come apart; this appendix measures how far, for node sets from the attribution baselines of \cref{app:attribution}.
All numbers here come from panels of three to four targets per layer under the evaluation that preceded protocol v1, and are indicative.

\paragraph{Control is concentrated.}
Restricting a node set to its most influential positions barely moves clamped faithfulness: it reaches 0.79--1.04 with only 10\% of positions kept, roughly flat as more are added.
What \emph{causally controls} a target lives in a handful of positions and a compact set of latents: a circuit in the classic, hand-annotatable sense.
The running example's controlling circuit reads like a chemistry syllabus: latents for Arrhenius kinetics, the third law, and absolute zero.

\paragraph{Faithfulness is collective, position-local, and depth-scaled.}
Recomputed faithfulness tells the opposite story.
At the same 10\% of positions a shallow target still passes (0.85), but deep targets sit at exactly 0.00 and climb only position by position as the rest of the prefix is restored (a layer-7 residual-stream target: $0 \rightarrow 0.24 \rightarrow 0.52 \rightarrow 0.82$).
This is the sense in which the cost is \emph{position-local}: no small set of positions carries it, and the score accrues position by position across the causal prefix.
The faithfulness-versus-size curve (\cref{fig:drivers-closure}) is two-regime on every target we measured: most compression is available down to $\phi^{\mathrm{free}} \approx 0.7$, then a hard limit, an irreducible core that even $\phi^{\mathrm{free}} = 0.2$ requires.
That core is the faithfulness cost $n_{\varepsilon}$ at loose tolerance, and it scales with depth: roughly $2$k at layer 3, $10$k at layer 7, $60$k at layer 11.
Compressibility follows the same gradient: shallow node sets prune to 13--23\% of their size, deep ones only to ${\sim}70\%$ with a sharp cliff.
Depth, not the selection rule, is the fundamental determinant of circuit size.
These are costs \emph{at natural scale} ($\alpha = 1$): \cref{sec:weighted} shows that fitted per-node scaling coefficients collapse them by a factor of ten to a hundred.

\paragraph{Individually negligible, collectively load-bearing.}
Leave-one-out is blind to all of this: removing any single node changes the target by $0.000$, the noise level.
Yet cutting nodes in bulk by their own attribution ranking craters function: on recurrence- and bisection-validated node sets, dropping the bottom half by score moves $\phi^{\mathrm{free}}_{\mathrm{Z}}$ from $0.975$ to $0.895$ at layer 2 but from $1.119$ to $0.595$ at layer 8; dropping the bottom 90\% yields $0.635$, $0.000$, $0.222$, and $0.005$ at layers 2, 8, 9, and 10.
The scores being cut are minuscule, $2$--$5 \times 10^{-4}$ in attribution units.
Nodes individually at the noise level collectively carry the entire deep reconstruction.
Individual-contribution thresholding, the standard selection rule across circuit discovery, therefore systematically undercounts what faithfulness needs; this is the finding that makes circuit size a measurement rather than a defect.

\paragraph{The union survives its controls.}
Node sets are target-specific, not noise or soup (cross-target node-set tails overlap at chance, Jaccard ${\sim}0.07$, against $0.91$ for a target's own split halves); the plain magnitude union Pareto-dominates every structured alternative we tried; and at depth, faithfulness is partly carried by the preserved SAE errors, the latent-targeted echo of the error-node finding for output targets~\citep{marks2025sparse}.
The full controls are in \cref{app:mode-studies}.

That sparsity is paid for in the calibration of sufficiency to induce: on the panel, deep targets are induced with overshoot at the full budget (median $\phiind^{\alpha}$ $1.48$) and calibrate at a quarter of it ($1.10$ deep, $1.01$ shallow), at two to three times the circuit size.
The sharpest case replicates held-out.
A layer-9 circuit that reconstructs perfectly yet barely induces its target at the full budget ($0.17$) \emph{induces} it ($0.83$) when discovered at a quarter of it; the calibration sweep shows the nodes that carry the induction being progressively pruned as training extends ($0.88 \rightarrow 0.57 \rightarrow 0.25$).
Sparsity bought by budget and sparsity bought by price are also \emph{different circuits}: at matched size the two prunings share only about half their nodes, a fifth non-identifiability probe (\cref{sec:underdetermination}).
Two further results on the training ablation methods close the loop.
Coefficient-only training on a frozen node set does not fix the induction overshoot but does inflate the coefficients monotonically; the objective simply does not constrain sufficiency to induce.
Removing the zero-ablation term from training explodes zero-ablation faithfulness to $36$--$1{,}681$ at depth while the trained ablation method stays in band: the sharpest demonstration of the \emph{home-turf effect} (\cref{sec:method-comparison}), now at the level of coefficients rather than node sets.
The training schedule therefore belongs in the method statement: the protocol fixes the full budget, and a quarter of it (at a raised price) calibrates sufficiency to induce better at two to three times the circuit size.

\paragraph{Attribution-mass recovery.}
\citet{ge2024automatically} score circuits by attribution recovery inside their linearised graph, with no intervention; our first-order analogue is \emph{attribution-mass recovery}, the share of total activation-times-gradient mass a node set captures (a surrogate: the exact leaf-sum identity exists only in their substitute model).
Under it the ranking of \cref{tab:matrix} inverts: plain top-$n$ attribution leads (median signed recovery $0.76$), the replication follows ($0.72$), and the weighted circuit trails ($0.52$) while being the only arm that passes the intervention test.
The home-turf effect therefore reaches evaluation frameworks themselves, and the gap carries a finding: a circuit that reproduces the target on held-out intervention while holding a third of the absolute attribution mass ($0.37$ median) shows that first-order attribution mass is not what faithfulness is made of.

\section{Dependencies Are Not Recoverable from Co-activation}
\label{app:coact}
\label{sec:coact-results}
% Moved from the main body (Results) on 2026-09-22 (DAN-24); figure and
% table in app:extra-results.

If the discovered dependencies were merely co-occurrence in disguise, the co-activation graph would predict them.
It does not.
In a full-scale run of the gradient methods producing 7{,}525 accepted circuits, the mean overlap between a circuit's discovered nodes and its target's top co-activation neighbourhood was approximately 3.6\%, the mean pairwise co-activation density among discovered nodes was approximately 0.26\%, and neither quantity correlated with sufficiency to induce (Pearson $r = 0.02$; distributions in \cref{fig:coact-overlap}).
Latent co-activation profiles across the bank are themselves nearly orthogonal (the first two principal components explain roughly 3\% of variance), so local co-occurrence structure carries little of the information that gradient-based contrast discovers.
Causally validated dependency neighbourhoods, in short, are not local co-activation neighbourhoods.

The converse test is to let co-activation structure propose circuits directly.
Under the same 128-target protocol, evidence base, and target-intervention gates, two correlational baselines produced no accepted circuits at all, while ablation gradient accepted circuits for 121 of the same 128 targets (\cref{tab:coact-methods}).
Sparse co-activation expansion follows the strongest co-activation edges outward from the target; differential activation ranks latents by their activation contrast between activating and contrast contexts.
Correlational proposals do not merely score lower; they fail the intervention gates outright.
The strongest version of this challenger --- anchor co-activation selection granted the weighted circuit's own coefficient fitting --- revives on reconstruction but fails the joint calibration suite on every target tested, and is treated in \cref{app:coact-amp}.

The effect of the contrast-context source is itself a finding.
One might expect close contrast contexts, semantically similar to the activating contexts but lacking the target's activation, to produce the sharpest contrastive signal.
In practice the reverse holds for contrastive gradient: random contrast contexts outperform close ones by approximately 0.16 median points of sufficiency to induce.

\section{Specificity}
\label{app:specificity}
% experiments/056-specificity (2026-09-23/24). Pilot numbers: 16 targets.

Faithfulness, necessity, and the random-circuit baseline all read the target alone.
A circuit can therefore restore its target by lifting the target's whole neighbourhood, its concept, topic, or context type, and pass all three: a concept amplifier rather than a mechanism for that latent.
The loss permits it, since reproducing the target's pre-activation constrains the site's stream along one direction of $d_{\mathrm{model}}$, and it leaks into the activation read only when the lifted latents overtake the target in its site's Top-K.

The diagnostic reads the whole code at the target's site, every latent's pre-activation and the SAE's Top-K set at the anchor, in the unmodified model, the empty circuit, and the fitted circuit under each ablation method, on the held-out contexts.
It reports the target's rank and Top-K membership; the number of latents lifted above the unmodified model's Top-K cut, against the empty circuit; the overlap of the circuit's Top-K set with the unmodified one; and the faithfulness of \emph{siblings}, latents in the unmodified Top-K at the anchor on at least half of the training contexts, computed exactly as the target's, against a control group of the site's other active latents.
A circuit whose siblings are restored as well as its target is flagged as an amplifier.

In a pilot, most circuits are specific: the target is restored near $1$ while siblings and other active latents are restored far less.
Under mean ablation, however, circuits lift many latents above the cut that the empty circuit does not, a minority of circuits are amplifiers that pass every faithfulness score, and several deep attention targets lose their Top-K place to lifted rivals although their pre-activation is reproduced.
The rank-keep term (\cref{sec:ablmask}) keeps the target in its Top-K more often but does not reliably remove amplifiers, so specificity is reported for every circuit rather than claimed for the method.

Under the two mean ablations, circuits lift a median of $578$ and $517$ latents above the unmodified Top-K cut, against $5$ and $4$ for the empty circuit, and the target stays in its Top-K on $84\%$ and $80\%$ of held-out anchors.

\section{Extended Related Work}
\label{app:related}
% Moved from the main-body Related Work on 2026-09-22 (DAN-24).

\paragraph{Position-aware circuits.}
Position handling divides prior work into three strategies, each right for its setting: position-indexed nodes on templatic data~\citep{marks2025sparse}, per-instance graphs built one prompt at a time~\citep{ameisen2025circuit}, and schema-aligned positions across variable-length examples~\citep{haklay2025position}.
Our node sets keep the position axis with none of these commitments, using per-position selection unioned over each sequence's causal prefix, for the setting none of the three targets: one reusable circuit over arbitrary non-templatic text (\cref{sec:membership}).

\paragraph{Contrast contexts.}
Retrieved non-activating examples appear in automated interpretability for explanation scoring~\citep{paulo2024automatically}; \citet{paulo2025evaluating} find evaluations collapse when negatives come from neighbouring features, convergent with our random-beats-close result (\cref{app:contrast-source}); and \citet{oneill2024sparse} design corrupted negatives for task-level circuit identification.
We use retrieved contrast contexts for causal discovery rather than explanation scoring, and treat their construction as a controlled variable with measured consequences.

\paragraph{The SAE basis.}
Static structure among latents is well documented: co-occurrence and decoder geometry reveal modularity~\citep{li2025geometry}, coactivation-derived components steer behaviour~\citep{deng2026coactivation}, meta-decompositions show latents are not atomic~\citep{leask2025canonical}, cross-layer feature-flow graphs track latents through the forward pass~\citep{laptev2025feature}, though decoder similarity misses most causally strong feature interactions~\citep{droste2026decoder}, and Jacobian SAEs train adjacent dictionaries for sparse computation between them~\citep{farnik2025jacobian}.
But that structure is correlational, geometric, or weight-based, where the relationships discovered here are context-conditioned and intervention-tested (\cref{sec:coact-results}).
The basis itself is imperfect: reconstruction errors are pathological~\citep{gurnee2024pathological}, downstream utility is mixed~\citep{smith2025negative}, and units are not canonical~\citep{leask2025canonical}.
This conditions our claims: reconstruction residuals stay as explicit anchors, evaluation intervenes on measured activations, and the SAE basis is treated as a useful but imperfect sample of the model's internal variables.

\section{Extended Discussion}
\label{app:discussion}
% Moved from the main-body Discussion on 2026-09-22 (DAN-24).

\paragraph{The non-identifiability probes.}
% Moved from sec:underdetermination on 2026-09-27 (DAN-128, page budget).
% 2026-09-24 leak-fix A/B (055, rkeep3e3 vs rkeep3e3fix, 16 targets): 8/16 pass in
% both; Jaccard median 0.66 (0.10-1.00); H100 vs local same-config refit 0.66.
The first five probes below were measured on unweighted circuit masking before scaling coefficients were added, the last two on WCM (\cref{sec:underdetermination}).
A float32 and a bfloat16 run differ only in rounding, yet exchange $16.5\%$ of nodes at layer 8 (Jaccard $0.835$) at unchanged faithfulness and size.
Raising the sparsity price swaps nodes rather than nesting them (containment $0.49$--$0.80$ between consecutive steps); no gate ever freezes, so the stopping step is another sampling axis; $8$--$25\%$ of nodes sit within $0.05$ of the decision cut and are load-bearing; and short-budget and long-budget runs at matched size share only $0.40$--$0.53$ of their nodes.
Recomputing the mean-ablation values without a quarter of their contexts, a change that leaves the pass rate and median faithfulness unchanged, moves node sets as far as a change of hardware (median Jaccard $0.66$).
A pre-v1 running example's full-budget and quarter-budget circuits both reconstruct the target, yet at $192$ and $250$ nodes they overlap at only Jaccard $0.60$.

\paragraph{Report node sets as equivalence classes.}
The non-identifiability results (\cref{sec:underdetermination}) change what a responsible node-set claim looks like.
Set-level and functional claims (size, faithfulness, the cost findings) are stable under every perturbation we applied.
Per-latent claims (``latent $g$ belongs to target $f$'s circuit'') are not, since node sets for the same target share only $40$--$85\%$ of their latents: the objective admits an equivalence class of near-equivalent optima, and a single run samples one of them.
Claims of the first kind carry this paper; claims of the second kind require survival across perturbations (precision, sparsity path, stopping step) before they are asserted, and the same redundancy that frustrates them is itself a finding: many latents each doing a small share of the same job is exactly what the collective-faithfulness picture predicts.

\paragraph{From circuits to chains.}
The co-activation results close a loop in the premise: if latent dependencies were recoverable from co-occurrence, latent-targeted circuit discovery could be served by cheap statistics, and they are not.
A weighted circuit is one hop: a set of upstream latents that reproduces its target alone, not yet a multi-hop pathway.
Because every circuit is indexed by its target, composition is available in principle: splice the circuit that explains an upstream latent into the circuits that use it, then re-validate by intervening at the earliest nodes and measuring propagation through intermediates.
Non-identifiability sets its boundary condition: each link is one sample from an equivalence class, so composition must manage classes of node sets, not single ones.
That composition-with-validation step, rather than more discovery methods, is the path from dependency neighbourhoods to genuine circuits.

\section{Pseudocode for the Gradient Methods}
\label{app:pseudocode}

\Cref{alg:cfgrad,alg:abgrad,alg:hybrid} summarise the discovery procedures of \cref{sec:cfgrad} as implemented; \cref{alg:restoration,alg:ablmask} summarise restoration-mode attribution (\cref{sec:modes}) and weighted circuit masking (\cref{sec:ablmask}).
Thresholds ($\tau_{\mathrm{act}}$, $\tau_{\mathrm{neg}}$, $\tau_{\mathrm{sup}}$, $\tau_{\mathrm{ind}}$, $\tau_{\mathrm{prune}}$) and top-$K$ sizes are configuration constants shared across the grid; upstream latents additionally require a minimum corpus active count $n_{\min}$, which filters dead or near-dead latents.
All forward passes run with identity passthroughs injected at every SAE site (\cref{sec:cfgrad}), so gradients flow to upstream latent activations, and the target-intervention scores $\phiind$ and $\phisup$ are computed exactly as in \cref{sec:eval}.
Hybrid fusion (\cref{alg:hybrid}) unions the two circuits by latent identity, $\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{hyb}} = \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{ctr}} \cup \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{ab}}$, each node keeping its per-source scores and each directed edge inheriting the higher attribution; optional leave-one-out pruning drops nodes with negligible effect on either acceptance metric, and acceptance requires the fused circuit to clear the sufficiency-to-induce and/or necessity thresholds, with the conjunction set per experiment.

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Contrastive gradient discovery (one target $f$)}
\label{alg:cfgrad}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require target $f$; activating contexts $\posset$; contrast-context source $m \in \{\text{close}, \text{random}, \text{distant}\}$
\State $\negset \gets \textsc{ContrastContexts}(f, m)$ \Comment{retrieved store, random corpus sequences, or maximally dissimilar sequences}
\State $\mu_f^{+} \gets$ mean target activation over $\posset$
\State $(\tau_{\mathrm{act}}, \tau_{\mathrm{neg}}) \gets (\tau_{\mathrm{act}}, \tau_{\mathrm{neg}}) \cdot \max(\mu_f^{+}, 0.1)$ \Comment{scale thresholds so focal targets are not penalised}
\For{each contrast context $x \in \negset$}
    \State forward pass; capture pre-activation $\preact(x, t)$ at the target's site
    \State $t^{*} \gets \arg\max_t \preact(x, t)$;\quad $\Lcf(x) \gets \bigl(\preact(x, t^{*}) - \mu_f^{+}\bigr)^{2}$
    \State backward pass: $s_g^{\mathrm{ctr}}(x) \gets a_g(x, t_g) \cdot \partial \Lcf(x) / \partial a_g(x, t_g)$ for every upstream latent $g$
\EndFor
\State $\Scf \gets \frac{1}{|\negset|}\sum_{x} s_g^{\mathrm{ctr}}(x)$ for every $g$
\State $P \gets$ top-$K$ latents by $\Scf$ with $\Scf \geq \tau_{\mathrm{act}}$ and active count $\geq n_{\min}$
\State $N \gets$ top-$K$ latents by $-\Scf$ with $|\Scf| \geq \tau_{\mathrm{neg}}$ and active count $\geq n_{\min}$
\State $C \gets$ star circuit $\{g \to f\}$ over $P \cup N$, edge weights $\Scf$
\If{$\tau_{\mathrm{prune}} > 0$}
    \State leave-one-out prune nodes whose removal changes $\phiind$ by less than $\tau_{\mathrm{prune}}$
\EndIf
\State $(\phiind, \phisup) \gets$ target-intervention evaluation of $C$ on $\posset$ and $\negset$
\State \Return $C$ if $\phiind \geq \tau_{\mathrm{ind}}$, otherwise reject
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Ablation gradient discovery (one target $f$)}
\label{alg:abgrad}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require target $f$; activating contexts $\posset$
\For{each activating context $(x, t) \in \posset$}
    \State forward pass; backward pass from the target's sparse activation $a_f(x, t)$
    \State $s_g^{\mathrm{ab}}(x) \gets a_g(x, t_g) \cdot \partial a_f(x, t) / \partial a_g(x, t_g)$ for every upstream latent $g$
\EndFor
\State $\Sab \gets \frac{1}{|\posset|}\sum_{x} s_g^{\mathrm{ab}}(x)$ for every $g$
\State $P \gets$ top-$K$ latents by $\Sab$ above threshold, active count $\geq n_{\min}$
\State $C \gets$ star circuit $\{g \to f\}$ over $P$, edge weights $\Sab$
\If{$\tau_{\mathrm{prune}} > 0$}
    \State leave-one-out prune nodes whose removal changes $\phisup$ by less than $\tau_{\mathrm{prune}}$
\EndIf
\State $(\phiind, \phisup) \gets$ target-intervention evaluation of $C$
\State \Return $C$ if $\phisup \geq \tau_{\mathrm{sup}}$, otherwise reject
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Hybrid gradient discovery (one target $f$)}
\label{alg:hybrid}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require target $f$; acceptance mode $\in \{\phiind, \phisup, \text{both, either}\}$
\State $C_{\mathrm{ctr}} \gets$ \cref{alg:cfgrad} on $f$;\quad $C_{\mathrm{ab}} \gets$ \cref{alg:abgrad} on $f$ \Comment{either may reject}
\If{both rejected} \State \Return reject \EndIf
\State $C \gets$ fuse returned circuits by latent identity: union of nodes, each retaining its per-source attribution scores; each directed edge keeps the higher attribution
\If{$\tau_{\mathrm{prune}} > 0$}
    \State leave-one-out prune nodes with negligible effect on either acceptance metric
\EndIf
\State $(\phiind, \phisup) \gets$ target-intervention evaluation of the fused $C$
\State \Return $C$ if the acceptance mode's condition holds ($\phiind \geq \tau_{\mathrm{ind}}$ and/or $\phisup \geq \tau_{\mathrm{sup}}$), otherwise reject
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Restoration-mode attribution (one target $f$; \texttt{ig\_restoration} substitutes per-round integrated-gradient scoring in line 4)}
\label{alg:restoration}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require target $f$; activating contexts $\posset$; round budget $R$ = number of upstream sites; per-round admission size $k_r$
\State $M \gets \varnothing$; ablate every upstream latent (every latent at its ablation value)
\For{round $r = 1, \ldots, R$}
    \State forward with nodes $M$ recomputed from the modified stream and all other latents ablated; capture the target pre-activation $\preact$
    \State score every latent $g \notin M$ by gradient-times-activation at the \emph{current} partially restored stream \Comment{re-linearisation: later rounds see the consequences of earlier admissions}
    \State $M \gets M \cup \{\text{top-}k_r \text{ latents not in } M \text{ by score}\}$
    \If{$\preact$ is within tolerance of its natural value} \textbf{break} \Comment{restoration certificate}
    \EndIf
\EndFor
\State $C \gets$ circuit over $M$ with per-round scores; target-intervention and ablation-faithfulness evaluation as configured
\State \Return $C$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Weighted circuit masking, WCM[Z+C+A] with rank-keep (one target $f$; protocol v1, \cref{tab:protocol})}
\label{alg:ablmask}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require target $f$; activating contexts $\posset$ and contrast contexts $\negset$, each split 48 train / 16 held out; $\lambda = 10^{-3}$; $\gamma_{\mathrm{C}} = \gamma_{\mathrm{A}} = 0.25$; $\gamma_{\mathrm{R}} = 3 \times 10^{-3}$; steps $S = 400$
\State target $\gets$ natural pre-activation of $f$ at the training anchors (no-grad forward);\quad $\nu \gets \operatorname{mean}(\text{target}^2)$
\State $r \gets$ rank of $f$ among its site's other latents at each training anchor, unmodified model
\State ablation values: C $\gets$ site means over the \emph{training} contrast contexts; A $\gets$ over the \emph{training} activating contexts
\State initialise $\theta_s = 4$, $\psi_s = \mathrm{softplus}^{-1}(1)$ for every upstream site $s$
\For{step $= 1, \ldots, S$}
    \State $T \gets 0.05^{\,\text{step}/S}$; \quad gates $m_s \gets \sigma(\theta_s / T)$; \quad coefficients $\alpha_s \gets \mathrm{softplus}(\psi_s)$ \Comment{geometric anneal $1 \rightarrow 0.05$}
    \For{$\pi \in \{\mathrm{Z}, \mathrm{C}, \mathrm{A}\}$} \Comment{same $\theta, \psi$ under all three ablation methods}
        \State forward with kept latents at $\alpha \times$ live value, closed latents at their $\pi$ ablation value
        \State $\mathcal{L}_{\pi} \gets$ MSE of $f$'s pre-activation against target; \quad $\mathcal{R}_{\pi} \gets \operatorname{mean}\,\operatorname{relu}(\tau^{(r)}_{\pi} - \preact)^2$
    \EndFor
    \State AdamW step on $\sum_{\pi} \gamma_{\pi}(\mathcal{L}_{\pi} + \gamma_{\mathrm{R}}\mathcal{R}_{\pi})/\nu + \lambda \sum_{s,i} \bigl[\sigma(\theta_{s,i}) + (1 - m_{s,i})\,|\alpha_{s,i} - 1|\bigr]$
\EndFor
\State circuit $\gets \{\,(i, \alpha_i) : m_i > \tfrac{1}{2}\,\}$; evaluate on the held-out contexts under \cref{sec:eval-abl}
\State \Return circuit
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Context Stores and Contrast Contexts}
\label{app:stores}

\subsection{Context Store Formalism}

The collection pass assigns each latent a scored context set
$\mathcal{C}_f = \{((x,t), a_f(x,t)) : a_f(x,t) > 0\}$.
The top-context store retains the $K_{\mathrm{top}}$ highest-scoring active contexts,
\[
    \operatorname{TopCtx}_K(f)
    =
    \operatorname*{arg\,top}_{S \subseteq \mathcal{C}_f,\ |S|=K_{\mathrm{top}}}
    \sum_{((x,t),s)\in S} s,
\]
approximating the upper tail of the conditional activation distribution.
The mid-context store is a bounded reservoir sample from the sigma band,
\[
    \operatorname{MidCtx}_M(f)
    \sim
    \operatorname{ReservoirSample}_M\!\left(
    \{(x,t) : \mu_f + 0.5\,\sigma_f \leq a_f(x,t) \leq \mu_f + 1.5\,\sigma_f\}
    \right),
\]
maintained without sorting every active occurrence.
Mid contexts distinguish a latent's ordinary operating regime from its most vivid examples and provide probe data where extreme contexts would be too narrow.

\paragraph{Contrast-context retrieval.}
Let $r(x) \in \R^{1024}$ be the retrieval representation of sequence $x$: the final layer's residual stream, mean-pooled over positions, captured once during collection, and let $\posset$ be the sequences stored as activating contexts for $f$.
The query is the normalised centroid of the activating contexts
\[
    c_f = \operatorname{normalize}\!\left(
    \frac{1}{|\posset|} \sum_{x_p \in \posset} r(x_p)
    \right),
\]
and the store retains the $n = 64$ sequences most similar to $c_f$ that are not stored activating contexts:
\[
    \operatorname{ContrastCtx}_n(f)
    =
    \operatorname*{arg\,top}_{S \subseteq \mathcal{D} \setminus \posset,\ |S|=n}
    \sum_{x \in S} \bigl\langle c_f,\, r(x) \bigr\rangle .
\]
Retrieval is exact rather than approximate: representations are $\ell_2$-normalised and full cosine similarities are computed by chunked matrix multiplication on GPU, querying the $K = 512$ nearest candidates before the filter that removes stored activating contexts.
This store serves the attribution baselines.
Its representation index is a sample of $2 \times 10^{5}$ sequences, so for targets none of whose activating contexts fall in the sample the centroid is undefined, and the store returns one shared default set for all of them (38\% of TuringLLM targets); the rest are anchored on the few activating contexts the sample contains.

\paragraph{Contrast contexts for WCM.}
Weighted circuit masking and its evaluation instead select contrast contexts per target at fit time.
Every activating context is represented, read from the index when present and otherwise computed by a forward pass; candidates from the sampled corpus are ranked by their highest cosine similarity to any activating context, $\max_{x_p \in \posset} \langle r(x_p), r(x) \rangle$, with the activating contexts excluded; and each candidate is run through the model and kept only if the target is outside its site's Top-K at every position, until 64 are selected.
The silence check rejects between 14\% and 51\% of the closest candidates on the targets we inspected, so unchecked retrieval would place many contrast contexts where the target fires.
The same selector with random or maximally dissimilar ranking gives the \emph{random} and \emph{distant} sources of \cref{app:contrast-source}.
% 2026-09-22/23 (DAN-64): the 15k production circuits were fitted with the store
% (floor_negctx_mode "store"); NegContextSelector("close") is protocol v1.

\paragraph{Logit faithfulness.}
\label{app:logit-faith}
The output-level score of \cref{sec:sfc-boundary} is the faithfulness formula of \cref{sec:eval} with the target's value replaced by the output metric $m$, the mean log-probability of the actual next token at the anchor: $(m(C) - m(\varnothing)) / (m(M) - m(\varnothing))$, with every in-scope latent outside the circuit mean-ablated.

\section{Evaluation Protocol Studies}
\label{app:eval-protocol}

\subsection{Why Position Aggregation Fails: Union Coverage}
\label{app:union-coverage}
The SAE code is $k$-sparse per position, so a position-collapsed circuit evaluated across a target's causal prefix must cover the \emph{union} of every position's active set.
Measured on a target's probe batch, that union is large at every site (roughly $1$--$3.7$k distinct latents per attention-output site, $7$--$10$k per residual-stream site, and $7$--$11.8$k per MLP-output site), and the burden concentrates in the additive residual carrier: mean-ablating any single residual-stream site alone sends the target to $0.000$, while ablating any single attention-output site leaves $0.90$--$0.96$.
% data: topk_distribution.py, verify_eval.py (dev-notes)
The harness passes its identity check, keep-all identity at the reduced-precision noise level ($0.994$), and a selection-agnostic density sweep reproduces the wall; two scoring defects found later in this harness (a mixed pre-/post-Top-K read and ablation limited to member sites) are corrected in protocol v1 (\cref{app:protocol}), so the numbers here are indicative; position-collapsed faithfulness failure is a property of the dense residual stream plus position aggregation, not an artifact of the harness.

\subsection{Sparsity-Preserving Mean Ablation}
\label{app:respect-topk}
The classic dense mean ablation assigns every latent outside the circuit its mean, producing a stream with tens of thousands of simultaneously active latents per position, outside the state space a Top-K SAE can produce.
The sparsity-preserving variant applies the means and then re-imposes the per-position Top-K (kept latents, then the largest means of the other latents up to $k$, rest zero), which restores the keep-all identity to exactly $1.0$.
At scale (32 targets, paired) the sparsity-preserving variant does not systematically shift faithfulness: it raises both $a_C$ and $a_{\varnothing}$, leaving the ratio a wash ($50$W/$46$L).
It therefore functions as a cleaner protocol rather than a score inflator; but at small node sets and tight Top-K budgets the two variants can disagree on \emph{rankings}, which is why the body's comparisons name their ablation method.
% data: analysis-restyled/respect-topk-sweep/

\subsection{Sources of the Mean Ablation}
\label{app:floors}
The empty-circuit baseline $a_{\varnothing}$ is a measured object with its own failure modes.
Means over \emph{activating} contexts leak target signal into $a_{\varnothing}$ increasingly with depth ($a_{\varnothing}/a_{\mathrm{pos}} = 0/23/30/26\%$ at layers 2/8/9/10); means over the target's \emph{contrast} contexts hold $a_{\varnothing} = 0.0000$ on every target measured, even under the hardest close contrast contexts.
As an ablation method during \emph{discovery}, however, contrast-context mean ablation is neutral for path attribution and destructive for restoration (recomputed faithfulness $-0.07$ to $-0.98$, $+26\%$ nodes, monotone in contrast-context distance), another instance of discovery and evaluation ablation methods needing to be chosen separately and stated.
A three-way cross-tab of mean sources (the target's activating contexts, a random corpus sample, and a farthest-point sample of the corpus by sequence representation) shows method \emph{rankings} are robust to the source while absolute levels shift with how much target signal the mean carries, and sufficiency to induce is invariant to it; every reported number therefore names its ablation method.

\section{Attribution-Mode and Pruning Studies}
\label{app:mode-studies}

% Cut 2026-09-24 (pass 3): mode comparison at scale (tri-mode-128), budget parity
% (restoration-parity), consistent re-scoring (polish-{off,on}), non-oracle gate.
Comparisons in this appendix use within-target pairing or depth stratification throughout.

\subsection{Recurrence Pruning}
A large tail of nodes appears in exactly one probe sequence: context-specific scaffolding rather than mechanism.
Pruning nodes that fire in fewer than two probe sequences removes ${\sim}10\%$ of nodes for ${\sim}0.010$ recomputed faithfulness at every depth; the harsher eight-sequence bar is smaller but fails at depth (recomputed faithfulness $0.680$ vs $1.049$ at layer 10, and the dense mean-ablation score goes negative at layer 8), so the two-sequence bar is the smallest setting that survives both ablation methods and depth.
Nodes from the contrastive gradient are judged on contrast contexts and nodes from the ablation gradient on activating contexts, and any node the prune cannot observe is exempted rather than dropped.
% data: dev-notes/node-count-and-pruning-progress.md

\subsection{Effect Thresholds Cannot Replace Validated Stopping}
Holding the ranking fixed and swapping the stopping rule isolates what does the work.
A fixed absolute effect threshold at the field-default value sits ${\sim}2.3\times$ above our \emph{maximum} node score (it deletes everything), and even percentile cuts calibrated to our own score distribution crater function (the dose-response of \cref{sec:drivers-closure}), while bisection over the identical ranking preserves it.
The stopping rule, not the ranking, carries the validity.
% data: experiments/003-eff-prune-test/

\subsection{Selection Rules and the Pruning Frontier}
A per-target percentile cut on $|$attribution$|$ dominates top-$N$ and every per-position--normalised selection rule at equal or better faithfulness with $1.4$--$2.7\times$ fewer latents (the driving signal is peaked in a \emph{global} magnitude sense; per-position normalisation wastes budget and needs a dead-position guard).
The magnitude-bisection prune then composes with it: percentile selection raises the achievable faithfulness ceiling, the prune lowers size at fixed faithfulness, and the composition dominates on all six test targets (layers 3--11).
On the mid-depth residual-stream target, top-$N$ selection cannot reach the requested faithfulness at all, while the composed recipe reaches it at less than half the node count.
% data: thresh_sweep.py, stack_sweep.py, target_phi_sweep.py; eval/magnitude_prune.py

\subsection{Direct-Effect Edges}
\label{app:wcm-edges}
Edges are attached with the direct-effect construction of \citet{marks2025sparse}: an edge's weight is the indirect effect of an upstream node on the metric via its \emph{direct} effect on the downstream node, with intermediate nodes stop-gradiented, computed as batched vector--Jacobian products.
Edges never change the node set or any evaluation score; they are descriptive.
But their statistics mirror the discovery mode: local attribution yields sparse shallow graphs (${\sim}9$ edges per node, depth ${\sim}4$) where path attribution yields dense deep ones (${\sim}40$ edges per node, depth ${\sim}10$), structure following the estimator's linearisation.
% data: analysis-restyled/edge-pilot/; code: src/circuit/instrument/edge_attribution.py
For weighted circuits the same construction is linearised around the circuit's own run (nodes at their scaled values, every other latent at the ablation value), so the coefficients enter both the upstream change and the downstream Jacobian, and edges are computed separately under each ablation method; a node's out-edges plus its direct edge to the target then sum exactly to its own attribution, which the implementation reproduces to within $1\%$.
% experiments/057-wcm-edges (weighted_edges.py). Pilot, main 16: pinned causal edge check r ~0.93-0.99;
% Z/A/C edge sets overlap at Jaccard ~0.28, so only edges shared by all three are drawn.
The edges also hold beyond first order: with every other node pinned at its circuit value and the upstream node set to its ablation value, the measured change in the downstream node reproduces the edge weight.
In a pilot, the wiring is multi-step rather than a star, with most of a circuit's attribution reaching the target through other circuit nodes.
It is also dense, with positive and negative routes largely cancelling and no small set of edges carrying most of the weight, and edge sets computed under different ablation methods overlap only partly.
We therefore interpret only edges that clear a threshold with one sign under all three ablation methods, and state the share of edge weight they carry; \pending{wiring statistics on the protocol-v1 circuits, DAN-75}.
An edge's sign combines whether the upstream node raises the downstream one with the downstream node's marginal effect on the target, so a negative edge need not mean suppression.
The residual-stream relay of \cref{sec:relay} is such a shared structure.
% 057 pilot (14 multi-site targets, medians over Z/A/C): direct share of attribution 0.22-0.37; member-edge mass
% 3.3-4.0x node mass; top-10 edges 4-5% of edge mass, half the mass needs 1,167-1,758 edges; depth ~10 at 1e-3;
% Z/A/C Jaccard at 1e-3 0.28, consensus 82 of 839 edges; pinned causal r 0.93 (Z) / 0.98 (A) / 0.99 (C).

\subsection{Error Nodes for a Target Latent}
Keeping SAE errors as ablatable nodes reproduces, for target latents, the error-node picture reported for output targets~\citep{marks2025sparse}, with one asymmetry in our favour: the empty-circuit anchor is error-free here ($a_{\varnothing}$ identical with and without error terms, $0.000$ at layers 2 and 9), so the collapse of the empty-circuit baseline does not arise.
Faithfulness at depth, however, is error-carried: zeroing all upstream error terms leaves recomputed faithfulness ${\approx}1.08$ at layer 2 but $0.005$ at layer 9.
Because raw and pruned node sets occupy every upstream site, including error nodes selectively awaits discovery-side error attribution.

\section{Weighted Circuit Masking Design Ablations}
\label{app:membership-studies}
Rejected or superseded designs for WCM, recorded because each closes a natural reviewer question.

\subsection{Loss Terms for Target Rank and Specificity}
\label{app:rank-designs}
% experiments/055-close-contrast (arms otcut*, otrank2, rtop*, marginp, rkeep*), 056-specificity.
An \emph{off-target} term charging every other site latent for rising above its unmodified and empty-circuit values cut lifts three- to sevenfold at no faithfulness cost, but addressed a property the method need not guarantee (\cref{app:specificity}) and grew circuits when it also charged harmless lifts below the cut.
A \emph{Top-K hinge} requiring only that the target beat the $k$-th other latent restored faithfulness but, used alone, let circuits keep the target in the Top-K by lifting it together with its neighbourhood.
A convex pull towards rank $1$ inflated circuits by an order of magnitude at every weight tried, since it never switches off and dominates once the reproduction error is small.
The \emph{firing-margin} objective, fitting the target's lead over the Top-K cut, needs its loss normalised by the target's pre-activation scale rather than the margin's, which is near zero for targets at the cut.
Rank-keep, which switches off once the target holds its natural rank, is the single term that kept the gains without these costs, and its effect is flat across a thirtyfold range of weights.

\subsection{Sparsity Pricing and Normalisation}
Five rejected variants of the sparsity price.
Gradient-scaled sparsity pricing is \emph{anti}-correlated with need; pricing inversely to site count is non-monotone; predicting the price from target-latent features misses requested sizes by $24$--$29\%$ where one-probe calibration lands within ${\sim}4\%$; initialising gates from observed activity buys no speedup (the optimiser updates all parameters in parallel, so deadweight costs nothing); and gradient-weighted per-site pricing is dominated by flat pricing, with the methodological footnote that quantiles over mostly-zero gradient tensors measure sparsity, not scale.

Two further design records support \cref{sec:ablmask}.
The normaliser for training under two ablation methods is target-scaled and bounded by construction because its predecessor was not: normalising each term by its own fully-closed-mask loss, which is unbounded far from the data, silently annihilated the zero-ablation term at depth (the two closed-mask losses differ there by up to eight orders of magnitude), reducing WCM[Z+C] training to the C term alone; the design rule is that a normaliser must never be able to annihilate its own term.
% AUDIT 2026-09-22 (DAN-71): single-ablation-method fits use raw MSE, not this
% normaliser, so single vs multi comparisons at matched lambda are size-confounded.
The one-probe size calibration is the formula $\lambda = \lambda_0 \, (n_{\mathrm{probe}} / n_{\mathrm{req}})^{1/q}$: a single reference run at $\lambda_0$ measures $n_{\mathrm{probe}}$, landing requested sizes within a few percent where a per-size search~\citep[cf.\ the sparsity-targeting of][]{bhaskar2024finding} would cost several runs per target.
% data: experiments/010-lambda-calibration/ (README carries the full trail)

\subsection{Binarisation Sweep}
The straight-through estimator makes interior gate values forward-irrelevant, so the sparsity penalty drifts nodes toward just-above-threshold: the decision boundary becomes an \emph{attractor}, and per-step node flips grow through training ($218$ per step at layer 10), a stopping-step lottery.
Annealing instead yields the tightest zero-ablation faithfulness in the programme ($0.002$--$0.10$ deviation from unity) at slightly worse mean-ablation scores, another home-turf demonstration.
Schedule compression does not help: a $200$-step run matches the $400$-step metrics at ${\sim}2\times$ the per-step churn (churn is priced by per-step pressure, not schedule shape); reach-then-hold leaves churn unchanged and inflates size $21$--$48\%$; and truncating a $400$-step run at $200$ yields node sets $57$--$72\%$ oversized with the gate still half-soft.

\subsection{Co-Activation with Fitted Scaling Coefficients: the Strongest Correlational Baseline}
\label{app:coact-amp}
% Data: experiments/036-coact-home (pre-v1). The 035 transcoder and 037
% GemmaScope JumpReLU replications were cut (C12: outside the Top-K scope).
\Cref{sec:coact-results} shows correlational proposals failing the intervention gates outright; the fairest possible strengthening of that challenger is to hand it the weighted-circuit machinery itself.
Take the $n$ latents with the largest summed activation at the target's anchors (size-matched to the discovered weighted circuit), and score them twice through the identical pipeline: at natural values ($\alpha = 1$), and with scaling coefficients fitted by the same objective, ablation methods, and budget as the discovered run.

At natural values the selection fails everywhere, and its failure is directional: $0.08$--$0.51$ at layer~2, but $7.6$--$9.1$ at layer~9 --- co-activation picks latents that fire alongside the target and misses the rest of the stream that normally holds it in range, so keeping only its picks \emph{overdrives} the target several-fold.
With fitted coefficients it becomes the strongest baseline in the programme: zero-ablation faithfulness reaches ${\sim}1$ at layer~2 on every target.
The joint suite still separates it from the discovered circuits.
Under the production recipe, WCM[Z+C+A], best against best, the discovered weighted circuits hold faithfulness under both zero and mean ablation on all five targets (three at layer~2, two at layer~9) (zero ablation $0.92$--$1.06$, mean ablation $0.86$--$1.18$), while the co-activation sets hold both on none: at layer~2 the fitted coefficients calibrate against one background and overshoot the other (mean ablation $1.30$--$2.47$, coefficients reaching $6$--$10\times$), and at layer~9 no coefficient assignment reconciles the co-activation selection with all three ablation methods at once (zero ablation $3.09$ on one target, $0.79$ on the other).
These are TuringLLM targets under the pre-v1 recipe (\pending{rerun under protocol v1}).
Reconstruction alone is therefore spoofable by a calibrated correlational set, and the revision to \cref{sec:coact-results} is precise: correlational \emph{selection} without coefficients fails outright, and with coefficients it fails calibration --- what it cannot buy at any coefficient setting is a node set whose contribution is invariant to the ablation method, which is what weighted circuit masking selects for.
