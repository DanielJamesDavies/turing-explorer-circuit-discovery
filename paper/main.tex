\begin{abstract}
Circuit discovery typically explains an output: a logit, a probability, a behavioural metric.
We ask instead what makes an internal latent fire.
For a target sparse autoencoder (SAE) latent, \emph{weighted circuit masking} (WCM) learns a circuit of upstream latents with a scaling coefficient for each, trained so that the circuit alone reproduces the target under three different ablations of everything else.
% REWRITE 2026-10-12 (DAN-161 step 9): sentences 4-5 = claim 1 (with the random null), 6-7 = claim 2.
% 2026-10-24 (paper prep, DAN-157/181): 46%, 1.5% / 45% and 747 are as-run numbers (original held-out sets, DAN-17
% null). Swap in the rebuilt-held-out macros (experiments/087-intervals, e.g. \passall) and the harder-null result.
At a sparsity penalty that yields circuits of $10^{2}$--$10^{3}$ upstream latents, $46\%$ of ${\sim}15{,}000$ sampled latents in TuringLLM, a 12-layer language model, get a circuit that reproduces the target under all three ablations and is necessary for it, on held-out text, while random circuits of the same size, given the same coefficient fitting, fail on all $747$ targets tested \pending{rebuilt held-out re-score, DAN-157; harder random nulls, DAN-181; Pythia-70m and GPT-2 small, DAN-40/41}.
A weaker penalty gives larger circuits that pass more often.
The coefficients are what keep these circuits small: without them the same nodes pass for $1.5\%$ of targets instead of $45\%$, and circuits for targets in the last four layers need ${\sim}10^{4}$ latents to pass, about $10\times$ more, with only half as many of those targets passing at all.
The procedure needs no behavioural task and applies to any latent in an SAE bank.
\end{abstract}

Consider a latent in layer 10 of a 12-layer language model that fires on the possessive in ``Ohm's'', ``Snell's'', or ``Kirchhoff's'', before the word ``law'' has appeared, but stays silent on ``Darwin's'', ``Rutherford's'', and ``Picasso's''.
Given an invented name, it fires in a passage on electromagnetism and not in one on the history of painting.
Something upstream supplies what the model knows about which names carry laws, and what the passage is about.
What makes this latent fire?

Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) decompose a model's activations into latents like this one, which often track coherent concepts~\citep{cunningham2024sparse, bricken2023monosemanticity, gao2024scaling, templeton2024scaling}, a working answer to superposition~\citep{elhage2022toy}.
A latent says what the model represents; it does not say how the model computes it.
That question belongs to circuit discovery, whose methods, over components or features and by search, attribution, or learned masks, predominantly target an output~\citep{conmy2023towards, syed2023attribution, marks2025sparse, ameisen2025circuit, bhaskar2024finding, caples2025scaling}.
An output is the model's computation expressed in our vocabulary; targeting latents instead can capture variables that never show up as a token.

Latent targets have been approached by attribution, tracing upstream of a chosen SAE latent and thresholding the scores~\citep{ge2024automatically, golimblevskaia2025circuit}.
To our knowledge, no method \emph{optimises} a circuit for a single target latent and tests it by intervening on that latent.
We call this \emph{latent-targeted circuit discovery} (\cref{fig:overview}).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\resizebox{\linewidth}{!}{\input{figures/overview}}
\caption{How weighted circuit masking works.
\textbf{(a)}~For a target latent $f$, every latent at every upstream SAE site is a candidate node.
\textbf{(b)}~Each candidate gets a gate $m$ (kept or closed) and a scaling coefficient $\alpha$: a kept latent contributes $\alpha$ times its live value, and a closed one is replaced by its ablation value.
\textbf{(c)}~They are trained so that, under each ablation method, the circuit reproduces $f$'s pre-activation at its anchor positions, with a sparsity penalty on every open gate.
\textbf{(d)}~The weighted circuit, typically $10^{2}$--$10^{3}$ latents, is tested on held-out contexts by faithfulness, necessity, and sufficiency to induce, against same-size random circuits with fitted coefficients.}
\label{fig:overview}
\end{figure}

Our method, \emph{weighted circuit masking} (WCM), learns two things for every upstream latent: a gate for whether it belongs to the circuit, and a scaling coefficient for how strongly it acts.
Both are trained so that the circuit alone reproduces the target with everything else ablated in three ways at once: set to zero, or to its mean over contexts where the target fires, or where it is silent.
We test each circuit on held-out text for \emph{faithfulness} (the circuit alone reproduces the target), \emph{necessity} (removing the circuit removes the target), and \emph{sufficiency to induce} (setting the circuit's values switches the target on where it was silent).

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Circuit discovery.}
Manual analysis of components~\citep{olah2020zoom, wang2023interpretability} gave way to automated search: ACDC~\citep{conmy2023towards} and Edge Pruning~\citep{bhaskar2024finding} prune components or edges against an output metric, and attribution patching~\citep{nanda2023attribution, syed2023attribution, kramar2024atp} and its refinements~\citep{hanna2024faith, rezaeijafari2025relp} score them to first order (our IG baseline adapts EAP-IG's estimator to nodes).
Sparse feature circuits~\citep{marks2025sparse} moved the nodes to SAE features with error nodes, and attribution graphs~\citep{ameisen2025circuit, lindsey2025biology} build per-prompt graphs over transcoder features~\citep{dunefsky2024transcoders}, a substrate we do not run.
Their objectives are anchored at an output, and circuits for one task can differ across its prompts~\citep{makou2026many}.

\paragraph{Circuits for internal features.}
Internal targets are not new.
Hierarchical attribution extracts the subgraph behind a chosen SAE feature~\citep{ge2024automatically}, dictionary features have been traced patch-free in Othello-GPT~\citep{he2024dictionary}, transcoder features are reverse-engineered from their input circuits~\citep{dunefsky2024transcoders}, latent attribution debugs a chosen latent~\citep{duprelatour2025debugging}, CircuitLens explains a feature's activation from upstream interactions~\citep{golimblevskaia2025circuit}, and attribution graphs have been extended to concept directions read by linear probes~\citep{palit2026probes}.
Upstream features have also been ranked by single ablations~\citep{cunningham2024sparse} or linked by sparse regression~\citep{yin2026scalable}.
These methods attribute, ablate one node at a time, or regress; circuit probing is closer in spirit, optimising a mask over model weights for a labelled intermediate variable~\citep{lepori2023uncovering}, and sparsely connected transcoders learn latent-to-latent masks while the dictionary is trained~\citep{drori2025sparsely}.

\paragraph{Masks and scalars.}
Learned masks descend from subnetwork probing~\citep{cao2021low, conmy2023towards} and edge pruning~\citep{bhaskar2024finding, li2024optimal}; over SAE latents they have localised attributes~\citep{chaudhary2024evaluating} and found behavioural circuits~\citep{caples2025scaling}, the latter being our unweighted circuit masking with its objective moved to a latent.
Such masks keep a node at its natural scale or ablate it; learned per-unit scales serve other jobs, in pruning, steering, and regression~\citep{liu2017learning, kwon2022fast, stoehr2024activation, yin2026scalable}, and gate-times-magnitude parametrisations separate membership from strength outside circuit discovery~\citep{louizos2018learning, lai2025joint}.

\paragraph{Evaluating circuits.}
What removing a latent means can decide the result~\citep{miller2024transformer, mueller2024missed}; optimal ablation learns it per component~\citep{li2024optimal}, the dual of our per-node coefficients, while our ablations stay fixed by data; denoising and noising answer different questions~\citep{heimersheim2024patching}, and our sufficiency to induce is a denoising-style test.
Prior work evaluates under several ablations but discovers under one; the nearest objectives sum two directions of one counterfactual~\citep{chen2025rethinking} or certify bounded patching perturbations~\citep{hadad2026formal}.
Size-matched random circuits~\citep{shi2024hypothesis, seth2026circuitkit} go unfitted, like the circuits they test, while ours share our circuits' coefficient fitting; causal scrubbing~\citep{chan2022causal} is stricter (\cref{app:resample}), and MIB standardises evaluation for output targets~\citep{mueller2025mib}.

What this paper adds is the conjunction: circuits of SAE latents \emph{optimised} for a single target latent, with a gate and a separate \emph{fitted} coefficient per node, trained under three ablations at once, and tested on held-out contexts against random circuits given the same fitting, for ${\sim}15$k sampled latents per model \pending{only TuringLLM so far; Pythia-70m and GPT-2 small, DAN-40/41} (further related work in \cref{app:related}).

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

For a latent $f$ and a sequence--position pair $(x, t)$, write $a_f(x, t) \geq 0$ for its activation (after Top-K) and $\preact(x, t)$ for its pre-activation (before it); pseudocode is in \cref{app:pseudocode}.

\subsection{Problem}
\label{sec:problem}
\label{sec:evidence}

\paragraph{Targets and contexts.}
Fix a target latent $f$ and let $U_f$ be the latents at every SAE site that causally precedes $f$'s site.
Each target has \emph{activating contexts} from two stores, up to 64 of its strongest-activating sequences and up to 64 drawn from the middle of its activation distribution, each with an anchor at the target's peak (\cref{app:stores}).
With full stores it trains on 32 strongest and 16 mid-band contexts and is scored on 16 held-out strongest contexts (primary) and 16 held-out mid-band contexts (reported); the split is stratified by activation and always trains on the two strongest (\cref{app:protocol}).
% 2026-10-24 (DAN-157, rule approved by Daniel 2026-10-24; src/circuit/heldout_rule.py, experiments/086-heldout-rebuild).
% No source-file rule. Details and the audit (085) in app:heldout.
Held-out strongest contexts exclude every training-side context and its exact, anchor-prefix and near copies (a shared run of $\geq 32$ tokens or $\geq 50\%$ shared 8-grams), filled to 16 from the next strongest unseen contexts (floor 8); held-out contrast contexts follow the same rule (\cref{app:heldout}).
It also has 64 \emph{contrast contexts}: corpus sequences ranked by their highest cosine similarity to any of its strongest-activating contexts in pooled final-layer representation space, excluding its activating contexts, and kept only if the target stays out of its site's Top-K on them, so that they are close to the target's contexts but verified silent (\cref{app:stores}).
For the running example they are history passages about other periods, such as the Enlightenment, Renaissance music, or the pre-Columbian era.
% PROTOCOL v1 (2026-09-24, DAN-64 / DAN-7): NegContextSelector("close"), silence =
% post-Top-K activation 0. The earlier pre-built kNN store gave 38% of targets one
% shared fallback list (seq_repr capped at 200k of 24.8M sequences); WCM no longer uses it.
Targets are drawn uniformly at random, the same number per site (before targets without usable contexts are dropped), with no prioritisation heuristic (\cref{app:protocol}).

\paragraph{Circuit-only execution.}
A \emph{weighted circuit} is a node set $C \subseteq U_f$ with a scaling coefficient $\alpha_{s,i} > 0$ for each node.
Under an ablation method $\pi$, the forward pass is modified at every upstream site $s$: the site's Top-K code $c_s$, computed from the (already modified) stream, is replaced by
\[
    \hat{c}_{s,i} =
    \begin{cases}
        \alpha_{s,i}\, c_{s,i} & (s, i) \in C \\
        v^{\pi}_{s,i} & \text{otherwise,}
    \end{cases}
\]
and the stream continues as $x + W_{\mathrm{dec}}^{s}(\hat{c}_s - c_s)$, so SAE errors pass through unchanged and every downstream site, including the target's, reads the modified stream.
Nodes are \emph{recomputed}, not clamped: a node's value at each position is whatever the modified stream gives it.
The ablation value $v^{\pi}$ is zero (Z), the latent's mean over activating contexts (A), or its mean over contrast contexts (C), optionally sparsity-preserving, with the per-position Top-K re-imposed.
An unweighted circuit fixes $\alpha = 1$.
Writing $a_C$ for the target's mean activation at the anchors under this intervention, $a_{\varnothing}$ for the empty circuit, and $a_{\mathrm{pos}}$ for the unmodified model, \emph{faithfulness} is
\[
    \phi^{\mathrm{free}}_{\pi}(C) = \frac{a_C - a_{\varnothing}}{a_{\mathrm{pos}} - a_{\varnothing}}.
\]

\paragraph{The problem.}
Faithfulness depends on the ablation method~\citep{miller2024transformer, mueller2024missed}, and a circuit trained under one method can fail under another (\cref{app:single-method}), so one weighted circuit is asked to serve all three:
\begin{equation}\tag{P}\label{eq:p2}
    n_{\varepsilon}(f)
    \;=\;
    \min \bigl\{\, |C| \;:\; \exists\, \alpha \ \text{ with } \ \phi^{\mathrm{free}}_{\pi}(C, \alpha) \geq 1 - \varepsilon \ \text{ for every } \pi \in \{\mathrm{Z}, \mathrm{C}, \mathrm{A}\} \,\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
WCM solves a differentiable relaxation of \labelcref{eq:p2}; with $\alpha = 1$ the smallest faithful node sets are several times larger (\cref{sec:weighted}).
Problem~\labelcref{eq:p2} asks for \emph{a} minimiser, and its near-optimal solutions form a large equivalence class (\cref{sec:underdetermination}), so only class-invariant quantities are treated as properties of the target.

\subsection{Weighted Circuit Masking}
\label{sec:ablmask}
% BUDGET 1.5 pp. (The DAN-64 sweep varied the contrast source only, not lambda or the gamma weights; app:contrast-source-v1.)
% RESTRUCTURE 2026-10-11 (DAN-161 step 1): the attribution-baseline summary (was 4.2) moved to app:attribution.

Rather than thresholding attribution scores (the rank-and-cut baselines it is compared against, \cref{sec:attr-baselines}), WCM learns the circuit directly, adopting the optimisation framing of learned-mask circuit discovery~\citep{bhaskar2024finding, caples2025scaling} with a continuous-sparsification gate~\citep{savarese2020winning}, and moving its objective from an output to a single latent.
For every upstream site $s$ it attaches gate parameters $\theta_s \in \R^{d_{\mathrm{sae}}}$ with gates $m_s = \sigma(\theta_s)$, and coefficient parameters $\psi_s$ with $\alpha_s = \mathrm{softplus}(\psi_s)$.
A latent is kept at $\alpha$ times its live value when its gate is open and sent to its ablation value when closed:
\[
    x \;\leftarrow\; x + W_{\mathrm{dec}}^{s}\!\left[\, m_s \odot \alpha_s \odot c^{s} + (1 - m_s) \odot c^{s}_{\mathrm{abl}} - c^{s} \,\right],
\]
where $c^{s}$ is the site's dense Top-K code and $c^{s}_{\mathrm{abl}}$ its ablation value, a dense mean in training (the sparsity-preserving variant is used only in evaluation); editing only the decoded difference leaves the SAE error untouched, as in evaluation.

\paragraph{Objective.}
The circuit is trained to \emph{reproduce} the target's pre-activation at the anchors of the training contexts, by mean squared error against its value on the unmodified stream.
The pre-activation is forced by Top-K: the activation is censored at zero, so a loss on it carries no gradient exactly where the mask must learn.
Reproducing the pre-activation does not, however, keep the target in its site's Top-K: the loss constrains the site's stream along one direction of $d_{\mathrm{model}}$, and a circuit can restore that projection while other latents at the site rise past the target.
A \emph{rank-keep} term therefore asks the target to hold its natural rank: with $r$ the target's rank among its site's other latents at an anchor in the unmodified model, capped at $K$, and $\tau^{(r)}_{\pi}$ the $r$-th largest of their pre-activations under the circuit,
\[
    \mathcal{R}_{\pi} = \operatorname{mean}\Bigl[\operatorname{relu}\bigl(\tau^{(r)}_{\pi} - \preact\bigr)^{2}\Bigr],
\]
which is zero whenever the target is at or above its natural rank, so it never pulls a circuit towards a state the unmodified model does not have.

\paragraph{Training under three ablation methods.}
A mask trained under one ablation method learns whatever that method rewards: trained only under mean ablation over contrast contexts, its zero-ablation faithfulness is $0.0$ at depth (it learns the difference from the contrast-context mean, never a set that suffices alone), and trained only under zero ablation, it is never asked what separates firing from a near-identical context that does not fire.
WCM therefore scores one mask under all three ablation methods at once, with gradients accumulating into the same parameters:
\[
    \mathcal{L}
    = \frac{\sum_{\pi} \gamma_{\pi} \bigl(\mathcal{L}_{\pi} + \gamma_{\mathrm{R}}\, \mathcal{R}_{\pi}\bigr)}{\nu}
    \;+\; \lambda \sum_{s}\sum_{i} \sigma(\theta_{s,i}),
    \qquad
    \nu = \operatorname{mean}\bigl(\text{target}^2\bigr),
\]
summed over $\pi \in \{\mathrm{Z}, \mathrm{C}, \mathrm{A}\}$, with $\gamma_{\mathrm{Z}} = 1$, $\gamma_{\mathrm{C}} = \gamma_{\mathrm{A}} = 0.25$ at every layer, and $\gamma_{\mathrm{R}} = 3 \times 10^{-3}$; the ablation means are computed on training-split contexts only (\cref{app:protocol}).
% PROTOCOL v1 (2026-09-24, DAN-7): rank-keep gamma_R = 3e-3. Local sweep on 16 targets
% (experiments/055, 056): 3e-3 to 1e-1 all give 7-8/16 passing faithfulness under
% Z, A and C (plain WCM 6/16) at ~330-350 nodes (plain 398). It does NOT reliably
% remove amplifier circuits; specificity is reported, not claimed.
All terms measure the same quantity on the same target, so one shared, bounded normaliser suffices; the sparsity penalty is flat per latent, and the calibration of the three-method design is in \cref{app:membership-studies}.
% RESTRUCTURE 2026-10-12 (DAN-161 step 5): the pre-v1 calibration sentences (A term halves layer-2 node sets;
% Jaccard 0.13 vs 0.54) moved to app:single-method; claim 15 of the claims map needs a v1 rerun before the main text.

\paragraph{Scaling coefficients.}
Coefficients start at $\alpha = 1$, so training starts exactly at unweighted behaviour; \emph{unweighted circuit masking} keeps them there.
One guard is required: with coefficients free, the optimiser can hold a gate just below the keep threshold while inflating its coefficient, emptying the node set while keeping function intact, so latents with closed gates are charged $\lambda\,(1 - \sigma(\theta))\,|\alpha - 1|$.
The zero-ablation term anchors the coefficients: trained without it, they calibrate against the mean ablation's background and overshoot by one to three orders of magnitude once that background is removed (pre-v1; \cref{app:single-method}).

\paragraph{Binarisation.}
Evaluation scores the hard node set $\{i : m_i > \tfrac{1}{2}\}$, so the gates are annealed during training, $m = \sigma(\theta / T)$ with $T$ decaying geometrically from $1$ to $0.05$ (continuous sparsification;~\citealp{savarese2020winning}), and the training forward converges to the binary gates that evaluation scores; the gates are optimised directly rather than sampled~\citep{louizos2018learning, li2024optimal}.
Training runs 400 AdamW steps; all settings are in \cref{app:protocol}, and the binarisation study in \cref{app:membership-studies}.

\subsection{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}
% BUDGET 0.75 pp; settings live in app:protocol.
\label{sec:eval-abl}

% RESTRUCTURE 2026-10-12 (DAN-161 step 5): the evaluation-family table moved to app:protocol (after tab:protocol);
% the necessity details, near-threshold and specificity definitions now live in its rows.
Every reported score is read on held-out contexts and reads the target's activation (after Top-K) in every term of its ratio; the full evaluation family and its settings are in \cref{tab:semantics,tab:protocol}.

\paragraph{Intervention scores.}
% PROTOCOL v1 (2026-09-22, DAN-7): necessity uses the SFC-completeness form
% below, on every model, replacing the earlier (A+ - A+_int)/(A+ - A-) form.
% Under a post-Top-K read a_empty is usually 0 and the ratio reduces to
% 1 - a/a_pos, which reconciles the engine and the standalone ports (DAN-65).
Let $A^{+}$ and $A^{-}$ be the target's mean activation on activating and contrast contexts.
\textbf{Sufficiency to induce}, $\phiind = (A^{-}_{\mathrm{int}} - A^{-})/(A^{+} - A^{-})$, sets the circuit's nodes in contrast contexts to their mean values at the anchors of the activating contexts, times $\alpha$ for a weighted circuit ($\phiind^{\alpha}$, the form reported throughout): near 1, the circuit switches the target on.
\textbf{Necessity} is one minus the completeness score of \citet{marks2025sparse}, with SAE errors preserved and the nodes zero-ablated: with $a_{M \setminus C}$ the target's activation on activating contexts when the circuit's nodes are set to zero and every other latent is recomputed,
\[
    \phisup(C) = \frac{a_{\mathrm{pos}} - a_{M \setminus C}}{a_{\mathrm{pos}} - a_{\varnothing}}.
\]
% 2026-09-22 (DAN-72): no per-node role labels in WCM. Leave-one-out effects are
% strongly non-additive and no signed subset passes a set-level test, so
% necessity and sufficiency to induce treat the circuit as one set.
Both scores intervene on the circuit as one set.
All scores are unclipped ratios: success means landing near $1$, and a score far above $1$ is overshoot, not quality.

\paragraph{Ablation methods.}
The choice of ablation is load-bearing~\citep{li2024optimal, miller2024transformer}.
Mean ablation over activating contexts leaks target signal into the ablation value increasingly with depth ($a_{\varnothing}$ up to $30\%$ of $a_{\mathrm{pos}}$; \cref{app:floors}), while mean ablation over contrast contexts does not; dense means put the model on streams a Top-K SAE can never emit, so evaluation re-imposes the per-position Top-K after applying them (sparsity-preserving; \cref{app:respect-topk}).

\paragraph{Random-circuit baselines.}
% 2026-10-24 (paper prep, DAN-181; design in experiments/084-harder-nulls/README.md, decisions 2026-10-24): the two
% harder nulls added; the first null is DAN-17 (random_null.py).
Fitted coefficients could make any set look faithful, so every random circuit, matched to the weighted circuit's size site by site, receives the \emph{same coefficient fitting} on its own nodes before scoring.
Its members are latents active on the target's strongest training contexts or, in two harder nulls, latents active near the anchor and matched on frequency or contribution (\cref{app:nulls}).

\paragraph{Specificity and near-threshold targets.}
Every score reads the target alone, so a circuit that restores the target by lifting its whole neighbourhood at the site passes them all; we report specificity (the target's rank, the latents lifted above the Top-K cut, and the faithfulness of co-firing \emph{sibling} latents) as a measured property, not a criterion (\cref{app:specificity}).
A \emph{near-threshold} target, whose median rank at the held-out anchors of the unmodified model is at least $K/2$, has an ill-conditioned activation read, since small shifts near the cut push it out of the Top-K; such targets stay in the headline pass rate and are also reported as a stratum.

\paragraph{Pass criterion.}
% DAN-8, 2026-09-27. experiments/062-h100-protocol-v1/pass_rule.py applies it.
A circuit passes if, on the held-out strongest contexts and the activation read, its faithfulness under all three ablation methods (Z, and sparsity-preserving A and C) lies in $[0.8, 1.5]$ and its necessity is at least $0.9$.
The band is asymmetric by design: below $0.8$ the circuit fails to restore its target, while an overshoot up to $1.5$ still restores it, and whether that overshoot is specific to the target is measured for every circuit.
Sufficiency to induce, specificity, and the random-circuit baseline are reported rather than required; targets with a vacuous zero-ablation denominator are excluded and counted, and an undefined A or C ratio counts as a fail (\cref{app:protocol}).

\paragraph{Circuit size is a measurement at a stated scaling convention.}
At natural scale a latent's circuit is large for deep targets (\cref{sec:weighted}), and in (pre-v1) attribution node sets every latent is individually removable at the noise level while bulk removal craters the target (\cref{sec:drivers-closure}).
A latent's activation, reached through an additive residual stream, is a dense function of many small contributions, so any rule that thresholds individual contributions undercounts it however the threshold is tuned.
We interpret most of that collective tail as redundancy that fewer, rescaled latents can carry, since fitted coefficients remove it (\cref{fig:budget}); a circuit's size is therefore meaningful only alongside whether its coefficients were fitted.

\paragraph{Ablation methods are part of the method.}
\citet{miller2024transformer} showed that faithfulness is not robust to the choice of ablation; our measurements push the point into training.

% hand-picked and size-confounded (app:single-method now says so). Replaced by the v1 contrast-source result.
The home-turf effect (\cref{sec:method-comparison}), the contrast contexts behind the C ablation changing how far a circuit generalises (\cref{app:contrast-source-v1}), the miscalibration of coefficients trained without zero ablation (\cref{sec:ablmask}), and circuits that fail a resample test until trained against one (\cref{app:resample}) all say the same thing: the ablation methods used for discovery, training, and evaluation belong in the method statement.
Equivalence classes of node sets, and composing circuits into chains, are discussed in \cref{app:underdetermination}.

\paragraph{Node sets are samples; function is stable.}
% RESTRUCTURE 2026-10-12 (DAN-161 step 4): the non-identifiability subsection moved to app:underdetermination.
Refitting a target with its training contexts in a different order, on a different GPU, gives a circuit that is about a fifth smaller, passes about as often, and shares a median $64\%$ of the original's latents, and the shared core alone passes on $48$ of the $56$ targets where both fits pass (\cref{app:underdetermination}); we therefore treat sizes and functional scores, not individual memberships, as properties of the target.

\paragraph{Recommendations.}
% DAN-151 / DAN-161 step 8 (2026-10-12): conditional advice collected from the results, AtP*-style.
For work that discovers circuits for SAE latents, our results suggest five practices.
Report faithfulness under more than one ablation method, and name the methods used for discovery, training, and evaluation, since rankings change between them (\cref{sec:method-comparison}).
Compare circuit sizes only at matched faithfulness, and state whether coefficients were fitted.
Report specificity alongside faithfulness, since a faithful circuit can be an amplifier.
Report sizes, scores, and stable cores rather than a single membership, and expect bitwise reproducibility only on one GPU type (\cref{app:underdetermination}).

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
% DAN-151 (2026-10-12): bold run-in items, one sentence each where possible; attention, regime and budget added.

\textbf{The SAE basis is not ground truth.}
Many latents resist clean characterisation, and circuits built from ambiguous latents inherit that ambiguity; our scores measure intervention effects, not interpretability.

\textbf{The objective is a surrogate.}
WCM trains on the pre-activation, a continuous surrogate for a discrete Top-K event, and evaluation scores the activation itself; for near-threshold targets the gap is partly a scoring effect: in a sample their clean margin above the first excluded latent is $25\%$ (against $81$--$91\%$ for other targets), and under zero ablation their circuits reproduce the pre-activation (median $0.93$) yet read $0$ once the target slips out of the Top-K, though under the mean ablations they fall short on the pre-activation too (\cref{app:production}).

% DAN-131 tests 1-3 (2026-10-03): 062 freeze_attn.py (out_freeze/), out_attn/, attn_heads.py (out_heads/);
% ~50% of attention targets pass at lambda 2.5e-5 (~9k latents), out_bands + out_depth.
\textbf{Attention-output targets pass least ($14\%$).}
An attention latent draws most of its signal from other token positions, while a circuit keeps one latent set at every position, so it must preserve the content of each source position; under mean ablation the failing circuits lose both where the dominant head attends and what it reads, and only larger circuits help (in a 22-target sample, about half pass at ${\sim}9$k latents; \cref{app:production}).
Position-specific membership, or a separate latent set for the source positions, may lower that cost; we have not tested either.

\textbf{Circuits are fitted to one regime and one budget.}
Circuits fitted mostly to strong contexts transfer only in part to weaker ones ($12\%$ pass on held-out mid-band contexts), and at the 400-step budget most are still shrinking, so pass rates and sizes describe a stated operating point (\cref{app:production}, \cref{fig:training-curves}).

\textbf{Faithfulness holds under the ablations a circuit was trained against.}
When everything outside the circuit takes its value on another real context, as in causal scrubbing, $0.1\%$ of circuits pass; training against such backgrounds raises this to $30\%$ on $99$ targets (\cref{app:resample}).

\textbf{Faithful at the anchor, not at every position.}
Read at every position, deep circuits under zero ablation also fire on most contrast-context positions where the target is silent ($74\%$ at layers 8--11), so a circuit explains the target where it fires rather than simulating it everywhere (\cref{app:resample}).

\textbf{Faithfulness does not imply specificity.}
A circuit can restore its target by lifting its whole neighbourhood; the rank-keep term limits the lifts that overtake the target but does not remove such circuits ($30\%$ are flagged), so specificity is measured rather than claimed.

% APPENDIX CUTS 2026-10-12 (tier 3, Daniel 1a): the wiring limitation was cut with the direct-effect-edges appendix.
\textbf{Contrast contexts.}
Contrast contexts are verified silent only in that the target stays out of its Top-K, and ``close'' is one of several defensible constructions; circuits trained on close contexts pass less often when evaluated on distant ones than circuits trained on random contexts (\cref{app:contrast-source-v1}).

\textbf{Generality.}
TuringLLM's synthetic corpus aids control but limits generality, which the two public models partly address; larger models remain open, and the method is scoped to Top-K dictionaries: on dense ReLU dictionaries it replicates, but a random-circuit baseline with fitted coefficients stops discriminating once a set spans the anchors (pre-v1, six targets; \cref{app:dense-relu}).

\section{Conclusion}
% BUDGET 0.25 pp.

% REWRITE 2026-10-12 (DAN-161 step 9): the two claims, then "so what?".
% 2026-10-24 (paper prep): 46% and "fail on all 747" are as run (DAN-157 re-score, DAN-181 harder nulls); update with
% the abstract (087 macros).
We asked what makes an internal latent fire, and answered with weighted circuit masking.
Two results carry the paper.
Circuits for arbitrary latents can be found at scale and tested strictly: at a penalty that yields $10^{2}$--$10^{3}$ upstream latents, $46\%$ of ${\sim}15{,}000$ randomly sampled TuringLLM latents get a circuit that is faithful under three ablations and necessary on held-out text, while random circuits of the same size and fitting fail on all $747$ targets tested.
And fitted coefficients are what keep those circuits small: without them, deep circuits are about $10\times$ larger, and half as many deep targets pass at any size we tried.
Because the targets are sampled rather than chosen, the results describe a model's internal variables as a population, not a few tasks we thought to ask about; and because a circuit's size depends on whether its coefficients were fitted, a claim that some computation is dense or sparse needs its scaling convention stated.
Circuits for single latents are a building block: composing them into chains, with propagation tested at every link, is the path from dependencies to a map of the model's internal computation.

\subsection{A Corpus Built by Design}

The synthetic corpus was constructed in two stages.
The first produced a relatively small but high-quality foundation: Phi-3 Mini~\citep{abdin2024phi3}, a 3.8B-parameter language model available under a permissive licence, generated instructional and educational text spanning 18 broad subject areas (Physics, Mathematics, Computer Science, Chemistry, Biology, Philosophy, English, Creative Writing, Languages, Art, Music, Engineering, Economics, Business, Geography, History, Culinary Arts, and Communication), recursively generating topics and sub-topics and producing structured documents of headings and text.

The second stage addressed scale.
A fine-tuned T5-based paraphrase model~\citep{vorobev2023paraphraser, raffel2020exploring} expanded the corpus by generating 11 paraphrased variants of each source document, motivated by knowledge distillation results~\citep{ba2014deep, hinton2015distilling} suggesting that varied outputs of a stronger teacher can be advantageous over raw data alone.
The final corpus totals approximately 2 billion tokens.

Two tokenized datasets were derived using the Phi-3 Mini tokenizer.
The language-modelling training set combines original and augmented texts shuffled into epochs, concluding with a second pass over only the high-quality originals to consolidate representations around the cleaner signal.
% 2026-10-23 (DAN-28): was "4,096 shards"; data/ holds shard_0 .. shard_3029 (3,030 files, contiguous).
The interpretability dataset consists of 64-token chunks shuffled into 3,030 shards, allowing the pipeline to process one shard at a time with a bounded memory footprint.

\subsection{Fitness for Interpretability}

TuringLLM is not designed to win benchmarks; it is designed to be a controlled subject.
Standard held-out benchmarks fit poorly for a base model trained on synthetic educational text, so fitness was evaluated qualitatively and through structured internal tests: Phi-3 Mini composed topic-specific opening sentences and scored TuringLLM's completions on English quality and topic adherence, yielding average grades of approximately B and A respectively across the 18 subject areas.
This level of competence supports the working hypothesis that the model contains structured internal representations worth studying; a model merely interpolating noise would not produce consistently topical completions.
Representative completions are shown in \cref{fig:completions}.

\subsection{Sparse Autoencoder Quality}
\label{app:sae-quality}

\Cref{fig:sae-quality} reports three standard quality metrics for the 36-SAE bank, per site.
\emph{Explained variance}, $1 - \operatorname{Var}(x - \hat{x}) / \operatorname{Var}(x)$, measures reconstruction fidelity.
\emph{Alive latents} is the fraction of each dictionary's 40,960 latents that fired at least once during the full collection pass over the interpretability corpus.
\emph{Loss recovered} splices each SAE's reconstruction into the model at its site and reports the recovered share of the cross-entropy gap between the original model and zero-ablating that site~\citep{marks2025sparse}, measured over 12{,}288 tokens of the interpretability corpus.
Because the SAEs are Top-K with $K = 128$, sparsity is fixed by construction and the usual $L_0$--fidelity trade-off curve is a single point per SAE.

Three observations matter for interpreting the paper's results.
First, reconstruction fidelity is healthy across the bank: attention SAEs explain 0.93--0.99 of variance, residual SAEs decline smoothly with depth to 0.85, and MLP SAEs bottom out at 0.77 in layer 9.
Second, the layer-1 attention SAE has a dead-latent crater: only 37\% of its latents ever fire (84\% at layer 2, at least 93\% everywhere else).
Third, loss recovered exposes a functional gap that explained variance misses: the layer-11 attention SAE recovers only 55\% of the cross-entropy gap despite explaining 0.96 of variance (layer 10: 84\%), indicating that the unexplained residual at the final attention site is disproportionately important to the logits.

The production run first built its held-out sets by the 3:1 split of each stored pool, and an audit of every record found that they were not clean: $66.8\%$ of held-out strongest contexts repeated a training-side context, $35.6\%$ exactly, $50.2\%$ up to the anchor, and $55.1\%$ as a near-duplicate.
A context identical up to its anchor is the same input for every score, since each score reads the target at the anchor of a causal model.
The cause is the corpus construction: about half of all 64-token windows ($52.9\%$) were written to the interpretability corpus twice, the two copies have identical activations and so sit at adjacent ranks of a pool, and the split, which holds out one rank in every four, places most such pairs on both sides.
The mid-band held-out sets were nearly clean ($3.2\%$ of contexts identical up to the anchor, $0.4\%$ near-duplicates).

We therefore rebuilt every held-out set for the fixed circuits, under the held-out rule of \cref{tab:protocol}; no circuit was refitted, and the training contexts, ablation means and contrast training contexts are unchanged.
Under the rule a median of only $5$ of the $16$ original held-out strongest contexts survive, so a new scan of the corpus's $18.2$M distinct windows ranked each target's $512$ strongest contexts by the stores' own statistic, and the held-out strongest set keeps the surviving originals, then adds the scan's strongest admissible contexts until it holds $16$.
An admissible context must fire at its anchor, both when selected and when scored in the evaluation's own batch.
Held-out mid-band contexts go through the same check and are only removed; held-out contrast contexts are checked against the contrast training contexts and every activating context, must stay silent at every position, and are refilled from the close selector's next candidates.
Targets with fewer than $8$ admissible strongest contexts are scored and flagged rather than dropped: headline pass rates are over targets with at least $8$, and a worst case counts the rest as fails.

\label{app:attribution}
\label{sec:attr-baselines}

WCM is compared against attribution methods that score every upstream latent and keep the top of a ranking.
All of them run on each target's training contexts and score a latent's effect on the target's \emph{encoder pre-activation} at the anchor,
\[
    \preact(x, t)
    = \bigl\langle W_e^{(f)},\, z(x, t) \bigr\rangle + b_e^{(f)},
\]
with $W_e^{(f)}$ the target's encoder row, $b_e^{(f)}$ its effective bias, and $z(x, t)$ the pre-SAE dense activation: unlike the activation, which Top-K censors to zero, it is defined on every forward pass, and gradients reach every upstream latent through identity passthroughs at each SAE site~\citep{marks2025sparse}, with the SAE errors at their clean values.

\subsection{Estimators}
\label{sec:modes}
\label{sec:cfgrad}
\label{sec:abgrad}
\label{sec:hybrid}

The two gradient estimators share one path, from every upstream site's mean over the target's activating training contexts (its A ablation value, $\bar{c}^{s}$) to its natural code $c^{s}$, all sites interpolated together and held at their interpolated codes, and both multiply a gradient by $(c^{s} - \bar{c}^{s})$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Attribution patching} (AtP) takes the gradient at the clean point, giving the first-order estimate of mean-ablating each latent~\citep{nanda2023attribution, syed2023attribution, kramar2024atp}.
\item \textbf{Integrated gradients from the mean} (IG) averages the gradient over $10$ points of the path. It follows the $\mathrm{IE}_{\mathrm{ig}}$ node estimator of \citet{marks2025sparse}, the node-level counterpart of EAP-IG~\citep{hanna2024faith}, in form but not in detail: theirs interpolates one submodule at a time with the rest of the model live, starts from a zero patch when no counterfactual exists, and scores the error terms as nodes.
\end{itemize}
The two differ only in where they linearise, with target, contexts, path, and selection held fixed.
Two further baselines use them:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Hierarchical attribution} applies the gating algorithm of \citet{ge2024automatically} to our SAE sites. Without transcoders our graph is not linear, so its scores are first-order gradients rather than their exact linear attributions, aggregated over contexts rather than per prompt; its threshold is bisected towards the weighted circuit's size, and every bisection point is scored.
\item \textbf{Ranked members with fitted coefficients} fill the weighted circuit's per-site counts from the top of a ranking at each site, then fit coefficients exactly as for random circuits (\cref{sec:eval}: only the coefficients move, with no sparsity penalty), and are scored at $\alpha = 1$ and fitted. The rankings are IG, the latent's mean activation at the target's anchor over the training contexts (no gradient), and the correlation of that activation with the target's across contexts (co-activation). Against WCM, these rows separate what the selection contributes from what the coefficients contribute.
\end{itemize}
IG makes the attribution baselines comparable in kind to the published estimator, but not identical to it, so the output-targeted comparison of \cref{sec:sfc-boundary} uses its own reimplementation of the published recipe; a version of that recipe with the target latent as its metric, which would isolate the choice of target from everything else, is \pending{DAN-48}.

\subsection{Rankings and Node Sets}
\label{sec:membership}
% 2026-10-24 (paper prep, DAN-159): retitled (was "Node Sets and Position-Aware Discovery"); the three rankings are
% 083's max, agg (mean summed |attr|) and sfc (|mean over contexts of the summed signed attribution|).

Attribution returns a score for every latent at every context and position, so the baselines must decide how the scores form one ranking and which node set a ranking defines.
Three rankings collapse contexts and positions: a latent's largest $|$score$|$ at any context and position; the mean over contexts of its summed $|$score$|$ over positions; and the magnitude of the mean over contexts of its summed signed score, the aggregate ranking of prior work~\citep{marks2025sparse}.
A truncated baseline keeps the top $n$ latents of one ranking, with $n$ the target's weighted-circuit size.

At natural size the Top-K constraint shapes the node set: the code is $k$-sparse \emph{per position} while a target reads its whole causal prefix, so a position-collapsed circuit must cover the union of every position's active set, thousands of distinct latents per site, or recomputed faithfulness collapses.
The baselines' node set is therefore a set of latents, the same kind of object \labelcref{eq:p2} optimises over, collected across positions.
Latents outside the circuit are ablated everywhere; nodes are not forced to any value: they are recomputed from the modified stream at every position, so positions instantiate naturally (allow, don't force), exactly the circuit-only execution of \cref{sec:problem}.
Discovery is position-aware to match: attribution is computed per position, a percentile cut on $|$attribution$|$ (pooled over sites and positions, resolved per batch of four contexts) keeps individual (latent, position) entries, and the node set is the union over the positions of the target's causal prefix.
With a mean-ablation path nearly every entry is nonzero, so the median cut keeps about half of them, which is why these unions are large (the natural-size rows of \cref{tab:matrix}).
No template or cross-example alignment is required.
Both attribution signs are kept, as a design principle rather than an implementation detail: the node set is \emph{stream reconstruction}, not a selection of what drives the target, and a negative-attribution latent still carries part of the residual stream that nodes are recomputed from, so removing it corrupts recomputed execution.

A node set's size at a stated faithfulness is a measured quantity, and \cref{sec:drivers-closure} argues it measures the structure of the computation rather than a defect of the method.

\paragraph{Attribution-mass recovery (pre-v1).}

This appendix is pre-v1, and so are all of its numbers: it uses node sets from an earlier version of the attribution baselines (\cref{app:attribution}) at natural scale ($\alpha = 1$), on one target each at layers 2, 8, 9, and 10, under the evaluation that preceded protocol v1.
Leave-one-out is blind to how these node sets work: removing any single node changes the target by $0.000$, the noise level.
Yet cutting nodes in bulk by their own attribution ranking craters function: dropping the bottom half by score moves $\phi^{\mathrm{free}}_{\mathrm{Z}}$ from $0.975$ to $0.895$ at layer 2 but from $1.119$ to $0.595$ at layer 8; dropping the bottom 90\% yields $0.635$, $0.000$, $0.222$, and $0.005$ at layers 2, 8, 9, and 10.

Nodes individually at the noise level collectively carry the entire deep reconstruction.
Individual-contribution thresholding, the standard selection rule across circuit discovery, therefore undercounts what faithfulness needs, which is why circuit size is reported here as a measurement at a stated faithfulness.

\paragraph{Where the fall with depth comes from.}
Zero ablation and necessity both hold for three circuits in four or more at every depth ($79\%$, $80\%$, and $76\%$ at layers 0--4, 5--7, and 8--11; \cref{fig:failure-anatomy}); what grows with depth is the share that fail only under the two mean ablations, from $10\%$ of circuits at layers 0--4 to $53\%$ at 8--11 (over all layer-11 circuits, median A and C faithfulness are $0.64$ and $0.48$), and these make up $59\%$ of all failing circuits.
Attention outputs are the exception: $40\%$ of their circuits fail even zero ablation, and $63\%$ of those belong to near-threshold targets, which barely enter their site's Top-K in the unmodified model and read $0$ once they slip out of it ($1{,}142$ targets, $7.6\%$ of the run, almost all attention outputs; $0.8\%$ pass, against $50\%$ for the remaining targets).
Layer 0 departs from the trend ($55\%$): its circuits are faithful ($90\%$), but over a third ($37\%$) fail necessity, because the token embedding reaches layer-0 targets directly and is never ablated.

\paragraph{Why attention outputs fail.}

Three local tests on samples of production attention targets locate the cost in source positions.
About $85\%$ of an attention target's clean contribution comes from source positions other than its own and the first token, for passing and failing targets alike.
Failing targets read from more positions and more heads than passing ones at the same circuit size (layers 0--4: $4.5$ against $2.4$ effective positions, $7.1$ against $4.5$ effective heads).
Under activating-mean ablation the dominant head of a failing circuit keeps $0.42$ of its contribution when only its attention pattern is ablated and $0.60$ when only its values are (passing circuits: $1.04$ and $0.80$), so the circuit loses both where the head attends and what it reads.
Holding attention patterns at their clean values does not rescue them ($5$ of $120$ failing circuits), and raising the A and C weights or the rank-keep weight barely helps ($21\%$ to $29\%$ on $24$ targets); at a weaker penalty about half pass, at ${\sim}9$k latents ($10$ of $22$ attention targets at $\lambda = 2.5 \times 10^{-5}$).

\paragraph{What the coefficients do to members.}

The C term of WCM trains against the mean over contrast contexts, so the choice of contrast contexts could shape what a circuit learns.

Circuits trained against close contexts are the most faithful on close backgrounds and the least on any other (\cref{fig:contrast-source}).
Their median C faithfulness falls from $0.90$ on close to $0.58$ on distant backgrounds, and on distant backgrounds they pass $0.27$, against $0.37$ and $0.38$ for circuits trained on random or distant contexts (paired differences $+0.099$ $[0.052, 0.149]$ and $+0.106$ $[0.065, 0.152]$, 95\% bootstrap intervals over targets).
Averaged over the three backgrounds, distant-trained circuits pass more often than close-trained ones ($+0.046$ $[0.014, 0.082]$), random-trained ones by a similar margin that the interval does not resolve ($+0.035$ $[-0.002, 0.074]$), and random and distant do not differ ($-0.011$ $[-0.045, 0.023]$).
The source acts mainly on the C term: zero-ablation faithfulness and necessity are the same across sources (activating-mean faithfulness is somewhat higher for close-trained circuits, median $1.00$ against $0.92$--$0.94$), and for each source the share of circuits that pass or fail only under C is the same on every background ($0.48$ to $0.51$), so a different background only moves circuits between passing and failing on C.

\paragraph{Why close contexts generalise worst.}
Variety alone does not explain it.
Fused contexts span the widest range of similarity to the target's activating contexts (10th to 90th percentile $0.25$ to $0.87$, against $0.82$ to $0.90$ for close, $0.47$ to $0.77$ for random, and $0.19$ to $0.27$ for distant), yet fused-trained circuits do no better than random-trained ones ($-0.019$ $[-0.054, 0.017]$).
What close contexts have that the others lack is the target's topic: they sit nearest the activating contexts and nearest to firing (median pre-activation $3\%$ of the activating level, against $0$ for random and distant).
A circuit trained against their mean keeps the topic's features in its background and learns only what separates the target from near misses; against a background without those features it falls short.
The same circuits, re-scored with the C mean taken from the nearest, middle or farthest third of each target's random set, show this within a single source: close-trained circuits pass $0.40$, $0.40$ and $0.33$ as the background moves away from the target's topic (nearest minus farthest $+0.068$ $[0.039, 0.100]$), while random-trained circuits pass $0.43$, $0.44$ and $0.41$ ($+0.014$ $[-0.007, 0.036]$).

The production run uses close contrast contexts, and the public-model runs keep them so that the model is the only change between models.
For new work, random or distant contrast contexts give circuits whose contrast faithfulness depends less on the background they are evaluated against.

\section{Specificity}
\label{app:specificity}
% experiments/056-specificity (2026-09-23/24). Pilot numbers: 16 targets.

Faithfulness, necessity, and the random-circuit baseline all read the target alone.
A circuit can therefore restore its target by lifting the target's whole neighbourhood, its concept, topic, or context type, and pass all three: a concept amplifier rather than a mechanism for that latent.
The loss permits it, since reproducing the target's pre-activation constrains the site's stream along one direction of $d_{\mathrm{model}}$, and it leaks into the activation read only when the lifted latents overtake the target in its site's Top-K.

The diagnostic reads the whole code at the target's site, every latent's pre-activation and the SAE's Top-K set at the anchor, in the unmodified model, the empty circuit, and the fitted circuit under each ablation method, on the held-out contexts.
It reports the target's rank and Top-K membership; the number of latents lifted above the unmodified model's Top-K cut, against the empty circuit; the overlap of the circuit's Top-K set with the unmodified one; and the faithfulness of \emph{siblings}, latents in the unmodified Top-K at the anchor on at least half of the target's strongest training contexts, computed exactly as the target's, against a control group of the site's other active latents.
A circuit is flagged as an amplifier when, under at least one ablation method and on the pre-activation read, its target is restored to at least $0.5$ and its median sibling to at least $80\%$ of the target's faithfulness.

% its rank-keep sentence is kept.

\section{Resample Ablation and Reads at Every Position}
\label{app:resample}
% 2026-10-23 (Daniel). Data: experiments/073-strict-eval (out/report.md: 747 targets; out/diag.jsonl: 45-target
% diagnostic, C donors, pinned medians by kind) and experiments/079-resample-training (out_silent_v2/report.md: the
% 27-target pilot, paired against W0 = the production configuration refitted on the same GPU; out_confirm/: the
% 99-target confirmation with arm RS0.25_S0.25, chosen on the pilot before that run).

All three of our ablation methods replace a removed latent with zero or a mean.
Causal scrubbing~\citep{chan2022causal} sets a stricter test: everything outside the circuit takes its value on another real context.
We scored the production circuits of the $747$ targets that have a random-circuit baseline this way.
At each position a removed latent takes a donor context's sparse code (so the donor's sparsity is kept), with donors drawn from the target's activating training contexts (A), its contrast contexts (C), or random corpus text (R); the scores and the pass rule are otherwise unchanged.
With A and C donors, $0.1\%$ of circuits pass, against $43.1\%$ under the production test, with median faithfulness $0.18$ (A), $0.04$ (C), and $0.01$ (R); random circuits pass on none.

The failure is one of closure, not of membership, for residual-stream targets.
In a 45-target diagnostic with C donors, holding the members at their own values in the base context restores residual-stream targets to a median $0.92$ (MLP $0.39$, attention $0.11$), while members recomputed from the mixed stream do not; the node set carries the signal, but the members lose the inputs they receive from outside the circuit.
The coefficients show the same dependence: with the base context as its own donor, so that nothing is ablated, residual-stream circuits overshoot to a median $2.16$, because coefficients fitted to stand in for an ablated complement over-drive the target when the complement is present.

Nothing in training asks a circuit to survive a real background, so we added one: a fourth ablation method whose replacement values are donor codes, drawn afresh at every step from contrast and random corpus contexts disjoint from the evaluation donors.
In a 27-target pilot, paired against the production configuration refitted on the same hardware, it raises the resample pass rate from $0\%$ to $33\%$ and the production pass rate from $37\%$ to $52\%$, at $1.15\times$ the circuit size.
Its side effect is that the target now also fires at nearby positions of the activating passage where it is naturally silent; a penalty on lifts at those positions roughly halves the effect, and with both terms at weight $0.25$ the resample pass rate is $37\%$, the production pass rate $59\%$, and the size $1.35\times$.
% 079 out_confirm/report.md (2026-10-23): 99 targets disjoint from the pilot, arm RS0.25_S0.25 fixed before the run.
With that configuration fixed in advance, $99$ further targets (11 per depth band and site kind) confirm the pilot: the resample pass rate rises from $0\%$ to $30\%$ ($39\%$, $36\%$, and $15\%$ by depth band), the production pass rate from $44\%$ to $57\%$, and the size by $1.22\times$, while the correlation with the target at every position stays where it was ($0.77$ against $0.79$).
Deep targets gain least on the resample test but most on the production test ($21\%$ to $45\%$).

% 2026-10-24 (Daniel): exp 074 disclosed. Data: experiments/073-strict-eval/out/report.md, "074 - all-position
% simulation" (747 targets; 16 held-out activating + 16 held-out contrast contexts; medians). The activating contexts
% are the as-run held-out sets; re-score on the rebuilt sets (DAN-157) and update these numbers. Leakage can only
% flatter the activating-context reads, and the contrast-context reads are unaffected.
Every score above, like the pass rule, reads the target at the anchor.
Read instead at every position of $16$ held-out activating and $16$ held-out contrast contexts, the circuits track the target only in part.
Under zero ablation the median correlation with the target's activation is $0.59$ ($0.75$, $0.58$, and $0.50$ by depth band), and beyond layer 4 the variance explained is negative ($-1.06$ at layers 5--7, $-2.14$ at layers 8--11).
The cause is firing where the target is silent: on $31\%$ and $74\%$ of contrast-context positions in those bands, against $1\%$ at layers 0--4.
Under the contrast-mean ablation the reads are closer (median correlation $0.66$; $3\%$ of contrast-context positions), and passing circuits track the target far better than failing ones (correlation $0.88$ against $0.35$; variance explained $0.46$ against $-0.25$).
A circuit is thus a mechanism for the target's activation where the target fires, not a simulation of the target at every position, and deep circuits under zero ablation overfire away from the anchor.

Faithfulness is therefore relative to the ablation methods a circuit is trained and tested against, and robustness to a stricter test can be trained for by giving training the backgrounds the circuit must survive.
Every result in the main text uses the three methods of protocol v1.

\label{app:related}

\paragraph{Discovery methods and their settings.}
Each family of discovery methods is built for its own setting.
ACDC~\citep{conmy2023towards} removes edges of a model's computational graph one at a time while an output metric holds, and Edge Pruning~\citep{bhaskar2024finding} optimises an edge mask by gradient descent so that the pruned model stays faithful to the full model's predictions; both work over components such as heads and MLPs, and both are anchored at an output rather than a latent.
Attribution patching~\citep{nanda2023attribution, syed2023attribution} replaces each activation patch with a first-order estimate, AtP*~\citep{kramar2024atp} adds two changes that address the two classes of failure behind its false negatives, and EAP-IG~\citep{hanna2024faith} scores edges by integrating gradients along a path from corrupted to clean input embeddings.
Our path-attribution baseline adapts this estimator to nodes, integrating jointly over the upstream SAE sites from their mean-ablation values (\cref{sec:attr-baselines}).
Transcoders~\citep{dunefsky2024transcoders} replace MLPs with sparse, wider layers whose circuits factorise into input-dependent and input-invariant terms, attribution graphs~\citep{ameisen2025circuit, lindsey2025biology} build one graph per prompt over cross-layer transcoder features, and PIE~\citep{chen2026pie} prunes cross-layer transcoder features by their gradient-weighted write contributions before interpreting them.
The transcoder-based methods need a substrate our SAE bank does not provide, and produce a graph per prompt where we fit one circuit over many contexts, so we do not run them here.

\paragraph{Masks, scalars, and ablations.}
Learned masks for circuit discovery descend from subnetwork probing~\citep{cao2021low}, applied to computational-graph vertices by \citet{conmy2023towards} and to edges by Edge Pruning~\citep{bhaskar2024finding} and by uniform gradient sampling with optimal ablation~\citep{li2024optimal}, on $L_0$-gate and continuous-sparsification machinery~\citep{louizos2018learning, savarese2020winning}.
Masks over SAE latents have been learned to localise attributes~\citep{chaudhary2024evaluating}, a procedure adopted by benchmarks~\citep{mueller2025mib, karvonen2025saebench}, and to find behavioural circuits by continuous sparsification~\citep{caples2025scaling}; with its coefficients fixed at 1, its objective moved to a single latent, and one mask shared across positions, the latter is our unweighted circuit masking.
Every such mask keeps a node at its natural scale or ablates it.
The continuous coefficients of \citet{li2024optimal} interpolate between the two and play the role of our gates; what they learn besides is a constant for each \emph{removed} component, the dual of our scale for each kept one.
That constant minimises the ablated model's expected loss, so a component's importance is what it carries beyond the best constant; we keep the three ablations fixed and defined by data (zero, and the means over activating and over contrast contexts), so that what absence means is set before training rather than fitted with the circuit.

Learned per-unit scales appear with other jobs: scales that double as the pruning criterion~\citep{liu2017learning, huang2018data, you2019gate}; scales for the units kept after pruning, fitted after the mask is fixed~\citep{kwon2022fast, he2017channel}; steering multipliers~\citep{stoehr2024activation}; sparse fitted weights on SAE latents for a task vector~\citep{kharlapenko2025scaling}; regression weights on feature-to-feature edges~\citep{yin2026scalable}; and manual rescaling of the nodes of a pruned circuit~\citep{gao2025weightsparse}.
Gate-times-magnitude parametrisations separate membership from strength in sparse training and fine-tuning~\citep{louizos2018learning, guo2021parameter, lai2025joint}, where a closed gate means no change or zero rather than an ablated node.
Feature-preserving subnetworks have also been pruned by saliency in vision models~\citep{hamblin2022pruning}.

Circuits are routinely evaluated under several ablations~\citep{miller2024transformer, mueller2025mib} but discovered under one.
The nearest objectives sum the clean-to-corrupted and corrupted-to-clean directions of one counterfactual~\citep{chen2025rethinking}, or certify a circuit against every patch value in a bounded set~\citep{hadad2026formal}.
% 2026-10-23 (Daniel): the sentence reading the three-ablation objective as resample ablation was cut; exp 073 contradicts
% it (app:resample: 0.1% of circuits pass a resample test).
Training under several ablations is the circuit-level counterpart of attribution averaged over several baselines~\citep{kapishnikov2019xrai, erion2021improving}; it is not resample ablation, which our circuits mostly fail (\cref{app:resample}).

\paragraph{Nulls, variance, and non-uniqueness.}
Random circuits as a null follow \citet{shi2024hypothesis}, who test whether a candidate is more faithful than about a hundred random circuits of at least its size, sampled as paths through the model, and CircuitKIT~\citep{seth2026circuitkit}, which reports a $z$-score against random subgraphs of equal size.
In both, the random circuits are sampled and ablated but not fitted, which matches circuits that are not fitted either.
Our circuits fit a coefficient per node, and fitting alone might rescale an arbitrary set into reproducing a target, so our random circuits receive the same coefficient fitting before scoring (\cref{sec:eval}); with every coefficient at 1 they reduce to the nothing-fitted null.
The broader concern is the one \citet{meloux2025dead} raise: interpretability methods produce plausible explanations even for randomly initialised networks, so findings are better treated as statistical estimates tested against alternative hypotheses.
Causal scrubbing~\citep{chan2022causal} is a stricter test in this spirit, resampling everything outside the hypothesis from other inputs (\cref{app:resample}).

Circuit discovery is also variable.
EAP-IG circuits vary with the data batch, the prompt template, and the example~\citep{wu2026demystifying}; on one task, defensible analytic choices change the derived claim for most pairs of specifications~\citep{mahale2026explanation}; and Certified Circuits stabilise membership by randomised subsampling of the data, keeping only components included consistently~\citep{anani2026certified}.
On small networks where every circuit can be enumerated, many circuits reproduce the same behaviour exactly~\citep{meloux2025everything}, and in language models structurally distinct, low-overlap circuits support the same task~\citep{chen2026rome}, with circuits for one task differing across its inputs~\citep{makou2026many}.
Our node sets are non-identifiable in the same way, which is why a circuit's function, not its exact member list, is what we report as the finding (\cref{app:underdetermination}).

\paragraph{Self-repair and inhibition.}
Ablation-based tests meet compensation: when one attention layer is ablated, later layers make up part of its effect~\citep{mcgrath2023hydra}; the self-repair of single heads persists over the full training distribution, partial and variable across prompts, carried in part by the final LayerNorm scale and by sparse anti-erasure neurons~\citep{rushing2024explorations}; and copy-suppression heads, which lower the logits of tokens that earlier layers would copy from context, account for part of it~\citep{mcdougall2024copy}.
Conditional co-ablation finds backup components that become important only once a primary set is removed~\citep{gong2026conditional}.
Our scores intervene on the circuit as one set (\cref{sec:eval}), so they measure what the set does with any compensation inside the model included; per-member roles, where backups matter most, are not claimed.

\paragraph{Position-aware circuits.}
Position handling divides prior work into three strategies, each right for its setting: position-indexed nodes on templatic data and scores summed over positions otherwise~\citep{marks2025sparse}, per-instance graphs built one prompt at a time~\citep{ameisen2025circuit}, and schema-aligned positions across variable-length examples~\citep{haklay2025position}.
WCM needs none of these commitments: one mask is shared across positions and nodes are recomputed at every position, which suits the setting none of the three targets, one reusable circuit over arbitrary non-templatic text; our attribution baselines instead select per position and take the union over the causal prefix (\cref{sec:membership}).

\paragraph{Contrast contexts.}
Retrieved non-activating examples appear in automated interpretability for explanation scoring~\citep{paulo2024automatically}; \citet{paulo2025evaluating} find evaluations collapse when negatives come from neighbouring features, convergent with our finding that close contrast contexts generalise worst to other backgrounds (\cref{app:contrast-source}); and \citet{oneill2024sparse} design corrupted negatives for task-level circuit identification.
We use retrieved contrast contexts for causal discovery rather than explanation scoring, and treat their construction as a controlled variable with measured consequences.

\paragraph{The SAE basis.}
Static structure among latents is well documented: co-occurrence and decoder geometry reveal modularity~\citep{li2025geometry}, coactivation-derived components steer behaviour~\citep{deng2026coactivation}, meta-decompositions show latents are not atomic~\citep{leask2025canonical}, cross-layer feature-flow graphs track latents through the forward pass~\citep{laptev2025feature}, though decoder similarity misses most causally strong feature interactions~\citep{droste2026decoder}, and Jacobian SAEs train adjacent dictionaries for sparse computation between them~\citep{farnik2025jacobian}.
But that structure is correlational, geometric, or weight-based, where the relationships discovered here are context-conditioned and intervention-tested.
Weight-based connections need that test: virtual weights between interpretable components include \emph{interference weights}, large connections between components that never interact on the data distribution~\citep{turner2026interference}.
Intervention-based latent graphs have been mapped at scale in single-cell foundation models, by ablating one SAE feature at a time and measuring the change in downstream features, finding inhibitory edges dominant~\citep{kendiukhov2026causal} and a heavy-tailed hub structure with redundancy that grows with interaction order~\citep{kendiukhov2026exhaustive}; those graphs measure the effect of one feature at a time, where our circuits are fitted jointly for each target and tested as a set.
The basis itself is imperfect: reconstruction errors are pathological~\citep{gurnee2024pathological}, although much of the error is linearly predictable from the activation~\citep{engels2025dark}; SAEs trained from different seeds learn largely different features~\citep{paulo2025sparse}; downstream utility is mixed~\citep{smith2025negative}; and units are not canonical~\citep{leask2025canonical}.
Interventions on the basis are imperfect too: a behaviour suppressed by clamping SAE features can be recovered through the reconstruction residual while the features stay clamped~\citep{cui2026sae}; the effect of ablating single-token features on their token's logit differs more across SAE families than across model scales~\citep{cho2026single}; and dictionaries trained only on what an affine map from an earlier layer cannot explain recover more of the loss when several layers are replaced at once~\citep{poduval2026resae}.
This conditions our claims: reconstruction residuals stay as explicit anchors, evaluation intervenes on measured activations, and the SAE basis is treated as a useful but imperfect sample of the model's internal variables.

A discovered node set is one sample from an equivalence class of near-optimal solutions to \labelcref{eq:p2}, so we report node sets as equivalence classes and let set-level and functional claims carry the conclusions.

\paragraph{A stable core at scale.}
To measure this on the production run, we refitted 175 of its targets (5 per layer and site kind) under protocol v1 with one change: the training contexts visited in a different fixed order.
The refit ran on a different GPU from the production fit, and a refit is bitwise reproducible on the same GPU type but not across types, so the two fits differ by the reordering and by float-level differences in the hardware.
The \emph{core} of a target is the set of latents in both circuits; its coefficients are refitted on the core alone, as for the random baseline, and it is scored with the production scorer.
\Cref{tab:stable-core} gives the result.
The two fits share a median $64\%$ of the original circuit's latents (Jaccard $0.54$), less at depth.
The second fit is smaller (median $402$ against $492$ latents; smaller for $160$ of the $175$ targets) but passes about as often as the first ($40\%$ against $43\%$), and where both fits pass, the core alone passes on $48$ of $56$ targets, and its median necessity is $1.0$ in every depth band.
Which latents a circuit contains is therefore partly an accident of optimisation, while a core of about two thirds of it carries the function.

\paragraph{From circuits to chains.}
\label{app:discussion}
A weighted circuit is one hop; because every circuit is indexed by its target, circuits can in principle be composed by splicing the circuit for an upstream latent into the circuits that use it and re-validating by intervening at the earliest nodes.
Non-identifiability sets the boundary condition: each link is one sample from an equivalence class, so composition must manage classes of node sets, not single ones.

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Weighted circuit masking, WCM[Z+C+A] with rank-keep (one target $f$; protocol v1, \cref{tab:protocol})}
\label{alg:ablmask}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require target $f$; activating contexts $\posset$ (train on 32 strongest + 16 mid-band; hold out 16 + 16) and contrast contexts $\negset$ (48 train / 16 held out); $\lambda = 10^{-3}$; $\gamma_{\mathrm{C}} = \gamma_{\mathrm{A}} = 0.25$; $\gamma_{\mathrm{R}} = 3 \times 10^{-3}$; steps $S = 400$
\State target $\gets$ natural pre-activation of $f$ at the training anchors (no-grad forward);\quad $\nu \gets \operatorname{mean}(\text{target}^2)$
\State $r \gets \min(K,\ $rank of $f$ among its site's other latents$)$ at each training anchor, unmodified model
\State ablation values: C $\gets$ dense site means over the \emph{training} contrast contexts; A $\gets$ over the \emph{training} activating contexts
\State initialise $\theta_s = 4$, $\psi_s = \mathrm{softplus}^{-1}(1)$ for every upstream site $s$
\For{step $= 0, \ldots, S - 1$}
    \State $T \gets 0.05^{\,\text{step}/(S - 1)}$; \quad gates $m_s \gets \sigma(\theta_s / T)$; \quad coefficients $\alpha_s \gets \mathrm{softplus}(\psi_s)$ \Comment{geometric anneal $1 \rightarrow 0.05$}
    \For{$\pi \in \{\mathrm{Z}, \mathrm{C}, \mathrm{A}\}$} \Comment{same $\theta, \psi$ under all three ablation methods}
        \State forward with kept latents at $\alpha \times$ live value, closed latents at their $\pi$ ablation value
        \State $\mathcal{L}_{\pi} \gets$ MSE of $f$'s pre-activation against target; \quad $\mathcal{R}_{\pi} \gets \operatorname{mean}\,\operatorname{relu}(\tau^{(r)}_{\pi} - \preact)^2$
    \EndFor
    \State AdamW step on $\sum_{\pi} \gamma_{\pi}(\mathcal{L}_{\pi} + \gamma_{\mathrm{R}}\mathcal{R}_{\pi})/\nu + \lambda \sum_{s,i} \bigl[\sigma(\theta_{s,i}) + (1 - \sigma(\theta_{s,i}))\,|\alpha_{s,i} - 1|\bigr]$ \Comment{penalties use $\sigma(\theta)$, not $\sigma(\theta / T)$}
\EndFor
\State circuit $\gets \{\,(i, \alpha_i) : m_i > \tfrac{1}{2}\,\}$; evaluate on the held-out contexts under \cref{sec:eval-abl}
\State \Return circuit
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Context Stores and Contrast Contexts}
\label{app:stores}

\subsection{Context Store Formalism}

The collection pass assigns each latent a scored context set
$\mathcal{C}_f = \{((x,t), a_f(x,t)) : a_f(x,t) > 0\}$.
The top-context store retains the $K_{\mathrm{top}}$ highest-scoring active contexts,
\[
    \operatorname{TopCtx}_K(f)
    =
    \operatorname*{arg\,top}_{S \subseteq \mathcal{C}_f,\ |S|=K_{\mathrm{top}}}
    \sum_{((x,t),s)\in S} s,
\]
approximating the upper tail of the conditional activation distribution.
The mid-context store is a bounded reservoir sample from the sigma band,
\[
    \operatorname{MidCtx}_M(f)
    \sim
    \operatorname{ReservoirSample}_M\!\left(
    \{(x,t) : \mu_f + 0.5\,\sigma_f \leq a_f(x,t) \leq \mu_f + 1.5\,\sigma_f\}
    \right),
\]
maintained without sorting every active occurrence.
Mid contexts distinguish a latent's ordinary operating regime from its most vivid examples and provide probe data where extreme contexts would be too narrow.

\paragraph{Contrast-context retrieval.}
% APPENDIX CUTS 2026-10-12 (tier 2): shortened (the retrieval equations were dropped); full text in
% paper/notes/main-before-appendix-cuts-2026-10-12.tex.
Each sequence $x$ is represented by $r(x) \in \R^{1024}$, the final layer's residual stream mean-pooled over positions, captured once during collection.
The attribution baselines use a retrieval store: for each target, the $64$ sequences most similar, by exact cosine search, to the normalised centroid of its activating contexts, excluding those contexts, with no check for silence.
Its index samples $2 \times 10^{5}$ sequences, so targets with no activating context in the sample fall back to one shared default set ($38\%$ of TuringLLM targets).

\paragraph{Contrast contexts for WCM.}
Weighted circuit masking and its evaluation instead select contrast contexts per target at fit time.
Each of the target's strongest stored contexts $\posset^{\mathrm{top}}$ is represented, read from the index when present and otherwise computed by a forward pass; candidates from the sampled corpus are ranked by their highest cosine similarity to any of them, $\max_{x_p \in \posset^{\mathrm{top}}} \langle r(x_p), r(x) \rangle$, with all of the target's stored activating contexts (strongest and mid-band) excluded; the $8{,}192$ most similar are then run through the model in rank order, and each is kept only if the target is outside its site's Top-K at every position, until 64 are kept.
A target with fewer than 4 kept is skipped ($19$ TuringLLM targets), and one with 4 to 63 uses what it has ($10$ targets).
The silence check rejects between 14\% and 51\% of the closest candidates on the targets we inspected, so unchecked retrieval would place many contrast contexts where the target fires.
The same selector with random or maximally dissimilar ranking gives the \emph{random} and \emph{distant} sources of \cref{app:contrast-source}.

\subsection{Sparsity-Preserving Mean Ablation}
\label{app:respect-topk}
The classic dense mean ablation assigns every latent outside the circuit its mean, producing a stream with tens of thousands of simultaneously active latents per position, outside the state space a Top-K SAE can produce.
The sparsity-preserving variant applies the means and then re-imposes the per-position Top-K (kept latents, then the largest means of the other latents up to $k$, rest zero), which restores the keep-all identity to exactly $1.0$.
At scale (32 targets, paired) the sparsity-preserving variant does not systematically shift faithfulness: it raises both $a_C$ and $a_{\varnothing}$, leaving the ratio a wash ($50$W/$46$L).
It therefore functions as a cleaner protocol rather than a score inflator; but at small node sets and tight Top-K budgets the two variants can disagree on \emph{rankings}, which is why the body's comparisons name their ablation method.

\subsection{Sources of the Mean Ablation}
\label{app:floors}
The empty-circuit baseline $a_{\varnothing}$ is a measured object with its own failure modes.
Means over \emph{activating} contexts leak target signal into $a_{\varnothing}$ increasingly with depth ($a_{\varnothing}/a_{\mathrm{pos}} = 0/23/30/26\%$ at layers 2/8/9/10); means over the target's \emph{contrast} contexts hold $a_{\varnothing} = 0.0000$ on every target measured, even under the hardest close contrast contexts.
These pre-v1 measurements, on four targets, are the source of the leakage figure in \cref{sec:eval}.

\section{Dense ReLU Dictionaries}
\label{app:dense-relu}
% Added 2026-10-12 (tier 3, Daniel 4a) as the support for the Limitations scope sentence. Data: experiments/030-cross-sae
% (2026-08-10, pre-v1: tri-amp transcription with free amplitudes, 400-step anneal, 48/16 held-out split, verified-silent
% negatives; rows.jsonl; anchor_support.py -> anchor_support.json). A protocol-v1 rerun is planned (Daniel 2026-10-12).
The method is scoped to Top-K dictionaries because of a pre-v1 test on Pythia-70m with the dense ReLU dictionaries of \citet{marks2025sparse}: six residual-stream targets at layers 2 and 4, an earlier form of weighted circuit masking, and held-out contexts.
The method replicated: $11$ of $12$ fits (six targets at two sparsity penalties) were faithful under zero and mean ablation ($0.80$--$1.00$) and necessary ($0.81$--$1.0$) on held-out contexts.
The random-circuit baseline did not: random sets given the same coefficient fitting failed at up to ${\sim}1{,}100$ latents, but at $2{,}000$--$6{,}000$ latents they reproduced the target under zero ablation ($0.71$--$1.00$) while still failing necessity.
The cause is the encoder's sparsity: $6.5$--$37\%$ of a target's live ReLU latents are nonzero at its anchor, against $0.3\%$ expected under Top-K ($k = 128$ of $40{,}960$), so under ReLU a random latent's fitted coefficient has a nonzero value to scale, and thousands of them can synthesise the target; under Top-K a random latent is almost always zero at the anchor.
On dense dictionaries, necessity rather than faithfulness is the discriminating test, and the random baseline needs its own design.

\subsection{Loss Terms for Target Rank and Specificity}
\label{app:rank-designs}
% experiments/055-close-contrast (arms otcut*, otrank2, rtop*, marginp, rkeep*), 056-specificity.
An \emph{off-target} term charging every other site latent for rising above its unmodified and empty-circuit values cut lifts three- to sevenfold at no faithfulness cost, but addressed a property the method need not guarantee (\cref{app:specificity}) and grew circuits when it also charged harmless lifts below the cut.
A \emph{Top-K hinge} requiring only that the target beat the $k$-th other latent restored faithfulness but, used alone, let circuits keep the target in the Top-K by lifting it together with its neighbourhood.
A convex pull towards rank $1$ inflated circuits by an order of magnitude at every weight tried, since it never switches off and dominates once the reproduction error is small.
The \emph{firing-margin} objective, fitting the target's lead over the Top-K cut, needs its loss normalised by the target's pre-activation scale rather than the margin's, which is near zero for targets at the cut.
Rank-keep, which switches off once the target holds its natural rank, is the single term that kept the gains without these costs, and its effect is flat across a thirtyfold range of weights.

\subsection{Binarisation Sweep}
The gates are optimised directly rather than sampled~\citep{louizos2018learning, li2024optimal}; the local minima that sampling avoids appear here as the equivalence class of near-optimal node sets (\cref{sec:underdetermination}).
The straight-through estimator makes interior gate values forward-irrelevant, so the sparsity penalty drifts nodes toward just-above-threshold: the decision boundary becomes an \emph{attractor}, and per-step node flips grow through training ($218$ per step at layer 10), a stopping-step lottery.
Annealing instead yields the tightest zero-ablation faithfulness in the programme ($0.002$--$0.10$ deviation from unity) at slightly worse mean-ablation scores, another home-turf demonstration.
Schedule compression does not help: a $200$-step run matches the $400$-step metrics at ${\sim}2\times$ the per-step churn (churn is set by per-step pressure, not schedule shape); reach-then-hold leaves churn unchanged and inflates size $21$--$48\%$; and truncating a $400$-step run at $200$ yields node sets $57$--$72\%$ oversized with the gate still half-soft.
