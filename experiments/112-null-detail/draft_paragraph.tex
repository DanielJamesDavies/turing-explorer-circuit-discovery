The nulls also fail on the contexts they were fitted on: none passes on the training contexts of any target, where the production circuits pass for $90$, $97$, and $88\%$ (TuringLLM, Pythia-70m, GPT-2 small), and their median zero-ablation faithfulness is $0.00$ on training contexts as on held-out ones (\cref{tab:run2026-nulls-detail}).
With $0$ passes of $112$--$128$ targets, the exact one-sided $95\%$ upper bound on each null's pass rate is $2.6\%$ on TuringLLM and $2.3\%$ on the public models ($6$--$9\%$ within a depth band).
Unlike the protocol-v1 null, these fits are not inert: under the production schedule the coefficient that weight decay alone reaches is $0.790$ (not $0.798$, which is the constant-rate value), and the median circuit moves $92$--$99\%$ of its coefficients more than $0.01$ from it, about as many as the production circuits ($99$--$100\%$), though about half of them down where $83$--$90\%$ of the production coefficients move up.
The weak null of the protocol-v1 run, refitted in the production run on the TuringLLM panel, moves $92\%$ of its coefficients too, yet still scores $0.00$, so on this schedule it fails for want of usable members rather than for want of gradient.
The anchor-active draws barely overlap the production circuits although production members could be drawn: a median $1.4\%$, $4.5\%$, and $1.3\%$ of a draw's members are production members (Jaccard $0.007$, $0.022$, and $0.006$; at most $0.10$).
The contribution-matched null falls short of the production members' strength where it matters most.
It finds a same-site non-member in the member's own contribution decile for a median $68\%$, $58\%$, and $71\%$ of members, but for only $22\%$, $11\%$, and $22\%$ of the members in the top decile; its drawn set's median contribution reaches $0.87$, $0.71$, and $0.94$ of the members' median but its mean only $0.51$, $0.29$, and $0.39$ of theirs, since the strongest contributors are already in the circuit.
(The anchor-active null matches every member's frequency decile exactly, and its members contribute about $1\%$ as much as the production members.)
That shortfall does not explain the failure: the contribution-matched sets are needed for the target without sufficing for it.
Their necessity grows with depth (TuringLLM $0.37$, $0.72$, $0.74$ by band; GPT-2 small $0.55$, $1.00$, $1.00$; Pythia-70m $0.05$, $0.21$, $0.53$) and with how much of the members' contribution the draw recovered (Spearman $\rho = 0.54$--$0.69$ across draws).
On GPT-2 small, removing the set from the running model switches the target off outright in $36\%$ of draws ($52$--$56\%$ from layer $5$ on), as removing the production members does in $87\%$, because a Top-K latent falls to zero once its pre-activation drops below the $k$-th largest; yet no draw reproduces the target alone, with $Z \ge 0.75$ in none of GPT-2 small's draws and $2\%$ of TuringLLM's.
Necessity is coefficient-blind (it removes the members from the live model), so the fitted and $\alpha = 1$ rows share it.
On the targets with two draws of a null, the production circuit's lowest faithfulness score exceeds both draws' on every target, its necessity is at least both draws' on $59$--$100\%$ of them (the low end being GPT-2 small's contribution-matched null), and it passes while both draws fail on $81\%$, $77\%$, and $81\%$, which is its own pass rate on those targets.
